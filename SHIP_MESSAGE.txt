v0.30.0 — THE DRILLING RECORDER'S EXPORT, READ AS DELIVERED

Three one-second drilling files arrived - a rig's electronic drilling
recorder, eighteen WITS channels, five million rows across three wells
drilled to 25,800 feet this summer - and the program could not read them.
The recorder writes the date and the time in two columns, and the
historian port, which takes one timestamp column, fell over on the first
row. The answer was not to convert the files by hand. It was a port.

The EDR port reads the layout as delivered: the two time columns, the
units from the header, -999.25 and the empty cell as missing and counted,
the sections from the gaps where the rig left the well and came back,
every sample with its wall-clock stamp - streamed, so the largest file
reads in half a minute. From the stream it writes the drilling record: per
section the hours, the hole depth where drilling began and the deepest it
got, the feet drilled, the hours on bottom and rotating, the rate of
penetration on bottom, the connections, the peaks; for the well, total
depth and the moment the bit was first on bottom there, the recorder's
own TVD at that depth; and the MD-TVD table, every row of which says it is
the recorder's interpolation and not a survey.

The real files taught the record two things on the owner's machine. A
recorder restarting after a casing run carries a stale depth in its first
rows and then resets its hole depth - 8,498 feet to 1,417, 25,779 to 8,285
- so the section's start is taken from the first on-bottom sample, and
where the channel's own upward steps disagree with start-to-deepest the
record carries both numbers and names the fall. And the recorder's TVD
stops short of total depth, by 29 to 117 feet; the record says how far.

What it will not do: fill a sentinel; call the recorder's TVD a survey;
name a formation, a landing point or a kick-off; judge the drilling; run a
drilling recorder through the gauge pipeline.

Section BY is new. The gate is 376 checks. The standalone guard is clean.
