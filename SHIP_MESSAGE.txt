v0.36.0 — THE WELL IN 3D

A well is a line through the ground, and until now the program only ever
showed it as numbers against depth. This release adds the 3D tab.

Each well's path is drawn from its directional survey by the minimum-
curvature method - between survey stations it follows the same arc, never
a straight chord - below sea level from the well's declared rig floor, and
set among the mapped formations: interpreted horizon surfaces as they were
delivered, or a formation-top map's contour lines at their elevations. The
picks sit on the path. The wells can be coloured by the formation they are
in, by inclination or dogleg severity, or by a drilling recorder's gamma,
rate of penetration or weight on bit along the bit's depth. Turn it, pan it,
zoom it, stretch the depth, see through the surfaces, point at anything for
its numbers.

The replay moves the bit down the hole - the hole drilled so far in full
colour, the rest faded - from the recorder's bit depth, every sample on the
recorder's own clock, or from the times of the survey stations measured
while drilling.

The Volve field in the Norwegian North Sea ships with the program as the
worked example: the original surveys of three wells, the field's 408
formation picks and five mapped horizons, released by Equinor. They were
checked against each other before anything was drawn: the paths reproduce
the survey companies' own positions to under a centimetre and land on
Equinor's picks to under a third of a metre; the coordinates are ED50 UTM
zone 31N, confirmed against the operator's log headers to a tenth of a
metre. One survey says true north but fits the picks only as grid north,
and the page says so.

A workspace well is drawn once it has a position, an elevation and its
survey; `gea trajectory` attaches one, kept byte for byte, with its azimuth
reference stated. Nothing is drawn between wells: no rock or fluid property
is measured there.

Section CE is new, three checks. The gate is 395 checks. The standalone
guard is clean.
