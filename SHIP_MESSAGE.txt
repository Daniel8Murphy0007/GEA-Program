v0.22.0 — THE INTERVAL AGAINST THE MAPPED TOPS: WHERE THE INJECTION SITS IN THE GEOLOGY

The first question a reviewer of a disposal well in a seismic response area
asks, answered from two things the program already holds: the operator's
declaration and the layers the site brought. The operator declares the
interval - top and base, true vertical depth, the elevation the depths are
measured from and what that elevation is (kelly bushing, ground, derrick
floor) and where it came from. The program converts the interval to subsea
with the arithmetic printed and sets it beside each formation-top bracket the
contour layers give at the well: ABOVE the mapped top, BELOW it, or NOT
RESOLVED where the bracket overlaps the interval - a map read between two
contours cannot place a top on one side of an interval it straddles. A layer
is told what it maps; a formation with a top and an isopach gets a base by
arithmetic the report shows, and the declared depth tier - shallow or deep by
the base the plan names - is CONSISTENT with the map, INCONSISTENT, or NOT
RESOLVED. That is the line the Commission's review turns on.

The catalogue's events inside the area are set against the mapped faults:
the nearest trace to each epicentre, the counts within 1, 2 and 5 km. The
ground elevation of a position comes from the USGS 3D Elevation Program's
point service when asked, kept with its raster and query time, and named as
the ground - the kelly bushing is higher by a height only the completion
report states, and every statement built on it says so.

What it will not do: interpolate a top; place an interval the bracket
overlaps; convert a measured depth; call the tier right - it says whether the
declaration and the map agree; attribute an event to a trace it is near.

Section BP is new. The gate is 353 checks. The standalone guard is clean.
