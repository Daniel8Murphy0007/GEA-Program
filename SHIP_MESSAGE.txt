v0.31.0 — THE DOWNHOLE TOOL LIBRARY, WITH A TUTORIAL ON EVERY TOOL

The tool library held seven gauges: the instruments this program reads.
A well holds far more than that. Bits and hole openers, motors and rotary
steerable systems, MWD and LWD, collars, jars and subs, shoes and float
collars, packers, plugs, sleeves and pumps, the wireline tools, the
fishing tools, the test tools. This release puts every class of downhole
tool in use across the industry on the Tools & ports page: ninety-six of
them in nine categories.

Each class is a chip. Point at it, or Tab to it, and its one-line
description appears. Click, tap or press Enter and its tutorial opens:
what it is, where it runs, how it works, what it shows in the data, what
to watch, and what this program does with it. Esc closes it and puts you
back where you were.

Every class was checked against public pages. There are 192 of them, all
fetched on one day and recorded with the class: the SLB Energy Glossary,
SPE PetroWiki, the OSHA eTool, maker datasheets, academic tool pages. The
tutorials carry no numbers of their own. The 196 figures on the cards are
the pages' own words, with the unit and the page, and the card says when
a figure is one maker's product rather than a limit for the class. A
class with no published figure says to supply the datasheet.

That is where the fleet comes in. Your own tools - maker, model, serial,
the datasheet that rates them - go in beside the classes, from the
tutorial or the command line. They are kept in the workspace and every
change is in the audit log.

Section BZ is new, three checks. The gate is 380 checks. The standalone
guard is clean.
