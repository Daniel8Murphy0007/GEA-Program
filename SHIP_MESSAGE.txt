v0.8.0 — THE MACHINE, THE GROUND, THE SITE, AND A PANEL THAT RESTARTS ITSELF

The machine behind the lines. gea/seismic_harmonic.py stops treating a
signature as a flat list of frequencies. A pump at 1.7 strokes a second is
one fact about a machine, not four about bins: a comb search over every line
divided by every order recovers the fundamental, normalised to the largest
spacing the orders found allow, and prints it as a rate per minute - the
number a driller reads. It says how strong the claim is and turns down the
weak ones: fewer than three lines is not a family, because any two lines
define a comb; a comb this record's line density would make by accident more
often than 5 % is set aside; two spacings that fit equally well are
AMBIGUOUS; a fundamental whose half falls below the band is flagged, because
the machine may be running at half that rate with only its even harmonics in
view. And the other half is time: a pump's rate follows its load, so a line
at 1.40 Hz walks to 1.85 Hz over a tour and a signature of fixed bins loses
it. Every line is now followed window to window, each peak refined inside its
bin, with STEADY, DRIFTING or INTERMITTENT and the drift in Hz per hour; a
drift no larger than the bin width is not called a drift. A tracked line is
attributed back to its source by proximity to a line that source was learned
on, or by a whole-number ratio to one it already claims; a line two sources
could both claim is given to neither. And the ground truth every verdict in
the leg rested on became measurable: each source's working spells are read
off the record and set beside the ones the rigs list declares, agreeing or
disagreeing, because a permit date is a permission and not a drilling log.
On the labelled scene - a pump ramping 1.40 to 1.85 Hz with a stop in it -
fixed bins hold two lines of it, the tracker holds every one with the
harmonics walking 1x, 2x and 3.01x the first, and the fundamental comes back
0.005 Hz from the rate the scene was running at in that window.

Which datum a position is on. gea/geodesy.py ends the program's oldest silent
assumption: that a latitude and a longitude mean the same ground whoever
wrote them. A Texas permit export is often NAD27 and a network's metadata
WGS84, and at 31 N, 102 W those name ground 46 m apart - the size of a
position ellipse, which is why a datum error never looked like an error.
Positions carry their datum, conversions carry their accuracy, a position
whose datum nobody stated is UNKNOWN and prints what assuming costs in metres
at that site, and the separation between two datums is computed there rather
than quoted, because it varies: 46 m in the Permian, 85 m in Bakersfield,
18 m in Ohio. Under it: Vincenty's inverse on the ellipsoid, the published
three-parameter shifts, transverse Mercator and Lambert conformal conic both
ways with the Texas state plane zones, and the US survey foot kept distinct
from the international foot, because over a state plane northing they differ
by metres. It is checked against values this program did not produce - the
WGS84 meridian arc to 45 N, a degree of latitude and a degree of longitude at
the equator, all three to the millimetre - and against the identities every
projection must satisfy at its own origin. It turns down a NAD83 state plane
zone handed NAD27 coordinates, an array whose sensors are on two datums, and
any suggestion that a three-parameter shift is survey grade.

Is this array any good? gea/seismic_qc.py checks what every bearing rested on:
that the sensors record the same ground motion at the same time, scaled the
same way, with the same polarity. An array is not a set of records, it is the
differences between them, so a fault invisible in one record is fatal across
several. The timing test needs no reference clock: for a plane wave the
arrival delays must lie on a plane, so the plane is fitted to the array's own
measured delays and each sensor's residual is the part of its arrival the
wavefront does not explain - the fit returns the velocity and direction as a
by-product, which is what makes it its own reference. Four faults put into a
clean array are each named and no others: a dead channel, a clock 40 ms out
and measured at 40.04, a sensor wired backwards, one at 8 % of the gain. What
they were doing to the answer is measured rather than asserted: excluding
them moves the bearing 173 degrees. The same array with no faults comes back
USABLE with nothing named, which is the half of the test that matters most.

What else is out there. gea/seismic_unlisted.py is the first part of this leg
that finds instead of confirming. Every test before it answered a question
somebody had already asked; this one asks what else is in the ground, because
the source nobody listed is the one nobody is watching. The tracked lines no
listed source claims are grouped into combs, and a comb with a fundamental is
a machine running at a rate, beamed on its own lines by exactly the code that
beams a listed rig. With one of three rigs left off the list it comes back at
half a degree of its true bearing and its true pump rate; with every rig
listed there is nothing to find. What it refuses matters more here than
anywhere else, because this is the one test whose output is a claim nobody
asked for: a line on the mains frequency or one of its multiples is
electrical and is named so, and so is a mains harmonic above half the sample
rate, which does not disappear but folds back into the band at an
arbitrary-looking frequency and looks exactly like a machine; a candidate
that beams to zero slowness arrived everywhere at once and is in the cabling,
not the ground; a candidate at a whole-number ratio of a listed rate is that
rig's harmonic; one line is not a machine and neither are two. And a
candidate is a direction with a rate on it, never an identification.

How well a bearing is known. Every ellipse this leg drew came from the array
response half-power width - what the array could resolve at perfect
signal-to-noise, which is a floor and not an error bar, identical for a clean
record and a marginal one. gea/seismic_uncertainty.py measures it: the window
is cut into sub-windows, each beamed on its own, and the scatter of their
bearings is what the noise does to the answer, with the floor at the
beamformer's own grid step. Then the part that makes it a measurement rather
than a number - coverage takes a record whose bearing is known from outside
it, runs every window, and counts how often the truth really falls inside one
sigma and two. On the labelled scene: CALIBRATED, 75 % inside one and 100 %
inside two, a median error of 0.37 degrees against a sigma of 0.54, and the
factor that would centre the claim exactly returned beside it, so a record
where the sigma is wrong says by how much. Resampling sees the random part
and not a bias steady through a window, and the module says so rather than
letting it through. The positions are crossed with what the data is worth,
and each one records which of its bearings carried a measured sigma and which
fell back to the geometry.

The site, and the two reports about all of it. The workspace held wells,
seismic stations and tracks as peers with nothing owning them, which made
this three tools in one package rather than one program. A site holds what
belongs to one client's ground, and the Site Report pulls every leg of it
into one document. Beside it, the Seismic Dataset Report says what is held -
every record with the span it actually covers, its rate, its gaps, the
checksum taken when it came in, the datum its positions are on, and which
outputs are older than the record they came from - and the Seismic Field
Report says what the site heard, rig by rig and station by station, with what
nobody listed beside it. All three name what needs attention, and all three
say plainly what they are not: what is held is not whether it is any good, a
source nobody heard is not a source that was not working, and a site is a
list of what belongs to an engagement and not a boundary on the ground.

Also in this ship: the live-port commands say who they are and what must run
beside them; the service turns every non-finite number into null, because NaN
and Infinity are valid to Python's json and a syntax error to every browser;
the permits importer reads a datum column or takes one and can read state
plane or UTM easting and northing in metres or either foot; the rigs CSV and
the sensors CSV take a datum column; and three new client reports joined the
sample set, re-rendered from this build.

And the control panel now owns its own stopping and starting. It had one way
to end - the window closed - and the operator was left looking at whatever
shell was underneath; on a console opened from a Python profile that is a
bare >>> prompt where a program used to be. The service decides how it ends
and says so in its exit code: 0 stopped on purpose, 86 start me again. The
launcher reads that code, runs the panel again on 86, and on anything else
hands the window to a PowerShell prompt that names the program and how to
start it. It never falls through to the shell underneath. Stop, Restart
behind an explicit authorisation, and a switch for letting the launcher start
it again by itself live on the Administration page; the switch is one
character in records/auto_restart.flag, because a batch file cannot read the
manifest, and it is capped at five stops in a row so a panel that crashes
while starting hands over the error instead of flickering all night.

Every start, stop, restart and recovery is one appended line in
records/runlog.jsonl, flushed to the disk before the thing it describes is
attempted - a log written afterwards is no use to a machine that lost power
during it. A run that logged a start and never logged a stop reads back as
killed, not as a clean stop. And the recovery runs in an order that is not
negotiable: the live patches come up first and the rebuilding of the records
runs behind them, because what the stream did not record is not recoverable
later and a report that is behind its source always is. The catch-up rebuilds
exactly what the staleness table says is behind and leaves the rest alone; it
does not call a gap in the stream recovered. gea doctor reads the installed
launcher off the disk and says whether it honours the contract, so a kit
built before this existed is three warnings with fixes rather than an
operator meeting an interpreter prompt.

The gate is 302 checks (sections AU, AV, AW, AX, AY, AZ, BA and BB are new).
The standalone guard is clean: no import, no text and no metadata outside
this package.
