v0.26.0 — SLIP TENDENCY ON THE MAPPED FAULTS: THE STRESS STATE, STATED, RESOLVED ON EVERY TRACE

After "how far is the nearest mapped fault" comes "how close to slipping is
it, under this injection". The open method - Morris slip tendency, the fault
slip potential arithmetic of Walsh and Zoback - is a stress state resolved on
a plane: the vertical and the two horizontal stresses on it, the pore
pressure taken off, the shear stress against the friction times the
effective normal stress. This program does that arithmetic on inputs the
site declares with their sources and on nothing it has not been told: the
gradients and their source, the SHmax azimuth with its source or from the
World Stress Map records near the well with their count and spread printed,
the strike from the map, the dip from the layer where it states one and
otherwise each declared dip, the friction coefficient printed against the
catalogue's measured envelope. For every trace within the radius: the slip
tendency at the ambient and the increased pore pressure, the pressure to
slip and the margin left, below or past the friction line in the worst and
the best declared case.

The BEG/TexNet Delaware Basin fault layers of Hennings et al. 2021 are read -
UTM, unprojected from the .prj's own parameters, 8,332 segments with their
dips - and where they publish their own pressure-to-slip under their
calibrated stress model, that number is printed beside this arithmetic with
the difference. The published model is the one with a paper behind it.

What it will not do: measure a stress; give a fault a dip the map does not
carry; give a probability of an earthquake; attribute an event.

Section BT is new. The gate is 365 checks. The standalone guard is clean.
