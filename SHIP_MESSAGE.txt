v0.14.0 — MACHINE VIBRATION: THE ZONE, AND WHAT THE BEARINGS ARE DOING

The one condition-monitoring deliverable an operations engineer expects by
name. gea/vibration.py reads the pump's record and answers the first
question ISO 20816 answers - which zone is this machine in - from the
broadband r.m.s. vibration velocity over 10 to 1000 Hz as ISO 20816-1
defines it, evaluated by ISO 20816-3's boundaries for the declared machine
group and support class, with the boundaries printed and the margin to the
next. Then the second question, by envelope analysis: the most impulsive
band chosen by kurtosis, the analytic signal's envelope, and its spectrum
searched for the bearing's own defect frequencies - BPFO, BPFI, BSF, FTF
from its geometry and the shaft speed - each MATCHED at its fundamental or
NOT MATCHED, the inner race with its sidebands at the shaft speed.

What it will not call a measurement: a group or a support class, which are
declared; a zone from a record that cannot carry the band, marked PARTIAL
BAND; a fault size; a defect frequency without the bearing's geometry; an
mm/s value from counts without a sensitivity. The fundamental is required
for a match because the harmonics of one defect frequency fall near
multiples of another - the first draft named three faults where the
labelled scene had one.

And a defect found on the way: the miniSEED writer rounded every record's
start to the header's 0.1 ms, so a 20 kHz record read back as fifteen
traces. Blockette 1001 carries the microseconds now and the round-trip is
exact.

Section BH is new. The gate is 330 checks. The standalone guard is clean.
