v0.52.0 — PORE PRESSURE AND FRACTURE GRADIENT

GEA now draws the mud-weight window for a well.

From a well's logs - sonic, deep resistivity, gamma ray and density - or
from its drilling record, through the d-exponent, it computes the
overburden, fits the shale's normal compaction trend over an interval the
operator declares normally pressured, and reads the pore pressure from
the departure from that trend by Eaton's method. The fracture gradient
comes from Eaton's relation with a declared Poisson's ratio, with the
Hubbert-Willis minimum beside it. Everything is shown as equivalent mud
weight against true vertical depth.

Formation tests, kicks and leak-off tests can be entered and sit beside
the curves; the Eaton exponent that best fits the formation tests is
given; and where the drilling record carries the mud weight, any stretch
where it fell below the pore pressure or above the fracture gradient is
flagged.

The worked example is the Dutch offshore well L07-01, from NLOG's open
data. A pore pressure read from a trend is only as good as the trend, so
the page draws the log, the shale points and the fitted trend beside the
result.

The Pore pressure tab, `gea pore-pressure`, a report with its PDF and the
help page are new; the signed dossier carries the declarations and each
well's window. Section CU is new, four checks. The gate is 451 checks.
The standalone guard is clean.
