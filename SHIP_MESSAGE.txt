v0.30.1 — THE DRILLING RECORD READS ITS OWN DEPTH BEFORE COUNTING

v0.30.0 read the drilling recorder's export as delivered, and on the real
files its arithmetic was still wrong. The third well's total depth came
out as 25,779 feet; none of the three footages equalled total depth less
the first depth. The record named the hole depth channel's falls and
carried two numbers instead of deciding.

A hole depth channel lies in four ways, and each leaves a shape. A
recorder moved from the last well opens on that well's depth and holds it
until someone sets it. A depth set back to 1,417 feet is counted up again
as pipe runs in, at 2,500 feet an hour with the recorder's own rate of
penetration at zero. A depth set back 8,047 feet and then time-drilled at
under a foot an hour for six hours is a sidetrack: the third well was
drilled to 24,902 feet, kicked off at 16,855 and drilled again to 25,722 -
its total depth - and the 25,779 in its file was a depth carried over
from the well before. And a stand counted twice is taken back.

The record now reads every one of these before it counts a foot, names
each as a depth event in the record and on the well page, takes total
depth from the final borehole, and keeps the abandoned hole out of the
gamma log and the TVD table. It also keeps out of the drilling hours the
28.6 hours, across two wells, in which the on-bottom flag was left on
with nothing drilled. Without a sidetrack the footage is exactly total
depth less the first depth - 23,796.2 and 23,879.0 feet - and with one,
31,752.2.

What it will not do: call a depth event more than the shape it read - the
daily drilling report decides; name a formation or a landing point; judge
the drilling.

Section BY gains a fourth check. The gate is 377 checks. The standalone
guard is clean.
