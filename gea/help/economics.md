# Economics

> Each well's days on the record, footage drilled and hours on bottom - measured, from its drilling record - beside what it cost by the operator's own declared day rates, items and AFE, set against the U.S. Energy Information Administration's daily spot prices for WTI crude and Henry Hub gas while it was drilled, and any other series the site imports (a rig count, a regional price).

**The command**: the Economics tab. The tiles give the latest price of each series and how it got here (shipped with the program, refreshed into the workspace, or imported). Each series has its own chart - one axis each, never two on one - over the wells' drilling records with sixty days either side; the shaded bands are the wells. Point at a chart, or focus it and use the arrows, for the day's value and which wells were drilling. The Wells table gives each well's time on the record, feet drilled, feet per day, the declared cost with cost per foot and per day, the difference from the AFE, and each price while it was drilled (mean, range, trading days). Declare the cost basis under it (the operator role): a day rate in USD with what it pays for, its period (from and to on the drilling recorder's clock, open at either end) and its source; a one-off item (a bit, a cement job) optionally tied to a section; the AFE. **Refresh the EIA prices** fetches the latest copies; **Import a series** keeps any CSV of dates and values with its name, unit and source. From a terminal: `gea economics --workspace C:\site` prints it all; `--rate 32000 --well Well-30636033 --what "rig spread" --from 2026-06-01 --source "drilling contract"`, `--item`, `--afe`, `--refresh-prices`, `--import-series FILE --name --unit --source`.

**What it writes**: the cost basis in `economics/costs.json` in the workspace, every declaration and removal a line in the audit log with its source; refreshed and imported series in `economics/series/`, each kept byte for byte with its hash, its source and when it came. The shipped series are in the program's reference folder with their provenance (`reference/eia_prices.provenance.json`).

**The number to check**: a cost against its arithmetic - the day rates multiplied by the days each covers within the drilling record's first and last sample, plus the items - and the days against the drilling record; a price against EIA's own table (series RWTC and RNGWHHD on eia.gov): the shipped WTI series carries the negative settlement of 20 April 2020 (-36.98) and the Henry Hub series the spike of 17 February 2021 (23.86), as EIA published them.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a cost - it is the operator's declaration, and the page says where each number came from; a cost per foot when the declared rates leave part of the record's time uncovered - the partial cost is shown, marked incomplete, and not divided; a price as the reason a well cost what it did - the prices are context; a correlation between cost per foot and price across fewer than ten wells with a complete cost - below that it is noise, and it is not computed; a day the publisher left blank - it is left out, never filled (the line runs on across a weekend or a holiday; a gap of more than six days is left open); the recorder's clock as UTC - the drilling record's times, and the rate periods declared against them, are as the recorder wrote them.
