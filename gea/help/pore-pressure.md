# Pore pressure

> The mud-weight window from a well's logs or its drilling record: the overburden; the pore pressure by Eaton's method from the sonic, the deep resistivity or the corrected d-exponent against the shale's normal compaction trend; the fracture gradient by Eaton's relation and the Hubbert-Willis minimum; measured pressures and the mud weight used beside them. The Dutch offshore well L07-01 is the worked example.

**The command**: the **Pore pressure** tab: the pressures as equivalent mud weights against true vertical depth, with the hydrostatic, the overburden, the fracture gradients, the measurements and the mud; beside it the indicator against depth on a log scale, the shale points, the trend and the interval it was fitted over; how the well is read and its measurements (the operator role); **Write the report**. From a terminal: `gea pore-pressure --workspace C:\site --well W` prints it; `--example` the worked example; `--indicator sonic --nct-top 600 --nct-bottom 2100 --gr-cutoff 75 --normal-gradient 0.465 --poisson 0.25 --density-above 2.1 --water-depth 0 --air-gap 0 --bit 8.5 --source "drilling program"` declares how it is read; `--measure 2400,5337,psi,pore,tvd --source "MDT station 14"` adds a measurement (`lot` or `fit` for a leak-off or formation-integrity test, `ppg` for an equivalent weight, `md` for a measured depth); `--write` writes the report.

**What it reads**: a well whose record is a depth log (LAS) with a sonic (DT, AC), a deep resistivity (RDEP, ILD, RT) and a gamma ray, a bulk density where there is one; or a drilling record with hole depth, rate of penetration, rotary speed and weight on bit, from which the d-exponent is computed on the drilling samples (the median every 3 m of hole), corrected by the record's mud density when it has one - the bit size must be declared. True vertical depth comes from the well's survey, else the recorder's interpolated TVD, else the well is taken as vertical.

**How it is computed**: the overburden integrates the density - the sea below the air gap, then the rock: the density log, else Gardner's density from the sonic (0.23 V^0.25), else the declared mean density above the first value. The normal compaction trend is a straight line in the logarithm of the indicator against TVD, fitted to the shale (gamma ray at or above the cut-off; the 60th percentile when none is declared) over the interval the operator declares normally pressured. Eaton's ratio gives the pore pressure: Pp = S - (S - Ph) (X/Xn)^n, sonic exponent 3.0, resistivity and d-exponent 1.2. A step read below 90 % of hydrostatic is rock faster than the shale trend and is left blank. The fracture gradient is Eaton's F = nu/(1 - nu) (S - Pp) + Pp with the declared Poisson's ratio, beside the Hubbert-Willis minimum (S - Pp)/3 + Pp. Overpressure is read where the pore pressure stays half a ppg over hydrostatic for 50 m. With formation tests declared, the Eaton exponent that best fits them is given; with a mud weight in the record, a stretch where it is below the pore pressure or above the fracture gradient is flagged.

**What it writes**: how each well is read and its measurements in `geomech\pore_pressure.json` (audit lines `pore_pressure.settings`, `pore_pressure.measurement`, `pore_pressure.measurement_removed`), the audit line `report.pore_pressure`, and the reports `reports\wells\<well>\pore_pressure.*` (or `reports\pore_pressure_example.*`; PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON).

**The number to check**: the overburden at TD against the density log integrated by hand; a formation test's pressure against the curve at its depth; the trend's slope against the shale points on the right-hand plot.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the pore pressure where nothing was measured - it is computed from a log against its trend, and the trend is the operator's choice of a normally pressured interval. The fracture gradient - a relation with a declared Poisson's ratio, not a leak-off test. The pressure in sand and carbonate - the method reads shale.

**Sources**: Eaton, "The equation for geopressure prediction from well logs", SPE-5544 (1975); Eaton, "Fracture gradient prediction and its application in oilfield operations", JPT 21(10) (1969); Gardner, Gardner and Gregory, Geophysics 39(6) (1974); Jorden and Shirley, SPE-1407 (1966); Rehm and McClendon, SPE-3601 (1971); Hubbert and Willis, Trans. AIME 210 (1957). The worked example: well L07-01, NLOG open data.
