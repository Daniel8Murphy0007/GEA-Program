# Mapped structure

> The first geological statement this program makes about a well: how far, and in which direction, the nearest mapped fault is; the nearest fold; what else the structure map holds within a radius. The map is a shapefile the site brought - a public structure layer (USGS, a state survey, BEG/TexNet) or the operator's own - read as the specification lays the format out, without a dependency, and kept in the workspace with every part hashed.

**The command**: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action add-layer --name "USGS Appalachian structure" --layer structure.zip --citation "USGS PP 1708 (2014)"` reads the shapefile once (the .shp, .shx, .dbf and .prj together, in a zip, a folder or by the .shp), refuses a coordinate system it cannot use by name (geographic NAD83 or WGS84 is taken as it is; the Texas State Plane NAD83 zones are unprojected; anything else is named and refused), and keeps it under `layers/<id>/` with its extent, feature count, the structure types it carries and the metadata it came with. A zip holding several shapefiles - EIA's `PermianBasin_Boundary_Structural_Tectonic.zip` holds nine - lists them with `gea structure --layer <zip> --list`, and one is named with `--member Faults_Permian_201712`; each becomes its own layer. `--action structure --well <id> --radius-km 50` asks every layer about the well's declared surface position. On the dashboard: Seismic -> Structure layers to add one; the well page's "Mapped structure" card; the SRA packet's section 2b for every well inside the area. `gea structure --layer structure.zip --lat 41.0997 --lon -80.6495` answers for any position without a workspace; `--info` describes a layer.

**What it writes**: per layer, what that kind of layer can say. A **structure** layer (faults and folds): the nearest mapped fault and the nearest mapped fold - distance in kilometres along the ellipsoid and bearing from the position to the nearest point of the trace - with the feature's type and name as the layer states them; the ten nearest features of any kind within the radius; the counts of fault traces, fold axes and other features within it. A **contours** layer (a formation top or thickness - `ZLEVEL`, `ElevTop_ft`, `Thick_ft`): the two contours the position lies between, said only when the two nearest are one contour interval apart, otherwise the nearest contour and its distance; "on" a contour within 250 m. An **areas** layer (sub-basins, plays, an extent): which polygon the position is inside, or none. A **points** layer (picked wells): the nearest picks with their values and distances. A position outside the layer's extent is OUTSIDE LAYER EXTENT - the layer says nothing about that ground, which is not the same as no faults. Written to `reports/wells/<id>/structure.json` and audited as `well.structure`; the layer's addition as `layer.add` with every part's hash.

**The number to check**: the nearest fault's distance and bearing, and the status beside it - `OK`, or `OUTSIDE LAYER EXTENT`, or `NO SURFACE POSITION` (declare it with `set-well --lat --lon --datum`), or `NO LAYER`.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a fault the layer does not hold - a position outside the extent is said to be outside it, never clear of faults. A depth between two contours - the position is said to lie between them, and no value is interpolated. A pick at another well - the nearest picks are given with their distances and are not a pick at this well. A fault's depth, dip, throw or activity - the layer's attributes are carried as the layer states them and nothing is added. A hazard - the distance to a mapped trace is a fact of the map at the map's scale, not a judgement about the well. The map's own accuracy - a compiled regional map is not a site survey; its scale and sources are in the metadata kept beside it and in the citation the site gave.
