# Frac hits

> What each fracturing stage did to the pressure in the wells around it: no response, poroelastic (the rock squeezed, falling when the stage stops), mixed (a hydraulic connection, holding or rising after the stop) or a frac hit (the stage's fluid reaching the well: hundreds of psi or more, not falling); the first response and the fluid pumped by then; the distance from the stage to the well and the earthquakes during the stage.

**The command**: the **Frac hits** tab: the four counts; every stage of every treatment well against every monitored well, each cell its type and peak; a cell picked shows the monitored pressure's departure from its trend around the stage, the threshold, the pumping time and the first response; the rule, the stage tables and the monitored channels (the operator role); **Write the report**. From a terminal: `gea frac-hits --workspace C:\site` prints it; `--well W --stages stages.csv --source "post-job report" --depth-unit ft --utc-offset -5` attaches a treatment well's stage table (CSV or Excel); `--well W --remove` takes it off; `--well M --channel P_gauge_psi` names a monitored well's pressure channel; `--hit-psi 250 --min-psi 5 --k-sigma 5 --source "frac plan"` keeps a rule; `--write` writes the report.

**What it needs**: a treatment well's stage table - one row per stage with its stage number and the start and end of pumping; the perforated interval (top and bottom), the fluid (bbl, gal or m3) and the proppant when given; columns found by their names, a unit in brackets read. Monitored wells: any well whose record is a time series with a pressure channel (the first in psi, or the one declared; pump and standpipe pressures are passed over). The stage times and the gauge record must be on the same clock: the record's own, as written. With both wells surveyed, the distance from each stage's mid-point to the monitored well's nearest point is given; with an earthquake catalogue attached (Operations & seismicity) and the stage clock's hours from UTC declared, the earthquakes within 5 km of the treatment well during the stage and the half hour after are counted.

**How it is read**: the monitored pressure's straight-line trend over the 30 minutes before each stage, and its noise (the median absolute deviation, as a standard deviation); the threshold is 5 times the noise and not less than 5 psi. The first response is the first moment the departure from the trend exceeds the threshold while the stage pumps and stays above it for 60 seconds; the fluid pumped by then is the stage's volume in proportion to its pumping time (the volume to first response of sealed-wellbore pressure monitoring). After the stage stops, the departure over the last two minutes of pumping is set against that 30 minutes later: lower by more than 10 % fell; higher by more than 10 % rose; else held. The type: below the threshold, none; 250 psi or more and not falling, a frac hit; holding or rising, mixed; falling, poroelastic. A frac hit is an alert (`frac.hit`), once per stage and well.

**What it writes**: the stage tables as attached in `completion\frac\<well>\` with `completion\frac\stages.json` (audit lines `frac.stages`, `frac.stages_removed`), the rule and the monitored channels in `completion\frac\settings.json` (audit line `frac.settings`), the audit line `report.frac_hits`, and the report `reports\frac_hits.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON).

**The number to check**: one stage's peak against the gauge's own plot over the stage; the first response against the pumping chart's time; a frac hit against the completion engineer's log.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: where the fracture went - a pressure response says the stage reached the monitored well's rock or its wellbore, not the fracture's shape. The volume to first response - the stage's volume in proportion to its pumping time, not a metered volume at that second. A cause for an earthquake - a count beside a stage is a coincidence in time and place.

**Sources**: Seth, Elliott and Sharma, "Rapid Analysis of Offset Well Pressure Response during Fracturing: Distinguishing between Poroelastic, Hydraulic, and Frac-Hit Responses in Field Data Using Pattern Recognition", URTeC 2020-3129 (the three responses: poroelastic about 1 to 100 psi, falling at the stop; mixed tens to hundreds; direct hundreds to thousands); Haustveit et al., "Monitoring the Pulse of a Well Through Sealed Wellbore Pressure Monitoring", SPE-199731-MS, 2020 (the volume to first response).
