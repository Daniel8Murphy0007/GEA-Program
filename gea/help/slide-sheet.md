# Slide sheet

> Each slide and each rotated stretch of a well, and what the assembly did to the hole in them. A steerable assembly drills two ways: rotated, when the string turns and the hole goes where the assembly tends to take it; and slid, when the string is held and the bent motor is pointed - its toolface - to build, drop or turn. This page makes the directional driller's slide sheet from two things the site already has: the drilling record, which says second by second whether the bit was drilling with the string turning, and the directional survey, which says how the hole's inclination and azimuth changed between its stations.

**The command**: the **Slide sheet** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder's export attached on the Wells tab; with a directional survey attached on the 3D tab it also gives the hole's build and turn and the assembly's tendency. **The wells** has a row a well - the slides, the footage slid and its share of the footage drilled, the median slide, the rate sliding against the rate rotating, the runs of an assembly, and three tendencies; a well's name opens its own sheet below: the charts down the hole, **The runs of an assembly**, and **The slides**. **Write the report** keeps every well's sheet. From a terminal: `gea slide-sheet --workspace C:\site` prints the wells and the first well's runs and slides; `gea slide-sheet --workspace C:\site --well "Smith 2H"` prints that well's; `--write` writes the report; `--json` prints the page; `gea slide-sheet` alone runs the self-test on a hole whose every slide and every degree is known. An assistant asks with the tool `slide_sheet` (`gea help mcp`).

**How it is made**: a sample of the record is slid when the bit is drilling and the rotary speed is under ten turns a minute, and rotated when it is drilling above that (the rig-time page's own rule, `gea help rig-time`). Consecutive samples of one kind make a stretch, carried across connections; a stretch under 3 ft is folded into the one before it; a stretch begins anew when the depth is set back, and depth moving faster than 2,000 ft/h is the depth being set and is left out, with its footage said. A new **run of an assembly** begins each time drilling starts again after the bit has been above 300 ft. For each stretch: from, to, footage, drilling minutes, rate, the mean weight on bit and differential pressure, and - from the survey, carried evenly between the stations either side - the inclination and azimuth at its two ends, their difference being its build and its turn. **The toolface** of a slide, where the record carries one, is the mean direction over the slide weighted by the footage - right or left of high side above 5 degrees of inclination, magnetic below it - with how tightly it was held; a toolface that is the same number at every sample of a slide is a value left standing, and none is given. **The courses** are survey station to survey station (stations nearer than 30 ft taken together): the footage slid and rotated in each, the dogleg, the build and the turn per 100 ft. **The tendency**, for each run and for the well, is the median over its courses with the middle half beside it, and is given only from five courses or more: the build per 100 ft in the courses that were rotated (no more than 5% slid) - the assembly's own build or drop - and, above 5 degrees of inclination, their walk; and in the courses with 10 ft or more slid, the dogleg per 100 ft slid.

**What it writes**: nothing until **Write the report**, which writes `reports/slide_sheet/slide_sheet.json`, `slide_sheet.md`, `slide_sheet.csv` (a row a stretch, slid and rotated, every well), `courses.csv` (a row a survey course) and `runs.csv` (a row a run), and a `slide_sheet.report` line in the audit log.

**The number to check**: on the Utah FORGE public pad (`gea help public-data`), the injection well 16A(78)-32 has 38 slides, 784 ft of its 10,705 ft drilled (7.3%); its curve was built by five of them between 5,892 and 6,797 ft - 28, 37, 60, 51 and 68 ft, the last four each building the hole 2.3 to 3.7 degrees by the survey - and over its 28 courses with a slide the dogleg per 100 ft slid has a median of 7.5 degrees; rotated, the hole built 0.22 degrees per 100 ft. The three vertical wells were nudged back with 27 to 31 slides each, 5.8 to 7.7% of their footage, sliding slower than they rotated (56-32: 27 against 48 ft/h). The production well 16B(78)-32 has 14 slides, 1.8% of its footage: its curve was not slid. Its record is the only one of the five with a toolface, read in ten of its slides.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the slide itself - it is the record's rotary speed under ten turns a minute while drilling, and a string rocked through that, or a rotary-steerable tool, reads otherwise; a toolface the record does not carry; the dogleg per 100 ft slid as the motor's own yield - the whole of a course's dogleg is set against its slide footage, the rotated part's share included; the attitude between two stations, carried evenly from one to the next; the bit's own attitude - a survey station is behind the bit, and the sheet says what the hole did; and a tendency from fewer than five courses.
