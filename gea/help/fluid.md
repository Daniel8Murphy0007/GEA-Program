# Fluid balance

> Where the water went, as far as arithmetic can say: every barrel that went into or came out of a formation through the site's wells, by day, with its density and its dissolved solids, set against the room the formation has for it - the storage the pressure says it has against the storage declared, the barrels the declaration cannot place, each injection well's Hall plot, and its neighbours with the day the pressure front reaches them and the volume it would take for the water itself to. The ledger is the record; the front and the pressure at a neighbour are SIMULATION on the declared formation, and the page says so on every line that is.

**The command**: the Fluid balance tab. Declare each well's role (injection or production), the formation it is completed in, and the density of what it injects (specific gravity, lb/gal or TDS in mg/L - the density from TDS is McCain's 1991 correlation, named) for a period, each with its source; attach the operator's injection or production report as a CSV (a date column and a volume column at least - barrels for the day; average and maximum surface pressure, bottomhole pressure, density or TDS when the report has them; a producer's oil and water columns are its liquid out); declare the formation's porosity, net thickness, area, compressibilities, permeability, viscosity, initial pressure and the formation water's density or TDS. A disposal well whose injection channels are declared (`gea workspace --action set-well --channel-rate ...`) needs no report: its days come from its own measured stream, the same arithmetic the SRA packet uses. From a terminal: `gea fluid --workspace C:\site` prints the balance; `gea fluid --workspace C:\site --declare --well SWD-1 --role injection --formation Ellenburger --source "completion report W-2"`; `gea fluid --workspace C:\site --declare --well SWD-1 --tds-mgl 120000 --source "water analysis 2026-03-14"`; `gea fluid --workspace C:\site --attach C:\site\_incoming\h10_2026.csv --well SWD-1 --source "H-10 filed 2026-09-02"`; `gea fluid --workspace C:\site --formation Ellenburger --porosity 0.08 --thickness-ft 400 --area-acres 2500 --k-md 20 --p-initial-psi 3900 --water-tds-mgl 90000 --source "petrophysics summary 2025"`; `gea fluid --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea fluid` alone runs the self-test on labelled scenes.

**What it writes**: `records/fluid.json` in the workspace (the roles, formations, densities and the list of attached reports with their SHA-256); each report verbatim under `wells/<well>/records/fluid/`; with `--write` or the button, `reports/fluid/fluid_balance.json` and `.md`, a `ledger_<formation>.csv` per formation (in, out, net, cumulative, brine and solids tonnes, bottomhole pressure, the closed-volume rise) and a `well_<id>.csv` per well; every declaration, attachment, removal and report a line in the audit log.

**The arithmetic**: pore volume V_p = 7,758 x area (acres) x net thickness (ft) x porosity, barrels; total compressibility c_t = c_water + c_pore (3e-6 and 4e-6 /psi stand in when none is declared, and the page says so); the declared storage is c_t V_p, barrels per psi - a closed, slightly compressible, single-phase formation raises its pressure by dV / (c_t V_p). The ledger's cumulative net volume against the wells' bottomhole pressures, least squares over the days that have both (ten at least), gives the slope in psi per barrel and so the storage the pressure says the formation has. The ratio to the declared storage reads CLOSED AS DECLARED within a factor of two, MORE ROOM THAN DECLARED above it (the fluid finds room the declaration does not have - an aquifer, leak-off through a fault or the confining layer, a larger connected volume), LESS ROOM below it (a smaller connected volume, or a poorly connected well). The unaccounted volume is the net volume kept less what the declared storage holds at the observed rise; its share of the brine and solids tonnes goes with it. Mass: a barrel is 0.158987 m3; brine tonnes are m3 x specific gravity; solids tonnes are m3 x TDS (mg/L is g/m3) / 1e6. The Hall plot (Hall 1963) is the running sum of (bottomhole pressure - initial pressure) x days against cumulative volume, the first half's slope against the second's: steeper is plugging or a filling formation, easier an opened path. The radius of investigation r = 0.0325 sqrt(k t / (phi mu c_t)) ft (k in md, t in hours) gives the day the pressure front reaches each neighbour's distance (surface to surface, geodesic on the declared datum - no survey is used); the pressure rise there is the line source, dp = 70.6 q mu / (k h) x E1(948 phi mu c_t r^2 / (k t)), at the well's mean rate, now and a year on; the swept radius of the water itself is piston displacement in a cylinder, r = sqrt(V / (pi h phi)), and the volume to reach a neighbour is the same cylinder at its distance. A neighbour in another formation gets its distance and no projection: a path between formations is a fault or a leaking confining layer, which the map must show. The density difference between the injected and the formation water says whether the real front under-rides or over-rides the piston.

**The number to check**: the net volume against the operator's own monthly totals (the ledger adds the report's days; it does not change them); on the labelled scenes `gea fluid` runs, a closed tank's storage fitted within 5 % of its truth and a tank that loses six barrels in ten read MORE ROOM THAN DECLARED with the unaccounted volume within 10 % of what left; 100,000 mg/L of brine at specific gravity 1.068 by McCain; E1(1) = 0.21938.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a volume (a well with no rate channel and no report has NO VOLUME RECORD, named, not a number); a density (undeclared, the mass columns stay empty); a formation (the assignment is the operator's declaration, and the interval statement - the declared disposal interval against the mapped tops - stands beside it so a declaration the map contradicts is seen); where the unaccounted barrels went (the balance names the volume and the mass; the path is the next module's grid and faults, not this page's inference); the front and the pressure at a neighbour (a line source in an infinite uniform formation at a mean rate, labelled SIMULATION - a projection to be checked against the neighbour's own gauge, not a reading of it); the Hall plot's cause (plugging, filling and fracturing all move the slope; the well file decides).
