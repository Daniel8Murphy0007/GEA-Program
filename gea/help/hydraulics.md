# Hydraulics and ECD

> The pressures the mud makes while it circulates: the standpipe pressure part by part (surface lines, string, motor and MWD, bit, annulus), the bit's hydraulics, the equivalent circulating density at every depth against the pore pressure and fracture gradient, the surge and swab of a trip and the fastest a stand may be run or pulled, and the standpipe pressure the rig measured against the model.

**The command**: the **Hydraulics & ECD** tab: the standpipe pressure, the ECD at the bit, the smallest margin to the fracture gradient, the bit's horsepower per square inch, and the rig against the model; where the pressure goes, at the declared rate and at three others; the ECD, pore pressure and fracture gradient by depth with the surge and swab of a trip; the standpipe pressure the rig measured beside the model; the annulus section by section; the declarations (the operator role); **Write the report**. The worked example (Volve 15/9-F-10) is under Scene. From a terminal: `gea hydraulics --example`; `gea hydraulics --workspace C:\site --well SWD-1` prints a well; `--string "BHA:350:6.75:2.875; drill pipe::5:4.276" --hole "9-5/8 casing:3500:8.681; open hole::8.75" --fann "600=60 300=38 200=30 100=21 6=7 3=6" --mw 11 --flow 500 --nozzles "14 14 14 14 14" --bit 8.75 --tools 400 --source "mud report 2026-06-14, BHA report run 3"` declares it; `--write` writes the report.

**What it needs**: a directional survey (for the true vertical depths; a well with none is read on the drilling recorder's own Interpolated TVD, written with its drilling record, and the page says so - an interpolation the recorder made, not a survey); the string from the bit up, each section with its length, outside diameter and bore; the hole from the surface down, each casing string's inside diameter to its shoe (9-5/8 in 47 lb/ft: 8.681 in; 7 in 29 lb/ft: 6.184 in; 13-3/8 in 72 lb/ft: 12.347 in) and then the open hole (the bit size, or the caliper's); the mud weight and the six-speed viscometer's dial (600 and 300 rpm at least; 100 and 3 for the annulus); the pump rate; the nozzles in 32nds of an inch; the pressure drop of the motor and MWD and of the surface lines (at a stated rate) where they are known. The window is the Pore pressure tab's for the well, or one pore pressure and one fracture gradient declared for the hole. The drilling record's standpipe pressure and pump rate, while pumping, are set against the model.

**How it is read**: the mud is a power-law fluid as API RP 13D reads it: in the pipe from the 600 and 300 readings, in the annulus from the 100 and 3. Each length of pipe and of annulus has its velocity, its effective viscosity (Metzner-Reed in the pipe, the slot in the annulus), its Reynolds number and its Fanning friction factor - laminar (16/Re in the pipe, 24/Re in the annulus), transitional from the critical Reynolds number 3470 - 1370 n, turbulent (Dodge and Metzner), blended as Churchill's method joins them - and loses 2 f rho V^2 L / D. The bit loses rho Q^2 / (2 Cd^2 A^2) with Cd 0.95 over the nozzles' total flow area. The ECD at a depth is the mud weight plus the annular loss above it over 0.052 times the true vertical depth. Surge and swab: the string closed at the bottom, the mud pushed at the pipe's peak speed (1.5 times the stand's average) times the clinging constant 0.45 plus the pipe's share of the annulus (Burkhardt); the fastest speeds keep the surge at the bit at or below the fracture gradient and the swab at or above the pore pressure. Against the rig: the median standpipe pressure and pump rate of each 250 ft of bit depth while pumping, and the model at that rate and depth; the median difference is what the declarations leave out (surface lines, a motor, a worn nozzle, the cuttings, a different mud).

**What it writes**: the declarations in `drillstring\hydraulics.json` (audit line `hydraulics.declare`), the audit line `report.hydraulics`, and the report `reports\wells\<well>\hydraulics.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON); `reports\hydraulics_example.*` for the worked example. The signed dossier carries the declarations and each declared well's result.

**The number to check**: the standpipe pressure the rig reads at the declared rate against the model's, and the difference that stays when the surface lines and the motor are declared; the ECD against a downhole pressure-while-drilling tool's where the string carries one.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the ECD - computed from the declared mud and geometry, not read by a downhole tool; the cuttings' weight in the annulus - not added; the pipe centred in the hole and its tool joints left out; surge and swab - the steady-flow estimate at the pipe's peak speed, not the mud's gel or inertia when the pipe starts and stops.

**Sources**: API RP 13D, Rheology and Hydraulics of Oil-well Fluids (the power-law method, the pipe's and the annulus's readings, the critical Reynolds number); Zamora, Roy and Slater, "Comparing a Basic Set of Drilling Fluid Pressure-Loss Relationships to Flow-Loop and Field Data", AADE-05-NTCE-27 (the friction factor's blending); Dodge and Metzner, AIChE Journal 1959 (turbulent friction of power-law fluids); Burkhardt, "Wellbore Pressure Surges Produced by Pipe Movement", JPT, June 1961 (the clinging constant); Bourgoyne, Millheim, Chenevert and Young, Applied Drilling Engineering, SPE Textbook Series 2, 1986 (the bit's hydraulics).
