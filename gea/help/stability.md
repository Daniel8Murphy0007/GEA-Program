# Wellbore stability

> The mud-weight window along a well, with the hole's own direction in it. The Pore pressure tab gives two sides of the window - the pore pressure a mud must hold back and the fracture gradient it must stay under - and they are the same for a vertical hole and a horizontal one. The third side is not: the rock at the wall of a hole carries the stress the removed rock carried, and where that is more than the rock's strength the wall fails and falls in. How much mud that takes depends on the stresses, the rock, and which way the hole points through them. This page works that out at every station of the open hole and sets the mud weight and the circulating density against it.

**The command**: the **Wellbore stability** tab, under Well design. **Scene** has a worked example, Volve 15/9-F-10's survey with stresses and a strength declared for the example. For your own well, declare in **The stresses and the rock** (the operator role), each value with where it came from: the rock's unconfined compressive strength in psi (the one value the page cannot do without) and its friction angle; the overburden, the pore pressure and the least horizontal stress as mud weights in ppg - or leave those three empty where the Pore pressure tab reads the well, and its overburden, pore pressure and fracture gradient are used; the greatest horizontal stress, as a mud weight or as its place between the least stress (0) and the overburden (1), with its azimuth; the breakout accepted on each side of the hole, in degrees of its wall. The mud weight is the Hydraulics tab's, the one its circulating density is worked out with; where that tab has no mud declared for the well, one may be declared here. The open hole starts at the deepest casing shoe unless a depth is declared. `gea stability --workspace C:\site --well "Pad 3 H-1"` prints the window, `--declare ucs_psi=6000 --declare shmin_ppg=13.5 --source "offset leak-off tests and core"` declares values, `--now` adds where the rig is against it, `--write` writes the report, `--example` prints the worked example; without a workspace it runs the self-test.

**How it is worked out**: at each station the three stresses - the overburden and the two horizontal ones, taken as vertical and horizontal - are turned into the hole's own axes by its inclination and azimuth. Round the wall, a degree at a time, the hoop stress, the stress along the hole and the shear between them are Kirsch's for a hole in an elastic rock; they give two principal stresses and the mud's pressure is the third. The wall fails in shear where the greatest effective stress passes the strength by Mohr-Coulomb's rule, UCS + (least effective stress) x tan^2(45 + friction angle / 2). **The collapse pressure** is the least mud pressure at which no point of the wall fails - or no more than the breakout accepted. **The breakdown** is the pressure at which the least stress round the wall falls to minus the tensile strength and the wall parts. **The window** runs from the greater of the pore pressure and the collapse pressure up to the least of the three stresses, above which the mud is lost; every line is an equivalent mud weight. Against it stand the mud weight (pumps off) and, where the Hydraulics tab has the mud and the string, the circulating density at each depth (pumps on). **Which way the hole points** draws the collapse mud weight at the narrowest depth for a hole pointing every way: the middle a vertical hole, the rim a horizontal one.

**At the end of the record**: for a well with a drilling record the page reads the bit, the hole depth and the pump rate of the record's last five minutes, and says whether the mud is below the floor anywhere in the hole drilled so far and whether the circulating density at that pump rate is over the least stress anywhere above the bit. On a record still being written the service checks this every fifteen minutes and raises `stability.mud` or `stability.ecd`, once for each 500 ft of hole the condition is first met in (`gea help alerts`).

**What it writes**: reading the page writes nothing. **Keep** writes `geomech\stability.json` and an audit line `stability.settings`. **Write the report** writes `reports\stability\` with a folder for the well: `stability.json`, `stability.md` and `window.csv` (a row a station), and the audit line `stability.report`.

**The number to check**: `gea stability` with no workspace must end `OK`. Its cases have answers in closed form: a vertical hole with unequal horizontal stresses collapses at (3 SHmax - Shmin - UCS + Pp (q - 1)) / (1 + q) with q = tan^2(45 + friction angle / 2) - 6,125 psi for stresses of 8,500 and 7,000 psi, a pore pressure of 4,500 psi, a strength of 3,000 psi and 30 degrees - and parts at 3 Shmin - SHmax - Pp, 8,000 psi; a horizontal hole along the least stress has the overburden and the greatest stress across it; a hole and the hole pointing the opposite way are the same hole; and the breakout's width at a mud pressure is the closed form's to a degree. On your own well the number to check is a leak-off test against the least stress declared, and a caliper or image log against where the page says the wall fails.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: any of it. The stresses are declared or estimated - the least horizontal stress is a fracture gradient until a leak-off test measures it, and nothing in a drilling record measures the greatest. The strength is one declared figure for the whole open hole, where rock changes bed by bed. The model is elastic, the wall impermeable and the rock the same in every direction; time, temperature, bedding and the mud's chemistry are not in it, and a shale that weakens in a water-based mud falls in above the figure here. A hole can stand below the collapse pressure and fall in above it: the caliper, the image log and the cavings over the shakers say whether it stood. No hole's caliper has yet been set against this page.
