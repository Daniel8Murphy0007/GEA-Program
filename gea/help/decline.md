# Decline curves

> Each well's monthly production fitted by Arps's curves - exponential, hyperbolic or harmonic, turned exponential at a terminal decline (the modified hyperbolic) - forecast month by month to its economic limit, and valued at the SEC 12-month average price or a declared one: the estimated ultimate recovery and the discounted cash flow.

**The command**: the **Decline curves** tab: pick a well; the EUR of oil and gas and the PV; rate against time on a log scale, the months as dots, the fitted curve and the forecast; the fitted qi, b and decline of each stream; the production file and the terms (the operator role); **Write the report**. From a terminal: `gea decline --workspace C:\site --well W` prints it; `--attach production.csv --source "RRC production query"` attaches the monthly production (CSV or Excel); `--remove` takes it off; `--terminal-decline 0.08 --price-basis sec --nri 0.8 --severance 0.046 --opex 8000 --discount 0.10 --start 2023-03 --source "lease terms"` declares the terms (`--price-basis flat --oil-price 70 --gas-price 3` for declared prices); `--as-of 2026-10-01` values as of a date; `--write` writes the report.

**What it reads**: a table of months - a month column (2026-07, 07/2026 or a date), oil (bbl), gas (Mcf), water (bbl), and the days the well produced when given; columns found by their names. A month's rate is its volume over its producing days (the calendar days when none are given).

**How it is computed**: Arps's rate q = qi / (1 + b Di t)^(1/b) (qi exp(-Di t) at b = 0), fitted to the rates by least squares in their logarithm over every b from 0 to the declared largest (2 by default) by 0.01 and a fine grid of Di, qi in closed form, from the highest month (or the declared start); turned exponential where the instantaneous decline reaches the terminal decline (8 % a year effective by default; Robertson's modified hyperbolic). Declines are shown effective annual (secant for the hyperbolic). The forecast runs a month at a time from the month after the last: each stream's volume is its curve's cumulative over the month; the net cash flow is the revenue times the net revenue interest less the severance tax, less the operating cost; it stops at the first month whose net is not positive (the economic limit) or at 50 years. The EUR is the production to date plus the forecast. The PV discounts the net mid-month at the declared rate (10 % a year). The price is the SEC's - the unweighted average of the first-day-of-the-month prices of the 12 months before the as-of date, from the EIA WTI and Henry Hub series shipped with the program - or the declared flat price; gas at the Henry Hub price per MMBtu times the declared heat content (1.0 MMBtu/Mcf by default).

**What it writes**: the production as attached in `economics\production\<well>\` with `economics\production\production.json` and the terms (audit lines `production.attach`, `production.remove`, `production.terms`), the audit line `report.decline_curves`, and the report `reports\wells\<well>\decline_curves.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON).

**The number to check**: one month's rate against the volume over its days in the file; the EUR against the production to date and a reservoir engineer's own forecast; the SEC price against the twelve first-of-month quotes it lists.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: reserves - a forecast is not a reserves report, and nothing here is classified as proved or otherwise. The future price - the SEC average or the declared price is held flat for the life of the forecast. What the well will make - the fitted curve carries the past forward; a change of operation breaks it.

**Sources**: Arps, "Analysis of decline curves", Trans. AIME 160 (1945); Robertson, "Generalized hyperbolic equation", SPE-18731 (1988); U.S. SEC, Modernization of Oil and Gas Reporting (2008), Regulation S-X Rule 4-10(a)(22)(v).
