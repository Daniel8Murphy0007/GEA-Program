# Kick & loss

> Kick and loss detection, replayed over every drilling record. A well in balance returns what is pumped into it: when the formation gives fluid more comes back and the pits rise, and when it takes mud less comes back and the pits fall. The recorder writes the two measurements that show it - the volume in the active pits and the flow in the return line - and this page reads them against their own baselines: the flowback at each pumps-off against the fingerprint of the connections before it, and the pits while circulating against their own trend, each event named by what the return flow did.

**The command**: the **Kick & loss** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder export with a pump output and an active pit volume (a return-flow channel as well lets a gain be told from mud added). The tiles count the flowbacks above their fingerprint, the kick-like gains and the losses. Pick a well: the chart is the pit gain at every pumps-off, the ones above their fingerprint in black; **The places to look** lists those and the circulating events, each with **show** - the pit volume, the pump output and the return flow around it, a panel each on one clock. **The rules' numbers** at the foot are changed by an operator, with where they came from. On a record still being written an event raises an alert once (`gea help alerts`). From a terminal: `gea kick --workspace C:\site` prints it; `gea kick --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea kick --workspace C:\site --set gain_min_bbl=10 --source "well control procedure, rev 4"` changes a number; `gea kick --workspace C:\site --json` gives the page as JSON; `gea kick` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/kick.json` in the workspace when a number is changed (the numbers and their source; audit line `kick.settings`); with `--write` or the button, `reports/kick/kick.json`, `kick.md`, `flowbacks.csv` (a row a pumps-off: the gain at 30, 60, 120, 180 and 300 s, its status and its excess) and `events.csv` (a row a circulating event), and a line `kick.report`.

**The arithmetic**: the pumps are ON above 30 gal/min, or a tenth of the 90th percentile of the pumping rate if that is more. A PUMPS-OFF is the pumps stopping after two minutes or more of pumping and staying off a minute or more. Its flowback is the pit volume above what it was in the 16 s before the pumps stopped, read at 30, 60, 120, 180 and 300 s for as long as the pumps stay off. Its FINGERPRINT is the median of the same reading over the last eight pumps-offs whose pump rate before was within 30 % of this one's - four at least - and their scatter (1.4826 times the median absolute deviation); a pumps-off with a step of 5 bbl inside half a minute in it (after its first half minute) has a transfer in it and is left out. A flowback is ABOVE when its last two readings are each above the fingerprint by four scatters and by 4 bbl, BELOW when both are below by as much. Where there is a return flow, the fraction of its level before the pumps stopped that is still running at the same moment is set against its own fingerprint, and said to be still running when it is above it by three scatters and by a tenth. CIRCULATING is judged in half-minute averages: the pumps on throughout, five minutes after they came on, and within a tenth of their own ten-minute median. The ten-minute change in pit volume is set against the median of the same change over the two hours of judged circulating before it (twenty values at least), and is an event when it is off that by four scatters and by 8 bbl and the pits themselves moved that way by half as much (a fall that slows is not a gain); a window with a 5 bbl step between two half-minutes in it is a transfer and is not judged. An event is KICK-LIKE when the return flow rose by a tenth of its level over the same ten minutes, LOSSES when it fell by a tenth, and a gain or a fall with the returns flat otherwise; with no return flow in the record it is a gain or a fall and no more.

**The number to check**: one flowback against the trip sheet or the driller's own fingerprint for that connection; one event against the daily report for that hour - what was added to the pits, what was dumped, whether a flow check was made. On the self-test `gea kick` runs, a record is built with a well that flows at one connection, a flowback 5 bbl short at another, a transfer, a gain with the returns up, the same gain with the returns flat and a fall with the returns down, and each is found where it was put and nothing else is.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: an influx - a flowback above its fingerprint, or a gain with the returns up, is what a kick looks like in these two channels, and a flow check, a shut-in and the pressures say whether it was one; the size of a kick - the pit gain is a volume at surface, and whatever was added or dumped in the same minutes is in it; a clean bill - no event means these rules did not fire, not that the well was in balance: gas in solution, a slow seep and a tank that is not counted in the total are invisible here; the return flow as a rate where it is a paddle - its percent is read only against itself; a cause for a fall - losses, a dump valve and the shakers all take volume; and a flowback below its fingerprint as losses - the return line lined up to the trip tank does the same.
