# The tutorial

> Every step of GEA and the programs around it - the control panel, the Reader, the Recorder on the pad, GEA-VPN, GEA Driller - as lessons: the office's first, the people on the pad after, each naming the pages it is done on, what you press, and how you know it worked. Every tab of the control panel is reached by a lesson, and each tab's help panel says which.

**The command**: the **Tutorial** tab. The card at the top says how many lessons are done, about how long the rest take at the screen, and has the button to the next one. The lessons are listed by part - Getting started; Wells and their data; Watching the pad; Where the wells are; Drilling; Completion, production and disposal; Seismic; Reports for the client; Keeping it running; On the pad; The driller's agent - each with who it is for, how long it takes and the tabs it walks through. **Find a lesson** narrows the list by a word - a tab's name, a help page's, a word of the title; **For** shows the office's lessons or the pad's alone; **only what is left** hides what you have ticked. Open a lesson: a button for each page it is about, the numbered steps that say what to press, **You know it worked when** for what to look for, and **Mark this lesson done**, which ticks it off for you and goes on to the next (each account has its own progress). Several lessons end with **On the practice pad**: what to look at on the public Utah FORGE site, once the lesson *A real pad to practise on* has made it. **Print the handout** opens the whole course as one page for the people who do not sit at this screen, and the browser's Print makes the PDF; **Write the handout into exports\tutorial** (the operator role) writes the same as `GEA-Tutorial-0.96.0.html` and `.md` into the workspace. On every other tab, the help panel under the heading names the lesson that teaches that tab. From a terminal: `gea tutorial` lists the lessons, `gea tutorial --lesson 3` prints one, `gea tutorial --lesson install` the same by name, `gea tutorial --handout --workspace C:\site` writes the handout into the workspace, `gea tutorial --handout --out C:\site\GEA-Tutorial.html` writes it anywhere. The lessons are Markdown files under the program's `help\tutorial` folder.

**What it writes**: `records\tutorial_progress.json` in the workspace (who finished which lesson, when, by the lesson's name - so a course re-ordered by a new version keeps everyone's ticks); `exports\tutorial\GEA-Tutorial-0.96.0.html` and `.md` when the handout is written, with the audit line `tutorial.handout`; `tutorial.print` when the handout is opened from the page. Reading a lesson writes nothing, and what the list is narrowed to is not kept past the window.

**The number to check**: the card's "done" count against the number of lessons, and which lesson is next. A lesson's own check is its **You know it worked when** - the thing on the screen or the disk that is there only if the steps were done; a tick without it is a tick. The course's own check is in the acceptance gate: no tab of the control panel without a lesson that walks through it, no help page without a lesson that names it, no lesson pointing at a page that is not there.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a lesson done is a person's tick, not the program's verdict - the Setup tab's checklist is what says what the workspace still needs, and the Verification tab's gate is what says the program works; the tutorial says how to get there, and nothing it says changes a report. The minutes are what each lesson says it takes at the screen, added up; a lesson that says a day to build the box is counted by its reading time.

Related: `gea help help`, `gea help start`, `gea help setup`, `gea help public-data`.
