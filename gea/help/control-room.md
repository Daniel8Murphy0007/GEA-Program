# The control room

> A wall screen for the doghouse or the office, read from across the room: one tile per well, worst first, its state in a shape and a word - Serious, Watch, Good, No data - with the reasons and the numbers that matter, refreshed by itself every 30 seconds. The shift handover beside it: what is open, what is being watched, what was done, signed by the outgoing operator and acknowledged by the incoming one, both in the audit log.

**The command**: the Control room tab, then **Open the wall**. On the wall, F (or the button) is full screen and Esc comes back to the Control room tab. When there are more wells than fit, the wall shows as many as fit and turns the page every 15 seconds, the worst wells always on the first page; a phone scrolls instead. The page asks the browser to keep the screen awake and says whether it was allowed (a browser allows it over https or on the computer running the service; otherwise set the display never to sleep). To write the handover, fill in any of Open, Watching and Done on the Control room tab and press **Sign and hand over** (the operator role); the note goes up on the wall until someone else acknowledges it with **I have read this** - the author cannot acknowledge their own note. For a screen that should not sit under anyone's account, an administrator issues a **wall pass** on the same tab (named for the screen, 1 to 365 days); on that screen open the program's address, choose **This is a wall screen** and type the code once. A pass shows the wall and the latest handover and nothing else, and can be revoked at any time. From a terminal: `gea wall --workspace C:\site` prints the board; `gea handover --workspace C:\site` lists the notes, `--write --actor NAME --open TEXT --watching TEXT --done TEXT` signs one and `--ack ID --actor NAME` acknowledges one.

**What it writes**: the handover notes and their acknowledgements, one line each, appended to `handover/notes.jsonl` in the workspace and never rewritten; each note keeps the board's counts at the moment it was written. Every note, acknowledgement, pass issued, pass entered, wrong pass and pass revoked is a line in the audit log. The passes are in `wall/passes.json` - the code is shown once when it is issued and only its SHA-256 hash is kept. The wall itself writes nothing.

**The number to check**: a tile against the page it summarises - its alarm count against the well's Alarms page, its gauge state against the drift report, its bottom, bit and on-bottom hours against the drilling record and the chart at the end of the record. The rule a tile is judged by: **Serious** is an unacknowledged P1 or P2 alarm, a gauge reading off with no explanation, or a live feed down or silent for five times its stale limit (fifteen minutes at least); **Watch** is any other active or shelved alarm, a gauge drift that has an explanation, or a live feed past its stale limit, degraded or stopped; **Good** is a well with reports and none of these; **No data** is a well with no reports and no live sample. The time at the top right says how long ago the service last answered.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a recorded well as live - a file or catalogue well is described at the end of its record, says so on its tile, and is never called stale; the latest rate of penetration as the rate now - it is the mean over the last ten minutes of drilling in the record, shown only when drilling was within half an hour of the record's end, else "off bottom"; a wall whose service has stopped answering as current - after two missed refreshes a notice across the screen says how old everything is and every tile is greyed, and a signed-out wall shows nothing old at all; a handover note as a check of the wells - it is what one operator wrote for the next.
