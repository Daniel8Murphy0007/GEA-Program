# Public data

> A real pad from published records. The Utah FORGE project - the U.S. Department of Energy's geothermal field laboratory near Milford, Utah - publishes the rig recorders of five wells drilled through the same basin fill and granite between 2017 and 2023, under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. This page turns those files into a site of its own, so the rig time, the roadmap, the drilling alerts and the benchmark run on real records whose figures anyone may check and repeat. The program does not contain the files and does not fetch them: the site downloads them in a browser, and the program checks each against the size and the SHA-256 it had when this release was checked with it.

**The command**: the **Public data** tab. **The files** lists, for each well, the file to download, its size, its hash, and the page of the Geothermal Data Repository it is on (a link). Download them into one folder - two wells or more make a pad; about 2.8 GB for all five. For an administrator the form then opens filled in: the folder is a `forge` folder in your Downloads (or the Downloads folder itself) when two or more of the files are in it, and the site is the public-data site this computer already has, or a `forge-site` folder beside the current site's - change either, **Check the folder** to see which files are there, and **Make the site**. Making it takes some minutes: each file is hashed, the three layouts that are not the recorder's own export are written out as one, and each well is attached. The new site is listed under Administration, Workspaces, and opens in its own window like any other. From a terminal: `gea public-pad --list` prints the files and where each is published; `gea public-pad --folder C:\forge` says which are in the folder; `gea public-pad --folder C:\forge --verify` hashes them; `gea public-pad --folder C:\forge --build --site C:\forge-site` makes the site, or adds to it the wells it does not have yet; `gea public-pad` alone runs the self-test on stand-ins in each published layout.

**The stimulation records** (optional): the same repository publishes each stage's pumping record for the two deviated wells - `16A78-32_Stimulation_Stage 1.zip`, `Stage 2.zip` and `Stage 3.zip` (April 2022), `16A Frac Data.zip` and `16B Frac Data.zip` (April 2024); the tab lists them with their pages, sizes and hashes. Put them in the same folder, or in a folder named `stimulation` inside it, and **Make the site** attaches each stage to its well - sixteen stages, about a minute - for the Frac treatment tab (`gea help frac-treatment`); made again later, the site takes the stages it does not have yet. The 2022 stages are a frac van's data-acquisition export opened in a spreadsheet, the 2024 ones a spreadsheet with a stacked header; both are read as they are.

**The surveys** (optional, under a megabyte each): the same repository publishes each well's directional survey, and with them in the folder **Make the site** places each well - its wellhead declared, its elevation with the file it came from, its survey attached - so the 3D, Simulation, Collision and Map tabs draw the pad; a site made before is given them by **Make the site** again. Five survey files and one more: `16B(78)-32 Well Survey.zip` (the directional company's final report as text: the wellhead on UTM zone 12N in US survey feet, the kelly bushing elevation, azimuths from grid north); `16A(78)-32 Survey.xlsx` (the planning package's design report, a word a cell: the platform reference point, the kelly bushing elevation, azimuths corrected to grid north; it does not name its grid, and is read on the one 16B's report names); `Well 56-32 trajectory survey data.zip` (the survey report printed to PDF, and beside it the publisher's own positions on the UTM grid, which give the wellhead and show the azimuths were laid on grid north); `78B-32 directional survey (1).zip` (a spreadsheet's CSV with the wellhead's latitude and longitude beside the table); `Directional_Survey_well_58_32.zip` (the survey report printed to PDF, alone); and `Utah FORGE Well and Seismic Locations.csv`, the field's GPS survey of its wells (gdr.openei.org/submissions/1358), which gives 58-32 its wellhead and 58-32 and 78B-32 the ground elevation their survey files lack. Without that last file 78B-32 is put on the map and not drawn in 3D, and 58-32 is not placed; the tab says so for each. A PDF is read only when its pages are plain compressed text, a word at its own position, as these two are. Every path is computed here by minimum curvature from the inclinations and azimuths and compared with the positions the file itself carries; the tab prints the largest difference for each well (hundredths of a foot to a tenth), and how each wellhead, elevation and north was read.

**What it writes**: the site's workspace in the folder named, with each well attached, and `records/public_pad.json` in it - the dataset, the licence, the credit, and for each well the source file, its size and SHA-256 as found, whether they match, and what was done to it, and for a placed well the survey file, its hash, the wellhead, the elevation and which it is, the north its azimuths were read from, and how each was read; a line `public_pad.build` in its audit log, and a `well.trajectory` line for each survey. The first time, the build also writes each well's drilling record, which the Simulation, Control room and Drill string tabs read; where it cannot (the site is open in another window and busy), it says so and the Home tab's Refresh writes it. Every page and report of that site then ends with the credit line the licence asks for. Nothing is written in the folder the files are in, and nothing is sent anywhere.

**What is done to each file**: `56-32` and `78B-32` are the recorder's own one-second export and are attached as they are. They carry no on-bottom flag, so the drilling record takes on bottom as the bit within 0.1 ft of the hole depth, and says so. `16B(78)-32` is a ten-second export of over a hundred channels inside a zip: the channels the program reads are written out, and the true vertical depth is added from the well's own published survey when `16B(78)-32 Well Survey.zip` is in the folder too - the hole is deviated 65 degrees, and without the survey its measured depth stands in. `58-32` is the 2017 recorder in another layout: its U.S.-unit columns are written out under the names the program reads; it has no weight on bit and no differential pressure, and the pages that need them say so for it. `16A(78)-32` is another recorder's layout with no bit depth: the bit depth is RECONSTRUCTED - the hole depth while the on-bottom flag is set, unmoved while the slips are set, otherwise moved by what the block moved - exact on bottom, approximate over a trip, and marked as reconstructed on every page that uses it. Two things in these records the drilling record now reads that it did not before: a depth set back thousands of feet and regained through hole already drilled, with a fraction of the weight on bit it was drilled with, is the string reamed back in with the depth tracker counting (REAMED BACK, excluded - `gea help edr`); and a ten-second record's pipe speeds are taken over two samples either side, where five seconds held no two samples.

**The number to check**: each well's total depth and dates against its published end-of-well report on the same repository page; the separation of the two deviated wells on the Collision tab - with the published surveys, 16B(78)-32 runs about 297 ft (90 m) from 16A(78)-32 at its toe, the offset the project drilled it at (it reports the producer some 300 ft above the injector); the hash of each file against the one printed here - `gea public-pad --folder C:\forge --verify`. A file whose hash differs is used all the same and said to differ: the repository revises its files, and the figures may then differ from another site's.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the reconstructed bit depth of 16A(78)-32, and so that well's trip speeds; the depth of the three vertical wells to better than the height of a rig floor - only their ground level is published, so their depths are counted from the ground and they are drawn up to some 30 ft too deep (the two deviated wells are from the kelly bushing their reports give); the direction of 58-32 and 78B-32 to better than the tab says - which north their azimuths are from is not stated in any file, they are read as from true north, and the tab prints what grid north or an uncorrected magnetic north would move (a few feet, and 15 to 33 ft, in holes that reach 78 and 167 ft from their wellheads); the position uncertainty on the Collision tab as these wells' own - it is the default tool model, no survey program being declared; a true vertical depth where the measured depth stands in for one; a comparison between these wells as like for like - they were drilled six years apart, in three hole sizes, two of them deviated, with the bits of their day; the hashes as a warrant of the data - they say a file is the one this release was checked with, not what the recorder recorded; and anything about a client's wells - this is a pad to learn the pages on and to show their figures with, and its rock is granite.
