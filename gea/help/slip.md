# Slip tendency on the mapped faults

> After "how far is the nearest mapped fault" comes "how close to slipping is it, under this injection". The open method (Morris slip tendency; the fault slip potential arithmetic of Walsh and Zoback) is a stress state resolved on a fault plane: the vertical and the two horizontal stresses on the plane, the pore pressure taken off, the shear stress against the friction times the effective normal stress. This program does that arithmetic on inputs the site declares with their sources, on the strikes the map gives and the dips the layer states or the site declares, and prints beside every number what it rests on.

**The command**: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action set-stress --sv 1.05 --shmin 0.65 --shmax 0.95 --pp 0.45 --gradients-source "Lund Snee & Zoback 2018, Delaware Basin" --shmax-azimuth 92 --azimuth-source "WSM 2016 records within 100 km" --dips 60 75 90 --mu 0.6 --delta-pp 300` declares the stress state (psi/ft gradients; the sources required). `--action add-stress-map --file wsm2016.csv --citation "World Stress Map 2016, Heidbach et al., CC BY 4.0"` keeps the WSM database so `--action wsm --lat --lon --radius-km` gives the SHmax azimuth from the records near a position (the axial mean of the A/B/C-quality records with its spread and count) - and a well with no declared azimuth takes it from there, stated. `--action add-layer --name "Hennings 2021 basement faults" --layer Hennings_2021_BSMT_Pp2Slip.zip --feature-kind fault --citation "..."` brings a faults layer whose attributes do not say "fault" (every feature is declared one); a UTM or state-plane layer is unprojected from its .prj parameters. `--action slip --well <id> --radius-km 25 [--delta-pp 300] [--depth-ft 9000]` writes the statement; the well page's "Slip tendency" card and the SRA packet's section 2e carry it. `gea slip --layer faults.zip --lat --lon --depth-ft --sv --shmin --shmax --shmax-azimuth [--wsm wsm2016.csv] --delta-pp` answers for any position without a workspace.

**What it writes**: the stress state at the depth (the midpoint of the declared interval, or the depth given) and the regime its ordering implies; for every mapped fault trace within the radius: its distance, its length-weighted strike and the angle to SHmax, its dip (from the layer's `dip` attribute when present, else each declared dip), the normal and shear stress on the plane, the effective normal stress at the ambient and the increased pore pressure, the slip tendency at both, the pore-pressure increase that brings the plane to the friction line and the margin left after the declared increase - BELOW or PAST THE FRICTION LINE, for the worst and the best declared case; where the layer publishes its own pressure-to-slip (the BEG/TexNet `Pp2S_MPa` of Hennings et al. 2021, computed under their calibrated stress model), that value beside this arithmetic with the difference. The measured friction envelope from the catalogue (`gea catalog --friction`) printed last, so the declared coefficient is seen against measurements. Written to `reports/wells/<id>/slip.json`, audited as `well.slip`; the stress state as `stress.declare`, the map as `stress_map.add`.

**The number to check**: the pressure to slip after the increase on the nearest traces, and the count past the friction line in the worst case against the best - a count that flips between the two is a statement about the declared dip and friction, not about the fault. Where the layer publishes its own value, the difference column: a large one means the declared gradients are not the published model's, and the published model is the one with a paper behind it.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a stress state - every gradient and the SHmax azimuth are declared with their source, or taken from the World Stress Map with the count and spread printed; nothing is measured here. A dip - a map carries none; the layer's own attribute or the declared range is used and named on the line. A friction coefficient for the fault - Byerlee's 0.6 unless declared, printed against the catalogue's measured 0.21-0.90. A probability of an earthquake - the method gives a pressure margin on a plane under stated assumptions; it does not say the fault exists at depth, is connected, or will slip. An attribution - an event near a trace is a distance, as the structure page says.
