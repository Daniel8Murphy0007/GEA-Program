# Tools and ports

> What this program knows how to measure with, what is run in the hole around it, and what it knows how to speak. The downhole tool library is every class of downhole tool in use across the industry - bits, motors and steerable systems, MWD and LWD, the drill string and its subs, casing and cementing equipment, completion and artificial lift, wireline and cased-hole tools, fishing and milling, test tools - each with a one-line description on hover and a tutorial that opens on a click; the fleet is your own tools in those classes, each tied to its datasheet. The gauge library is the downhole and surface tools - each with the citation its numbers came from, the conditions it is rated for, and the telemetry interface it declares; the port registry is the file formats and live protocols that bring a tool's readings in. The page draws the line between them: a tool declares an interface, a port speaks one, and a tool whose interface no port in this build speaks is named rather than hidden.

**The command**: the page is Tools & ports on the dashboard. Point at a tool class (or Tab to it) for its one-line description; click, tap or press Enter for the tutorial - what it is, where it runs, how it works, what it shows in the data, what to watch, what this program does with it, the published figures and the pages it was checked against; Esc or Close puts it away. `gea tools --action classes` lists the classes (`--category measure`, `--find packer`), `gea tools --action tutorial --class pdm_motor` prints one, `gea tools --action fleet-add --workspace C:\site --class pdm_motor --maker Acme --model M1 --serial A100 --datasheet "Acme M1 datasheet rev B"` adds a tool of yours (an operator may also add it from the tutorial), `--action fleet` lists them and `--action fleet-remove --id ...` takes one out. `gea tools` alone prints the same register at the command line (`--json` for the machine record, `--rating --station 9850:quartz_pt_geoq177_16k ...` for the rating check). A tool whose vendor published no numbers is listed as PARAMETERS_USER_SUPPLIED: `gea config` takes your own datasheet (`load_gauge_spec_json`) and the tool is then simulated and aged from it. The live protocols each name what the site must supply before they run and what to rehearse against - `gea wits0-sim`, `gea etp-sim`, `gea seedlink-sim`, or the stand-in slave, outstation, device and historian the field ports ship with.

**What it writes**: the fleet, to `config\fleet.json` in the workspace, every addition and removal in the audit log; everything else on the page is a register, not a record. Per tool class it states the tutorial, the published figures - each the cited page's own words and unit, quoted as read on the date the pages were fetched, most of them one maker's product and never a limit for the class - and the sources. Per gauge it states: the class, what it measures, the temperature and pressure ratings, the published aging rate in %FS/yr where the datasheet gives one (and the reason there is none where it does not), the interface it declares, the ports that can carry it, and its source. Per port: the transport, the status (IMPLEMENTED, or DECLARED_SITE_DETAILS_REQUIRED with what is missing), and what it speaks to. The rating check returns, per station, the temperature and pressure at that depth, the tool's ratings and whether the station is within them, with the basis of the conditions printed beside the answer.

**The number to check**: a published figure against the page it cites, and your own tool's rating against its datasheet, never against the class's figures; the aging rate against your own datasheet before you trust a drift report built on it, and the rating check's per-station temperature against the tool's rating - the check that catches a 177 °C gauge hung in a 233 °C zone before the well does.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a figure for a whole class of tools - the tutorials carry no numbers at all, and every number on a tool's card is quoted from the page it cites; a rating for a fleet tool its datasheet does not give - the fleet carries the datasheet citation and nothing more unless you give the numbers; a vendor number nobody published - a tool with no datasheet rate carries none and raises rather than inventing one, and the page says PARAMETERS_USER_SUPPLIED. A rating as a prediction - a station within its ratings is arithmetic on the vendor's number and the declared conditions, not a promise the tool survives. The conditions themselves - without a well profile they are the gradients declared on the page, and the answer prints which basis it used. A port as a connection - a port listed here speaks a protocol; whether the site's own device answers is what the Patch panel shows.
