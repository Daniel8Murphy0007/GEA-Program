# Drilling roadmap

> What the fastest stands on the pad were drilled with, stretch by stretch. Every well's drilling record is cut into stands of 100 ft; each stand goes into the stretch of hole it was drilled in - 1,000 ft of true vertical depth down to the heel, 1,000 ft along the hole beyond it; in each stretch the fastest quarter of the stands is found, and the window is what those stands had in common: the weight on bit, the rotary speed, the flow and the differential pressure they ran. Then the roadmap checks itself: each well is set against the roadmap made from the other wells, and the page says whether its stands nearer the window were in fact its faster ones.

**The command**: the **Roadmap** tab, once two or more wells have a drilling recorder export attached on the Wells tab (one well gives a roadmap of its own stands and no check). The tiles give the wells, the stands, the stretches that have a window and what the check found. **The roadmap** is the table to drill the next well from: for each stretch and for rotary and slide stands apart, the median rate of every stand beside the rate of the best quarter, the window of each parameter, and the well that was fastest there. **Well by well** shows each well's own rate in the stretch and what it ran, the fastest in bold - where one well ran 115 rpm and another 60 through the same rock, this is where it shows. **The check** gives, for each well, how many of its stands were inside the other wells' window, the rate of the half nearer the window against the farther half, the rank correlation of distance with rate, and the room. **The next stand** gives the window at the bit of the well whose record ends last, and its last ten minutes against it. From a terminal: `gea roadmap --workspace C:\site` prints it; `gea roadmap --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea roadmap --workspace C:\site --json` gives the page as JSON; `gea roadmap` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: nothing until asked. With `--write` or the button, `reports/roadmap/roadmap.json`, `roadmap.md` and `roadmap.csv` (a row a stretch and mode: the stands, the wells, the two rates and each parameter's low, middle and high) in the workspace, and a line `roadmap.report` in the audit log. The signed dossier carries the report when it has been written.

**The arithmetic**: a stand is 100 ft of hole depth; its on-bottom hours are the seconds the record was drilling in it (hole a later sidetrack abandoned is left out), its rate the footage over those hours, its parameters the time-weighted means over the same seconds; a stand with less than 80 ft of footage is incomplete and left out. A stand is ROTARY when the rotary turned for 70 % or more of its drilling time, SLIDE at 30 % or less, MIXED between - mixed stands are counted and belong to no window. The heel is the first stand from which the hole gains or loses less than 17 ft of TVD in 100 ft of hole (within ten degrees of horizontal) with nine in ten of the stands beyond it the same, so a lateral drilled up or down dip is still a lateral; above it a stretch is 1,000 ft of the recorder's own TVD, because wells on a pad meet the same rock at the same TVD and not the same measured depth. A record with no TVD channel has its measured depth stand in - right for a vertical hole, wrong for a deviated one - and the page names every well this was done for; a single stand with no TVD in a record that has one is placed by the stands either side of it. A stand that reads faster than 2,000 ft/h is a depth the recorder was given, not hole a bit made, and is left out. A stretch needs twelve stands for a window. The best stands are those at or above the 75th percentile of the rate; the window of a parameter is the 25th to the 75th percentile of what the best stands ran; the pace is their median rate. The check makes the roadmap again without the well; a stand's distance from the window is the largest shortfall or excess of its weight on bit, rotary speed (rotary stands) or flow beyond the window, as a fraction of the window's middle, and a stand within a tenth is inside. In each stretch the well's stands are split at their median distance; the nearer half's rate and the farther half's are averaged over the stretches by footage. The room is the sum, over the stands slower than the pace, of the hours each took beyond its footage at the pace.

**The number to check**: one stretch's fastest well against the daily drilling reports for that interval - the rate and what was run; and the check's nearer against farther, which should be positive on every well, with a negative rank correlation, for the roadmap to be worth drilling from. On the self-test `gea roadmap` runs, three labelled wells go through rock with a known best weight and speed in each stretch: the windows hold them, every well's nearer half is the faster by a quarter or more, and a pad whose rates were shuffled shows no gain.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a cause - the fastest stands were drilled in this window, and whether the window made them fast is what the check supports or does not; the bit, the motor and the mud, which the record does not carry and which differ between wells (the Drill string tab holds the runs); a formation boundary - a stretch is a depth, named for the mapped formation only where the workspace has formation-top layers and the well a surface position and a reference elevation; a limit - the window is what was run, not what the string, the motor or the hole can take (Torque & drag, Hydraulics & ECD and the motor's own ratings are other tabs); the room - the most there was to gain at the pace of the best quarter, never a forecast of the next well.
