# Torque and drag

> The hook load and torque the drill string should take at each bit depth, by the soft-string model: pulling out (pick-up), running in (slack-off) and rotating off bottom, for the declared friction factors and a fan of others (the broomstick); against the rig's own hook loads and torque from the drilling record, and the friction factors that fit them, cased and open hole apart. Volve 15/9-F-10 with an example string is the worked example.

**The command**: the **Torque & drag** tab: the hook load against bit depth - pick-up, slack-off and rotating for the declared friction factors, faint lines for 0.1 to 0.5, the rig's own points - and the off-bottom torque; the friction factors that fit the rig; the string, the mud, the friction factors and the block weight (the operator role); **Write the report**. From a terminal: `gea torque-drag --workspace C:\site --well W` prints it; `--example` the worked example; `--string "BHA:300:6.75:95; drill pipe::5:22.6:6.625" --mud 10 --mu-cased 0.25 --mu-open 0.3 --block 40 --source "BHA report, pipe tally"` declares the string; `--write` writes the report.

**What it needs**: the well's directional survey; the string, from the bit up - each section's name, length (ft; the top section has none: it runs to the surface), outside diameter (in), weight per foot in air with its tool joints (lb/ft) and the diameter it turns on (in; the tool joint's) - with the mud density and its source. The deepest casing shoe declared (Rig time) divides cased from open hole. For the rig's side, a drilling record with hook load, bit depth and hole depth (block height, pump output or standpipe pressure, rotary speed and torque when it has them).

**How it is computed**: the soft-string model of Johancsik, Friesen and Dawson (SPE-11380-PA, 1984), element by element from the bit up every 30 ft of the survey's arcs: the normal force N = sqrt((F d_alpha sin theta)^2 + (W sin theta - F d_theta)^2) from the tension below F and the buoyed weight W (weight in air times 1 - mud/65.5 ppg); the tension above F + W cos theta plus (pulling out) or minus (running in) mu N, or without friction rotating; the torque plus mu N r rotating, r the contact radius. The hook load is the surface tension plus the travelling block, declared or estimated from the record (the rig-time rule). From the record, the rig states of Rig time give the hook load tripping out and in with the pumps off, and the hook load and torque rotating off bottom with the pumps on; each is the median of each 100 ft of bit depth. The friction factor that fits each is found over the bins in cased hole first, then in open hole with the cased factor held.

**What it writes**: the string and its figures in `drillstring\torque_drag.json` (audit line `torque_drag.declare`), the audit line `report.torque_drag`, and the reports `reports\wells\<well>\torque_drag.*` (or `reports\torque_drag_example.*`; PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON).

**The number to check**: the string's buoyed weight against the hook load of a vertical well at TD less the block; one depth's pick-up against the driller's broomstick sheet; a fitted friction factor against the field's usual range.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the friction factor - it is fitted, a number that makes the model match the rig, not a property measured in the hole. Where the string touches - the soft-string model lays the string on the hole as a cable, without its stiffness or the hole's clearance. Buckling - a string pushed past its buckling load in compression is not modelled.

**Sources**: Johancsik, Friesen and Dawson, "Torque and Drag in Directional Wells - Prediction and Measurement", SPE-11380-PA, JPT 36(6), 1984. The worked example: Volve 15/9-F-10's survey (Equinor's released data) with a string, mud and friction factors declared for the example.
