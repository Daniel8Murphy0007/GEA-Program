# Calibration

> The plume and the front, run many times over the numbers nobody knows exactly and ranked against what is measured - a SIMULATION. The Pressure plume and Fluid front tabs each give one projection from one set of declared numbers. This page runs both over a range for the permeability, the thickness, the porosity and the compressibility, with each fault layer open in half the runs and sealing in the other half; scores every run against the pressures in the ledger (a gauge in an observation well, an injector's or a producer's own bottomhole pressure) and the producers' measured TDS; keeps the runs the measurements do not contradict; and gives every forecast as a range - what the declarations alone allow beside what the measurements leave - with the measurement that would narrow it most.

**The command**: the Calibration tab, once the Fluid balance tab has an injection well with a formation, a volume record and a surface position, and the formation its permeability, thickness and porosity. **Run the calibration** starts the runs (a fraction of a second each; 48 unless changed) and keeps the result; the page says CURRENT while everything the result was computed from is the same and STALE once the ledger, a declaration or a setting has changed. A well with a gauge and no flow is declared on the Fluid balance tab with the role `observation`, and its report needs only a date and a pressure (or a TDS) column. Each uncertain number's range is the default spread about its declared value (permeability a factor of three either way, thickness and porosity 30 %, total compressibility a factor of two) until a range is declared with its source. Settings: the runs, the pressure error (25 psi), the share error (five points), the wellbore radius, and whether the faults are sampled open and sealing. From a terminal: `gea calibrate --workspace C:\site` prints the kept result; `gea calibrate --workspace C:\site --run` runs it; `gea calibrate --workspace C:\site --range k_md --formation Ellenburger --low 15 --high 200 --source "core report 2024, 12 plugs"` declares a range (`--clear` goes back to the default); `gea calibrate --workspace C:\site --runs 96 --sigma-psi 10 --sample-sealing no` keeps settings; `gea calibrate --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea calibrate` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/calibration.json` (the settings and the declared ranges with their sources) and `records/calibration_result.json` (the last run, with the hash of everything it was computed from) in the workspace; with `--write` or the button, `reports/calibration/calibration.json` and `.md`; the signed dossier carries `results/calibration.json` with whether the result was current at signing; the settings, each range, each run and the report are lines in the audit log.

**The arithmetic**: the runs are a Latin hypercube over the ranges (log-uniform for permeability and compressibility, uniform for thickness and porosity) with a fixed seed, so the same inputs give the same runs; run 0 is the declared case. Each run steps the plume on a 41-cell grid as the Pressure plume tab does, and the front on a 41-cell grid as the Fluid front tab does but with five-day mean rates. A pressure series is compared after its own mean is taken off, measured and predicted alike - the level holds a skin and an initial pressure nobody declared; the shape through time is the formation's. An observation well is compared with the rise in its cell; a flowing well with its cell's rise plus its own draw by Peaceman's well index, q mu ln(0.2 dx / r_w) / (2 pi 0.001127 k h). A measured TDS is compared as the share of injected water it implies, (TDS - TDS_formation) / (TDS_injected - TDS_formation), against the front's share that day. Each dataset's misfit is the root-mean-square of (predicted - measured) / error; a run's misfit is the root-mean-square of its datasets'. A run is KEPT at a misfit of 1.5 or less, or within a quarter of the best run's; over 3 for the best run is NO RUN FITS; with no measurement every run is kept (NO MEASUREMENT). Every range is the tenth to the ninetieth percentile, of all the runs and of the kept runs; "narrowed" is one less the ratio of the two widths (of their logarithms for the numbers sampled on a log scale). A fault's "past the friction line" is the share of the kept runs whose rise on it exceeds its pressure to slip in the worst declared case. What to measure next: across the kept runs (or the eight that fit best when fewer were kept), how far they disagree about a reading in units of its error, and the linear estimate of the narrowing, 1 - sqrt(1 - rho^2 v / (v + sigma^2)), rho the rank correlation between the reading and the forecast, v the readings' variance; a falloff test reads k h to 15 %.

**The number to check**: the best run's misfit (about 1 means it fits as well as the measurements are good; much under 1 means the errors are set too wide, much over means nothing fits) and whether the declared case is kept. On the self-test `gea calibrate` runs, a formation whose true transmissivity is 8,800 md ft against a declared 5,000 is found from a gauge, the injector's pressure and a producer's water: the truth inside the kept runs, the range narrowed by a third or more, the declared case not kept; and a gauge that falls while the well injects reads NO RUN FITS.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a kept run (it is a run the measurements do not contradict, on uniform rock - not the formation); a range's end (a tenth or ninetieth percentile of the runs kept, not a confidence limit); the share of kept runs past the friction line (not a probability of an earthquake); the earthquakes listed beside a fault's rise (a coincidence in the model's terms, never a cause, and not part of the ranking); what was not sampled - a boundary, a second layer, a leak, a well missing from the ledger are outside the ranges, and NO RUN FITS is how they show; the narrowing a new measurement would give (a linear estimate across a few runs, for ranking what to pay for, not a promise).
