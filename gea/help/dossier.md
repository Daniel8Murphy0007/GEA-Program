# The signed dossier

> A well's - or the pad's - record in one file that anyone can check without this program: the reports as they stand, the declarations behind them, the audit log and the source records' hashes, signed with this site's Ed25519 key, with a checker inside.

**The command**: the Reports tab, **Signed dossiers**: **Sign a dossier for the pad**, or one of the wells (the operator role); tick *embed the source records* to carry the drilling records themselves rather than their hashes. **Check** re-checks a dossier this site signed; *Check a dossier someone sent you* checks an uploaded one. From a terminal: `gea dossier --workspace C:\site --well W` (or no `--well` for the pad, `--include-sources` to embed); `gea dossier --verify FILE.zip` anywhere, with `--workspace C:\site` to check it against this site too and `--sources FOLDER` to check the source records; `gea dossier --workspace C:\site --key` prints the key's fingerprint; `--list` the dossiers signed; `--rotate-key --reason "..."` retires the key (the administrator role on the tab), `--key-history` lists the keys. For clients without Python: **Download GEA Reader for your clients** on the same card (or `gea reader --workspace C:\site`) - one HTML file that makes the same checks in any browser and shows the reports (`gea help reader`).

**Changing the key**: retire it when it may have been copied, the machine was lost, or on a schedule - the Reports tab, **Rotate the key**, with the reason (the administrator role). A new key is made; the old one signs a statement naming the new one (and the new one signs it too), and the old seed is then destroyed. Dossiers signed with the old key before that time still check out, here and anywhere; one signed with it afterwards is not the site's, and the workspace check says so. Every later dossier carries the rotations (`keys/rotations.json`, covered by the signature), and the checker verifies each and prints the chain, so a client who was given an earlier fingerprint can follow it to the current one. If the key file is ever missing when a dossier is signed, a new key is made and the rotation is recorded as not signed by the old key: tell your clients the new fingerprint directly. The key history (public keys and statements only) is `keys\dossier_key_history.json`.

**What is in a dossier**: `manifest.json` - the scope, the time, the program and version, and every file with its size and SHA-256 (each report with the history version it is); the audit log's hash chain (each line chained to the one before, across its rotated segments) and length; the previous dossier's id and manifest hash, so a site's dossiers form one chain; the signer's public key. `signature.json` - the Ed25519 signature (RFC 8032) over the manifest's exact bytes. The files. `verify_dossier.py` - a checker in standard Python 3.8+, nothing else to install: `python verify_dossier.py DOSSIER.zip`. `README.txt`.

**What else it carries**: the contract's terms and the invoices and daily reports attached to the invoice check, the rig-time targets and the casing, and - computed when the dossier is signed - each well's invoice cross-check and the rig time (`results/`), so the signed record holds the checks as they read that day.

**Trusted timestamps**: the time in a dossier is this machine's clock. A public time-stamping authority (RFC 3161) can vouch for it instead: it signs, at its own time, the SHA-256 of the dossier's signature, and the token rides in the dossier as `timestamp.tsr` - only that hash and a random nonce leave the machine. Set it on the Reports tab, **Trusted timestamp** (the administrator role): the service's address (DigiCert `http://timestamp.digicert.com`, Sectigo `http://timestamp.sectigo.com`, or the site's own), **Test the service**, and *stamp every dossier as it is signed*; **Stamp** adds a token to a dossier signed before. From a terminal: `gea dossier --workspace C:\site --timestamp-url http://timestamp.digicert.com --timestamp on`, `--timestamp-test`, `--stamp DOSSIER_ID`; `--timestamp-anchors C:\site\keys\tsa_root.pem` adds a root this machine's store does not hold. A dossier is still signed when the service cannot be reached; the Reports tab says it was not stamped, with why, and **Try again** stamps it later. The check reads the token, confirms it covers this signature, verifies the authority's signature (RSA, or ECDSA on P-256, P-384 or P-521) and its signing-certificate attribute, confirms a time-stamping certificate valid at the time, and follows the chain to a root this computer trusts (Python's `ssl` reads the operating system's store); it prints the authority's time beside this machine's and the difference. TRUSTED: the chain reaches a trusted root. VERIFIED: the token is sound but its root is not trusted on this computer - the time is that authority's word only for whoever trusts it. Revocation is not checked.

**What it writes**: the dossier in `reports\dossiers\<well or pad>\`, a line in `reports\dossiers\ledger.jsonl`, and the audit line `dossier.sign`. The key: made on this machine the first time a dossier is signed, kept in `keys\dossier_ed25519.json` in the workspace - outside every report, export and evidence pack - and never sent anywhere. Whoever holds that file can sign as this site: keep it with the workspace and its backup, and nowhere else.

**The check**: VALID when the signature over `manifest.json` verifies against the key it names and every file it lists is in the zip with the size and SHA-256 given, and nothing else in the zip claims to be covered (the checker and the README are not). Inside the workspace, four more: signed with this site's key; in this site's ledger; the source records still match their hashes; the audit log still holds the lines the dossier chained (an earlier line changed or removed breaks the chain). A dossier valid in itself but failing one of those reads VALID, BUT THE WORKSPACE HAS CHANGED.

**The number to check**: the fingerprint the checker prints against the one on the Reports tab - give it to your clients by email or contract, separately from the dossiers; a source record's SHA-256 against the manifest (`certutil -hashfile <file> SHA256`).

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the truth of what was signed - a valid signature shows the files are byte-for-byte those the key's holder signed at the time stated, not that they are correct; who signed - only the fingerprint, compared by another channel, says that; the time - without a trusted timestamp it is this machine's clock; with one, the authority's word that the signature existed by then, not when the reports were written.
