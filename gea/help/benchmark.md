# Benchmark

> The wells of a pad against one another, and each against the best the pad has done. Every well with a drilling record is measured the same way from its own record - how deep, in how many days, at what rate on bottom, how long its connections took, how fast it tripped - and ranked on each count. Then each well is set against the pad's best: the well it would have been had every stretch been drilled at the rate of whichever well was fastest through that stretch, and every connection made in the pad's best quarter.

**The command**: the **Benchmark** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder export attached on the Wells tab (two or more to rank). The tiles name the well with the most feet per day and the fastest on bottom, and the hours the pad spent beyond its own best. **Depth against days** draws every well from its first stand on one chart. **The counts** gives each well's footage, days, feet per day, on-bottom rate, rotary and slide rates, share of the days on bottom, connections and trip speeds, each with its rank. **Against the pad's best** gives each well's on-bottom hours, the hours its footage takes at the best rate the pad made through each stretch, and the gap between them, with the connection time beyond the pad's best quarter; the table under it shows every stretch, each well's rate there and the fastest in bold. From a terminal: `gea benchmark --workspace C:\site` prints it; `gea benchmark --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea benchmark --workspace C:\site --json` gives the page as JSON; `gea benchmark` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: nothing until asked. With `--write` or the button, `reports/benchmark/benchmark.json`, `benchmark.md`, `benchmark.csv` (a row a well) and `stretches.csv` (a row a stretch, each well's footage, hours and rate) in the workspace, and a line `benchmark.report` in the audit log.

**The arithmetic**: the footage and the on-bottom hours are the roadmap's stands of 100 ft (hole a sidetrack abandoned, hole reamed back through, and depth the recorder was given and not drilled all left out). The days run from the first stand drilled to the last, on the recorder's clock; feet per day is the footage over them; the share on bottom is the on-bottom hours over those days. A connection is timed weight to weight as on the Rig time tab; a well needs five to have a median. A trip's speed is its footage over its pipe-handling hours. The ranks put the best first - the most feet per day, the shortest connection - with ties sharing a rank and a count a record cannot give left blank. The stretches are the roadmap's: 1,000 ft of true vertical depth above the heel (the measured depth standing in where a record has no TVD), 1,000 ft along the lateral. In each, a well's rate is its footage there over its on-bottom hours there, and the best is the fastest well's with 300 ft or more in the stretch. A well's time at the pad's best is the sum, over its stretches, of its footage at the better of the stretch's best rate and its own; the gap is its own time less that, never below nothing. The connection gap is each connection's minutes beyond the 25th percentile of every connection on the pad, summed.

**The number to check**: one well's footage and days against its end-of-well report; one stretch's fastest well against the daily reports for that interval. On the self-test `gea benchmark` runs, a fast 200 ft does not set a stretch's best, a well is not charged for being faster than the best over a short stretch, and a stretch with no best is left out of both sides of the gap.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a cause - a well can be fastest through a stretch for its bit, its motor, its mud or its hole size, and none of those is in the record; a like-for-like comparison where the wells differ in diameter, direction or year; the gap as money, or as a promise for the next well - it is hours the pad has already shown can be saved, on some well in that stretch; the days, where the recorder was off for part of them; and a rank among two wells as more than which of two.
