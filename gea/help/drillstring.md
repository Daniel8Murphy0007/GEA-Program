# Drill string

> What was run in the hole and how much each piece has been used: each run's string from the bit up as a build diagram, every component's hours, footage and revolutions from the drilling record summed across runs against its declared service interval, and the downhole motor tracked - its differential against the maker's limits, its speed, its stalls, a torque estimate, and the stability of the slow oscillations the one-second record carries, compared from the start of the run to its end.

**The command**: the Drill String tab. Choose the well; **Declare a run** (the operator role) lists the components from the bit up - the class from the tool library, maker, model, serial, outside diameter and length, and for a motor either one of the catalogued power sections or its own figures with its spec sheet - with the run's span on the drilling recorder's clock (empty is the whole record), and for any component its service interval, what it is counted in (circulating, drilling, rotating or in-hole hours, footage, or a motor's revolutions) and where the interval comes from. The run shows its build diagram, the components' service life across every run, the usage, the motor's charts by the hour, the hours on bottom by differential, the stalls, and the stability: the oscillation index and the distinct oscillation frequency block by block, and the spectrum of the first third of the run against the last. Without a run the whole record is shown without a motor. The Tools tab lists the downhole motors with the maker's figures. From a terminal: `gea drillstring --workspace C:\site` prints it; `--declare run.json`, `--remove RUN`; `gea tools --action motors` lists the motors.

**What it writes**: the runs in `drillstring/runs.json` in the workspace, each declaration, replacement and removal a line in the audit log. The motor catalogue ships with the program (`reference/mud_motor_specs.json`): the maker's handbook, its address and the date it was read, and how the figures were transcribed.

**The number to check**: the revolutions against the gallons pumped times the motor's revolutions per gallon; the circulating hours against the record's pump output; the stall times against the differential channel at those times; a distinct oscillation frequency against the period of the swings in the surface torque on the well's chart (a 0.10 Hz oscillation repeats every ten seconds); a motor's figures against its maker's sheet.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a motor's speed - revolutions per gallon times the flow is its no-load speed, and under load it turns slower; a motor's torque - estimated from the differential by the maker's maximum torque over its maximum differential; the rotor's whirl and turning frequencies - computed from the declared motor and the flow, far above what a one-second record holds; the stability rule - its thresholds (a quarter in frequency, half again in amplitude) are the program's, stated, not a standard; a service interval - it is the operator's declaration with its source; a change seen across the whole record without runs as a change in one motor - it may be a change of hole section or string.
