# The field recorder - the black box

> GEA Recorder is the program on the pad: a Raspberry Pi in a sealed box that records the ground channels (geophones, accelerometers, electrode dipoles) through a 24-bit ADC and the rig's WITS0 feed, every sample on one GPS-disciplined clock, into hourly miniSEED files with a SHA-256 beside each and a manifest, and serves one status page on the pad network. Nothing to type: power it and it records. GEA pulls its files in and makes stations of them.

**The command**: the **Seismic** tab, the card **Field recorder**: the box's address on the pad network (`http://192.168.1.50:8780` - the status page shows the address) or the folder of the USB mirror carried to the office, a station name if the site's is not wanted, then **Pull** (a job). GEA fetches the manifest, downloads every file it does not have, checks each against its SHA-256, and makes or updates two stations: the array of the ground-motion sensors (band 8-80 Hz, the sensors' positions from the box's config) and the electrode station (0.01-5 Hz); each sensor's hours are joined in order into one record, nothing re-sampled or filtered. Pull again later: only the new hours come across, and the stations are updated in place. From a terminal: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action field-pull --box http://192.168.1.50:8780`. On the box: `gea-recorder status`, `gea-recorder selftest`; `sudo sh kiosk.sh` once puts the live view full-screen on a monitor on the box's HDMI port from boot - RECORDING in green or what is wrong in red, the clock, the rig's depth, every channel's last minute and the ground sensors' spectrogram, nothing to press - and the same view is at `/live` on the box's page from any browser on the pad network; `sudo sh install.sh` once, from the release's `GEA-Recorder-0.93.0.tar.gz`; the box's README has the parts list, the wiring and the three chrony lines for the GPS clock.

**Before there is a Pi**: on the same card, **Start the simulated box**: the recorder's own program runs on this computer with its test signal in place of the ADC (a process of its own, its files under `field\sim-box\` in the workspace, its page on port 8781), **Open its monitor** shows the live screen the attendant will see, **Pull from it** brings its files in as stations, **Stop it** closes every file and lists them. **Open the box's monitor** opens any real box's live screen from its address. The kit and the setup install the recorder's program beside GEA for this; a pip install adds it with `pip install gea-recorder`. **From the office, over the internet**: GEA-VPN (`gea help vpn`, Administration) gives every box a private address the office pulls from (`http://10.77.0.3:8780`) through a tunnel from the pad's cellular modem; the box's zip from that card holds `vpn.sh`, run once on the Pi.

**What it needs**: the box's kit on a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with the ADS1256 HAT (Waveshare High-Precision AD/DA), a GPS receiver with a pulse-per-second line on GPIO 18, buried geophones and electrode pairs wired to the ADC's inputs, and the box's config: the site, the wellhead's position, each channel's input, id, kind and place in feet from the wellhead, the ADC driver, the rig feed's port. The rig feed is optional and read-only; the EDR's WITS0 over a serial line or TCP, when the operator gives it.

**What it writes**: on the box, `<YYYY>/<DDD>/GE.<sensor>.<code>.<YYYY>.<DDD>.<HHMM>.mseed` (512-byte INT32 records with blockette 1000 and a blockette 1001 carrying the timing quality: 100 locked to the PPS, 0 the box's own clock), its `.sha256`, `manifest.json`, `sensors.csv`, `rig/depth.csv` and `rig/wits0.<hour>.log`; in the workspace, the same under `field\<box>\` exactly as the box wrote them, `field\<box>\last_pull.json`, the stations under `seismic\` with the audit lines `field.pull`, `seismic.add` and `seismic.update`.

**The number to check**: on the box's screen or page, the clock's timing quality (100 means the GPS pulse holds it under 100 microseconds; anything under 90 is a clock to look at before the data is trusted for bit seismic) and each channel's samples this hour against the rate times the minutes elapsed; in GEA, the pull's count of files that failed their hash (zero) and the station's record count against the hours pulled.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the files. A sample is a count from the ADC; the sensitivity is in the manifest, not applied; what a count means in ground velocity or volts per metre is the sensor's calibration, declared on the Instruments page when the site has it. The ADS1256 driver has run on the bench through the recorder's simulator only; the first box with the HAT verifies the rate it achieves, and the page reports what was recorded, never what was configured.

**Sources**: SEED Reference Manual v2.4 (FDSN), data record and blockettes 1000 and 1001; Texas Instruments ADS1256 datasheet (SBAS288); chrony documentation, refclock PPS; WITS0 (Wellsite Information Transfer Specification, level 0), record 1 item codes.
