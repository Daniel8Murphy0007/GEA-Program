# Fluid front

> Where the injected water itself has gone - a SIMULATION. The pressure plume is miles wide because pressure travels through the formation's compressibility; the water has to push the formation's own water out of the pore space ahead of it, and is hundreds of feet from the well. On a fine grid around each injection well this page moves the injected water with the wells' steady flow and lets it spread under its own weight against the formation water's: the fraction of the thickness it fills, drawn as the tip, the half and the whole; the day it reaches each producer, its share of the producer's stream and the TDS that predicts beside the TDS the producer's own report measures; and an account of every injected barrel - in place, produced back, across a fault, beyond the grid.

**The command**: the Fluid front tab, once the Fluid balance tab has an injection well with a formation, a volume record and a surface position, the formation its permeability, thickness and porosity, and - for the tongue and the TDS - the injected water's density or TDS and the formation water's. Pick a snapshot: the plan view draws the tip (5 % of the thickness, dashed), half the thickness (bold) and the whole thickness, with a piston of the same volume dotted; the section under it draws the interface through the thickness, along the base when the injected water is the denser and along the top when it is the lighter. The producers' table gives each one's arrival, share and predicted TDS, and beside them the last TDS its report measured with the share that implies. Settings: the cells per side and how far to project (five years unless changed). The faults declared sealing on the Pressure plume tab are sealing here. The 3D tab draws the interface at its depths within the declared interval. From a terminal: `gea front --workspace C:\site` prints the snapshots; `gea front --workspace C:\site --cells 101 --projection-days 3650` keeps settings; `gea front --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea front` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/front.json` (the settings) in the workspace; with `--write` or the button, `reports/front/front.json` and `.md`; the signed dossier carries `results/front.json` (every snapshot's figures, the producers and the account, without the contour coordinates); the settings and the report are lines in the audit log.

**The arithmetic**: the grid is square, centred on the injection well, reaching 2.5 x the swept radius at the end of the projection (widened to hold the wells within four of them). This near the well the flow is steady and incompressible to a very good approximation, so each well's flow is solved once for a unit rate: the steady pressure equation on the grid by conjugate gradients, face transmissibility 0.001127 k h / mu, the faces a sealing fault separates closed, the edge held on the well's own logarithmic potential p = -mu / (2 pi 0.001127 k h) ln r; a day's flow is the wells' rates times their unit fields, added. The quantity moved is s, the fraction of the net thickness the injected water fills in a cell of pore volume dx^2 h phi / 5.6146 barrels: carried by the flow across each face at its upwind value (second order with van Leer's limiter, two stages in time, the step under 0.4 of the Courant limit over the cells that can change), fed 1 at an injection well, removed at s by a producer. With a density difference dsg between the injected and the formation water, and equal viscosities, the sharp-interface (vertical-equilibrium) equations add an exchange between neighbours of G s (1 - s) ds barrels a day, G = 0.001127 (k / mu) x 0.433 x |dsg| x h^2: the heavier water runs under the lighter. The gravity number 2 pi G / q compares that with the well's own flow: under 0.1 the front is close to a piston, over 10 a thin tongue. A producer's predicted TDS is s x TDS_injected + (1 - s) x TDS_formation at its cell (the injected TDS volume-weighted over the record); the share a measured TDS implies is (TDS_measured - TDS_formation) / (TDS_injected - TDS_formation). The projections start from the last day: CONTINUED holds every well's last thirty days' mean rate; SHUT IN stops the injection and lets the producers go on. The account: injected = in place on the grid + produced back + beyond the edge, with the closure printed; the water across an open fault is counted on the faces the fault separates and is still in place, on the far side.

**The number to check**: the account's closure (a few barrels in millions); on the self-test, one well's half contour at the piston radius sqrt(V / (pi h phi)) within a cell, the flow across a face ten cells out the radial share, an injector and an equal producer 600 ft apart with the analytic breakthrough pi phi h d^2 / (3 q) between the 1 % and the 20 % days, a denser water's tip farther and its full thickness nearer for the same volume, nothing beyond a sealing fault. On the page: the producer's measured TDS against the predicted - that is the check that matters, and the page says which way it differs.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: any of it - a projection on declared, uniform rock, labelled SIMULATION on every line; the front's sharpness (a real front disperses, and this one is smeared a cell or two by the scheme itself: the 1 % arrival day is early by that much); the tongue's side or reach where the densities are not declared (then the front is a piston, and the page says so); the barrels the material balance holds UNACCOUNTED (they stand beside the account, not in it); a regional flow nobody declared; a producer's share where it does not drain the whole thickness. A neighbour's produced-water analysis is the measurement; the calibration that reconciles the two is the next module.
