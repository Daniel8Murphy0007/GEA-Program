# The earthquake watch and the program's alerts

> While a well operates, the program reads the USGS earthquake catalogue every few minutes from this machine and raises an alert when an earthquake near the well reaches the chosen regulator's protocol level. It also raises alerts for the conditions nobody watches by hand: the watch itself going quiet, a component's service interval used up, a penetrator check outside its rating, and a signed dossier the workspace no longer matches.

**The command**: the Operations & Seismicity tab's **Live earthquake watch** card - on or off, the interval (5 minutes by default), whether it polls only while an operation is under way (the default) or always, and **Poll now** to try the connection. Every alert is listed on the Alarms tab under **Program alerts**. From a terminal: `gea seismicity --workspace C:\site --watch-once` polls now and says what came back; `--watch on|off`, `--watch-every 5`, `--watch-mode always`; `gea alerts --workspace C:\site` lists the alerts, `--check` checks the motors, penetrators and dossiers now.

**How the watch reads the catalogue**: it runs in the service's background poller, so only while `gea serve` (the control panel) is running. An operation is under way when a declared operation (Operations & Seismicity, or the declarations sheet's `[operations]`) has started and has not ended, at a well with a surface position. A poll reads the USGS real-time summary feed of the past day, all magnitudes, updated every minute - the feed USGS asks automated programs to use - and keeps the earthquakes inside the circle around the operating wells (out to the screening distance, 20 km by default, beyond the farthest). On the first poll, or after a gap of a day or more, it asks the FDSN event service for the whole gap instead (from the operation's start, at most 30 days back). USGS revises magnitudes and locations; the newest version wins, and a revision that reaches a higher protocol level is a new alert.

**The alerts**: `seismic.protocol` (a protocol level reached, once per level), `seismic.event` (any earthquake within the association radius during or after an operation, once), `seismic.watch_down` (no answer from the catalogue for three intervals while an operation runs) and `seismic.watch_up`; `motor.due` (checked every 15 minutes), `penetrator.outside` (every 15 minutes), `dossier.changed` (the newest dossier of each well and of the pad, re-checked daily), `invoice.differs` (a well's invoice cross-check finds a day that differs; checked daily and when a file is attached), and `condition.cleared` when one of the last four ends.

**Getting them to a person**: every alert is written to `records/alerts.jsonl` and shown on the Alarms tab. To have it sent, set up notifications (Administration, Notifications: load the example, edit, validate and commit) with an email (SMTP) or webhook channel and a rule naming the alert, for example `{"event": "seismic.protocol", "channels": ["mail"]}`; the password stays in an environment variable named by `password_env`. A text message: most carriers deliver email sent to the phone's email-to-text address, or a webhook to a messaging service. The `quiet_s` window keeps one alert from repeating.

**What it writes**: `seismicity/watch.json` (the settings), `seismicity/live/events.json` (each earthquake's latest version) and `state.json`, the catalogue the tab reads (`seismicity/catalogs/live_usgs.txt`, listed with the window and circle the watch covered), `records/alerts.jsonl`, `records/alerts_state.json`; the audit log records each change of settings and each manual poll.

**The number to check**: an alert's magnitude, time and distance against the earthquake's USGS event page (its id is in the alert); the watch's last good poll on the tab against the clock.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the earthquake - its magnitude and position are the catalogue's, automatic at first and revised later; a cause - an alert says an earthquake reached a level near an operation, not that the operation caused it; the regulator's decision - the protocol's magnitude of record may be another network's. A watch that cannot reach the catalogue says so; it never reports quiet it did not see.
