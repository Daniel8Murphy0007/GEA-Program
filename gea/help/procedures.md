# Procedures

> Every connection and every trip of a drilling record against the rules the operator declares. A drilling program says how a connection is to be made and how a trip is to be started - circulate so long before the slips, ream the stand, stop the pumps first, bring them back up slowly, check for flow before pulling out, do not pull faster than so much. Whether the crews did is in the drilling recorder second by second, and nobody reads it. This page does, for the part of a procedure a recorder can see: each rule is counted connection by connection and trip by trip, by tour, with the ones that did not meet it listed with their time and depth. With no rule declared it still shows what the crews do now, which is where a rule usually starts.

**The command**: the **Procedures** tab, under Drilling. Pick the **Well**; every well of the workspace with a drilling recorder's export is read. **What the crews do now** needs nothing declared: for each measure, the middle value over the record and the range eight in ten fall in. To hold the record to a rule, **The rules** (the operator role): choose what the rule is about, give its figure, say where it came from - the drilling program and its section - and **Declare the rule**. A rule is for every well unless it is given one, and for the whole hole unless it is given a stretch in ft MD. **Take off** removes one. From a terminal: `gea procedures --workspace C:\gea-site` prints the page, `--kinds` lists what a rule can be, `--add conn.circulate_before_s=60 --source "the drilling program, section 4.2"` declares one, `--remove R1` takes one off, and `--write` writes the report.

**What is measured**: at every drilling connection the Rig time tab finds (from the last second drilling to the first second drilling again) - the seconds the pumps ran after the bit came off bottom and before the slips were set; the reaming passes in that time, a pass being the bit run back down 20 ft or more with the pumps and the rotary on; whether the pumps were off at the second the slips were set, and how far off bottom the bit was at the last second they ran; the seconds the pump rate took, the last time the pumps were started before drilling again, from stopped to nine tenths of the rate drilling resumed at; and the weight-to-weight minutes. At every trip of 1,000 ft or more - the pipe's speed, as the speed that 95 in 100 of its moving seconds stayed under, taken over ten seconds; and for a trip out that starts within 500 ft of bottom, between the last second drilling (or the trip before, and no further back than six hours) and the trip: the minutes the pumps ran, and the longest stretch with the pumps off and the pipe still.

**When the pumps are on**: the pump rate above 50 gal/min. A recorder works its pump rate out from the strokes, and some hold the last figure for most of a minute after the strokes stop; read as it stands, that makes every connection look as if it were made with the pumps running. So where the record has the standpipe pressure too, the pumps are off from the second the pressure began a fall that took it under a quarter of its greatest in the two minutes before, however long the rate goes on reading. A reading or two dropped by the recorder (three seconds or less) is not taken as a stop.

**Met, not met, not read**: each connection or trip a rule covers is MET, NOT MET, or NOT READ where the record cannot give the measure - a connection made with the pumps on has no ramp to time, a record with no pump rate cannot say when they stopped. NOT READ is counted and shown, and is in neither the met nor the not met. A tour is the half of the report day the connection or trip began in, from the day start the Invoice check tab holds (06:00 unless set); it is not a crew.

**On a record still being written**: the service checks every fifteen minutes and raises `procedure.departed` for a connection or a trip that began in the record's last two hours and did not meet a declared rule, once for the rule and the connection or trip (`gea help alerts`).

**What it writes**: reading the page writes nothing. **Declare the rule** and **Take off** write `records\procedures.json` and an audit line, `procedures.rule` or `procedures.rule_removed`. **Write the report** writes `reports\procedures\` with a folder for the well: `procedures.json`, `procedures.md` and `departures.csv` (a row for each connection or trip that did not meet a rule, the latest 60 a rule), and the audit line `procedures.report`.

**The number to check**: `gea procedures` with no workspace must end `OK`. It makes a record of twelve stands and a trip whose connections were made a known way - so many seconds circulated, so many passes reamed, the pumps brought up over so many seconds, one connection made with the pumps on - and reads each back: the seconds to three, the passes exactly, the flow check and the circulating before the trip to half a minute, the pipe pulled at 120 ft/min and run at 90 to five. It makes the record again with the pump rate reading on for forty seconds after every stop, and nothing read may change. On the public Utah FORGE records, with no rule declared, the page reads 580 drilling connections and 547 trips over five wells, 149 of the trips out and off bottom: the pumps were off when the slips were set at 95 to 100 in 100 of the connections of the four wells sampled every second, and the weight-to-weight middle value runs from 7.5 to 14.1 minutes a well.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: compliance with a procedure. It is the part of a procedure a drilling recorder can see, and a procedure asks for much it cannot - a survey taken, a pill pumped, the pipe filled, the flow line watched. A flow check here is the pumps off and the pipe still for so long before a trip out; whether anyone watched the well in that time is not in a recorder. A reaming pass shorter than 20 ft, or made without rotation, is not counted. A recorder that smooths its pump rate lengthens every ramp, one that reads it in steps shortens it, and one sampled every ten seconds cannot place a stop or time a ramp closer than that. A tour is a half of the report day, not a crew. And a connection that did not meet a rule may have had a good reason - a drill, a survey, a tight spot: the daily report is where that is written, and this page does not know it.
