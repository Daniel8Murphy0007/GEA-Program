# Frac treatment

> Each fracturing stage from its own pumping record. A stage is recorded second by second at the surface - the treating pressure, the slurry rate, the proppant concentration. From that record alone, with nothing fitted to a model of the fracture, this page reads what was pumped, the pressure the formation held the moment the pumps stopped (the ISIP) with the range it can be read to, the friction the rate added and its two parts where the rate was stepped down, the pressure after the shut-in against Nolte's G, and a step-rate test where there was one.

**The command**: the **Frac treatment** tab. As an operator, under **Attach a stage's treatment record**, pick the well, name the stage (1, 3R, 6A), choose the file and say where it came from. The file is the stage's record as the frac service company gave it: a spreadsheet or CSV whose header is one row or several stacked rows with a row of units, a frac van's data-acquisition export, or a plain CSV with a time, a pressure and a rate. **Stage by stage** then has a row a stage; pick a stage for its charts - the stage (pressure, rate and proppant, a panel each on one clock), the shut-in second by second with the fitted lines, the step-down with its fit, the fall-off against G. Under **The depth of the perforations**, declare their true vertical depth and the gradient of the fluid in the well, with their source, to have the bottomhole ISIP and its gradient. **Give the Frac hits tab this well's stage times** hands that tab each stage's first and last moment of injection, so the two pages read the same stages. From a terminal: `gea frac-treatment --workspace C:\site` prints the table; `gea frac-treatment --workspace C:\site --well "Well 1" --stage 3` gives one stage in full; `gea frac-treatment --workspace C:\site --well "Well 1" --stage 3 --attach C:\data\stage3.csv --source "service company post-job data"` keeps a record; `gea frac-treatment --workspace C:\site --well "Well 1" --tvd 8500 --source "perforation record; fresh water"` declares the depth for every stage of the well; `gea frac-treatment --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea frac-treatment` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: on attaching, the file itself, verbatim, under `completion/frac/treatment/` in the workspace with its SHA-256, the channels read from it beside it, and a line `frac_treatment.attach` in the audit log; a declaration is `frac_treatment.declared`. With `--write` or **Write the report**: `reports/frac_treatment/frac_treatment.json` (everything read from every stage), `frac_treatment.md`, `stages.csv` (a row a stage) and `stage_table_` a well (the stage times, as the Frac hits tab reads them), and a line `frac_treatment.report`.

**The arithmetic**: the stage PUMPED while the rate was above 0.3 bbl/min (or 2 % of its greatest), gaps under half a minute closed; a stretch under a minute is brief and one pumped at under a twentieth of the record's greatest pressure is surface work (priming, a line test) - neither is injection. The slurry volume is the rate summed over the injection; the proppant is the concentration times the clean rate, summed (a concentration above 12 lbm/gal is a densitometer being flushed, and is left out and counted); each is shown beside the record's own totaliser. The ISIP: after the rate reaches zero the pressure rings (the water hammer), so a straight line is fitted to the pressure over 15 to 60 s, 20 to 90 s and 30 to 120 s after the shut-in and each is read back to the shut-in; the ISIP is their median and its range is their spread - wide (more than 5 %) where the pressure was still falling fast. The stage's shut-in is the last one that follows a tenth or more of the stage's volume; every other shut-in with a minute of pressure after it is read too. The friction is the pressure at the end of the last rate held a minute or more, less the ISIP. A step-down is three or more rates held 8 s or more on the way to the shut-in: each one's friction is fitted as a x q-squared + b x root-q, neither negative. The first pressure peak is a peak in the opening of the main injection after which the pressure fell 100 psi and 3 % within two minutes with the rate held. The fall-off runs from the shut-in to the next pumping, or to where the pressure drops 100 psi in five seconds (the well opened): the pressure at 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 minutes; with five minutes or more, G = 4/pi (g(dt/tp) - g(0)), g(x) = 4/3 ((1 + x)^1.5 - x^1.5), tp the injection time; a closure candidate is offered only where G dP/dG runs straight through the origin over a fifth of the record, the pressure then stays above its own straight line in G, and the record runs half as long again in G beyond that point. A step-rate test is four or more rising rates held half a minute each with the pressure rising at every one: two straight lines are fitted, and a break is where the second is under 0.6 of the first's slope.

**The number to check**: one stage's ISIP and its pressures at 5, 10 and 15 minutes against the service company's post-job report - on the public Utah FORGE stages of April 2022, whose reports are published with the data, the company's stage 1 gives a final ISIP of 4,019 psi and 3,715, 3,582 and 3,501 psi after 5, 10 and 15 minutes; this page reads 3,985 psi (3,975 to 3,997) and 3,720, 3,585 and 3,486. Its stage 2 gives 3,918 psi; this page 3,859 (3,852 to 3,862). The page's ISIP is the lower because it is read back along the decline and not taken at the top of the ringing. One stage's slurry volume against its totaliser on the same row. On the self-test `gea frac-treatment` runs, a stage is built with its ISIP, its friction's two parts, its closure and its extension pressure known, and each is read back.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the ISIP as one number where the pressure was still falling fast when the ringing stopped - it is a line read back, and its range is what the windows disagree by; a closure pressure - the G-function pick is where one curve leaves one straight line, to be looked at on the plot and set beside a second method; the perforation friction alone - the part that goes with the square of the rate is the pipe and the perforations together, and a service company that takes the pipe out with its own friction tables will give a smaller figure (for FORGE's 2022 stage 2, 622 psi at the perforations and 638 near the wellbore where this page reads 797 and 1,006 with the pipe in); the bottomhole pressure - the surface pressure plus a declared column of fluid; a breakdown as the rock breaking - friction reducer reaching the perforations, or a ball seating, makes the same peak; a step-rate break in a job's own ramp-up as the extension pressure - there it is as likely the perforations opening; and the fracture - its height, length and width and where the proppant went are not in a pressure and a rate.
