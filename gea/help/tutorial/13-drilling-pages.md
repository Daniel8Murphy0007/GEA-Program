# 13. The drilling pages
Who: office
Where: #/drillstring
Takes: 45 minutes
> Each engineering page reads the drilling record, takes what the operator declares with its source, and writes a client report with one button.

## How every page works
The same shape each time: pick the well; the tiles at the top; the charts; a **declare** form (the operator role) for what the record cannot know - with a **source** box for where the figure came from; **Write the report**. Every page has a worked example under **Scene** (Volve wells from the public record) to look at before declaring your own.

## Steps
1. **Drill string**: **Declare a run** - the components from the bit up (class from the tool library, maker, model, serial, OD, length), the run's span on the recorder's clock, and for a motor a catalogued power section or its own figures. The page shows the build diagram, each component's hours and footage against its service interval, and the motor's differential, stalls and stability from the start of the run to its end. A component past its interval raises a `motor.due` alert.
2. **Rig time**: what the rig did each second, read from the hook load, the block, the bit, the pumps and the rotary - connections weight to weight, trips by speed, time lost against the declared targets or the pad's own best quarter.
3. **Roadmap**: with two or more wells' drilling records attached, the page cuts every well into stands of 100 ft, puts each in its stretch of hole (1,000 ft of TVD down to the heel, 1,000 ft of lateral beyond it) and gives, for each stretch, the weight on bit, rotary speed, flow and differential pressure the fastest quarter of the stands were drilled with, and which well was fastest there. **The check** sets each well against the roadmap made from the others: the half of its stands nearer the window against the farther half. Drill the next well from the table only as far as the check supports it.
4. **Drilling alerts**: three rules replayed over every record - the off-bottom pressure falling at a steady flow (a washout), the differential pressure spiking with the bit stopped (a motor stall), the pick-up weight leaving its own trend (the hole getting tight). Each well's card shows the readings connection by connection, what each rule would have said and when, and whether a trip out of the hole followed. On a record still being written an event raises an alert; **The rules' numbers** at the foot change the thresholds, each with its source.
5. **Kick & loss**: the same records replayed for the mud that comes back. At each pumps-off the pits rise by the rig's own few barrels as the lines drain; the page sets each one against the fingerprint of the eight before it and marks a flowback that runs above it. While circulating, a ten-minute change in the pits that leaves its own trend is named by what the return flow did: up with the pits is kick-like, down with them is losses, flat is mud added, moved or dumped at surface. **The places to look** lists them; **show** draws the pit volume, the pump output and the return flow around one. An event is a place to look, not a kick called.
6. **Benchmark**: the wells against one another, each measured the same way from its own record - feet per day from its first stand to its last, the rate on bottom, the median connection weight to weight, the trip speeds - and ranked on each. **Against the pad's best** sets each well against the well it would have been with every stretch drilled at the rate of whichever well was fastest through it: the gap is hours the pad has already shown can be saved, not a forecast and not a cause.
7. **Public data** (an administrator): a real pad to practise all of this on. The Utah FORGE project publishes the rig recorders of five wells; download them in a browser from the pages the tab links to, name the folder they are in, **Check the folder**, name a folder for the new site and **Make the site**. It opens in its own window with five wells attached, and every page of it ends with the credit its licence asks for. Put the wells' published survey files in the same folder (the tab's **The surveys** card lists them, under a megabyte each) and each well is placed as well: open **3D** in the new site to see the two deviated wells side by side, **Collision** for how far apart they run, and **Simulation** to replay one down its own path.
8. **Torque & drag**: declare the string, the mud and the friction factors; the page draws pick-up, slack-off and rotating hook loads against bit depth with the rig's own points over them, and the friction factors that fit.
9. **Hydraulics & ECD**: declare the string, the hole, the Fann readings, the mud weight, the flow rate and the nozzles; the standpipe pressure part by part, the ECD against the pore pressure and fracture gradient, surge and swab.
10. **Pore pressure**: how the well is read (sonic, resistivity or the d-exponent, the normal compaction trend, the shale cutoff) and its measured pressures (an MDT station, a leak-off test); the mud-weight window against depth.
11. **Casing & kick tolerance**: each string as `what:od:lb_ft:grade:top-shoe:hole:mud` - the page checks burst, collapse and tension by API's formulas and the kick each open hole can take.
12. **Collision**: every well against every other by the ISCWSA separation factor, the travelling cylinder, each well's uncertainty from its survey program and the Earth's field at the well.
13. **Plan vs actual**: under **The plan**, **Attach** the directional plan with its azimuth reference and source; at each survey station, how far the well is from the plan against the survey's own uncertainty, the bit projected, the arc back.
14. **Simulation**: the well drilled again on the screen - **Play** runs the record on the recorder's clock while the mud-log panel grows with the hole.

## You know it worked when
Each page you declared on shows no *declare it* placeholder in its tiles, and its report is under the well's reports with the declarations and their sources on the last page.
