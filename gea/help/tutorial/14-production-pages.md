# 14. Completion, the frac treatment, frac hits, the fluid balance, the plume, the front and their calibration, decline curves, economics and the invoice check
Who: office
Where: #/completion
Takes: 30 minutes
> The pages after drilling: the ESP penetrator, what each frac stage's own pumping record says and what the stage did to the wells around it, where the disposal water went, the decline forecast, the cost against the market, and the contractor's invoice day by day.

## Steps
1. **Completion**: **Record the installation** of the ESP wellhead penetrator - the penetrator (a catalogue entry or a fleet tool with its datasheet), the date, the installer, the motor type, the gauge distance, the insulation readings, the void test - and **Declare the drive** (the drive's output limits, the motor nameplate, the hazardous-area classification, the ambient range). The page checks the well against the penetrator's ratings one line at a time; a line outside raises a `penetrator.outside` alert.
2. **Frac treatment**: under **Attach a stage's treatment record**, pick the well, name the stage and choose the stage's pumping record as the frac service company gave it - the pressure, the rate and the proppant, second by second - with its source. **Stage by stage** gives each stage's volumes beside the record's own totalisers, the ISIP with the range it can be read to, the friction at the last rate held and, where the rate was stepped down, its two parts; pick a stage for its charts. Declare the depth of the perforations for a gradient. **Give the Frac hits tab this well's stage times** saves typing the stage table.
3. **Frac hits**: attach each treatment well's stage table (CSV or Excel, with its source and the UTC offset of its clock) and name each monitored well's pressure channel. The grid shows every stage against every monitored well - no response, poroelastic, mixed, or a frac hit - and a cell picked shows the pressure's departure around the stage.
4. **Fluid balance**: declare the disposal well's role, formation and the density (or TDS) of what it injects, and its neighbours' roles and formations, each with its source; attach each well's injection or production report as a CSV (or declare the disposal well's injection channels and let its own stream be the record); declare the formation's porosity, thickness, area, permeability and initial pressure. The page adds every barrel in and out by day with its tonnes of brine and dissolved solids, fits the storage the pressure says against the storage declared, names the unaccounted barrels, draws the Hall plot, and for each neighbour gives the day the pressure front reaches it and the volume it would take for the water to - both labelled SIMULATION. **Write the report** writes the ledger CSVs.
5. **Pressure plume**: with the ledger's injection well placed and its formation's permeability declared, the page puts the barrels on a grid and steps the pressure through time - pick a snapshot to see the rise as contours, the area of review over the threshold, and each mapped fault's rise and slip tendency; declare a fault layer sealing (with its source) and the plume piles up against it. Every figure is a SIMULATION; the 3D tab draws the same contours at the declared top.
6. **Fluid front**: the same ledger again, for the water itself: declare the injected water's TDS or density and the formation water's on the Fluid balance tab, and the page shows how far the injected water fills the thickness (the tip, the half, the whole, against a piston of the same volume), the tongue it runs along the base or the top, the day it reaches each producer and its share of that producer's stream. Where a producer's report has a TDS column, the measured value stands beside the predicted one - that is the check. A SIMULATION; the 3D tab draws the interface at its depths.
7. **Calibration**: when a neighbour has a gauge (declare it on the Fluid balance tab with the role observation and attach its record), the injector's report has a bottomhole pressure, or a producer's report has a TDS column, **Run the calibration**: the plume and the front are run over a range for every uncertain number and ranked against those measurements. The page shows which runs are kept, each forecast as a range - what the declarations alone allow beside what the measurements leave - and **What to measure next**, ranked by how much each would narrow the area of review and the front. With nothing measured yet it still shows the spread the declarations allow and what to measure first. A SIMULATION.
8. **Decline curves**: attach a well's monthly production and declare the terms (terminal decline, price basis, NRI, severance, opex, discount rate). The page fits Arps's curves, forecasts to the economic limit and values it: the EUR and the PV.
9. **Economics**: declare the day rates with what they pay for and their period, the one-off items (a bit, a cement job) and the AFE. Each well's days, footage and hours on bottom come from its drilling record; **Refresh the EIA prices** fetches the WTI and Henry Hub spot series; **Import a series** keeps any CSV of dates and values with its source.
10. **Invoice check**: declare the contract's terms once (the hour the report day starts, the day rates, the repair allowance), attach the daily drilling reports and the invoice as CSV, and **Write the report**: each day AGREES, is in QUESTION, or DIFFERS, with the dollars each difference puts in question.

## You know it worked when
The Economics table shows cost per foot beside the WTI price while the well was drilled, and the invoice check names the days that differ.
