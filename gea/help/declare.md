# The declarations sheet

> Everything the operator declares about a pad, in one spreadsheet: who each well is and where, its reference elevation, its directional survey, the day rates, the one-off costs and the AFE, the fleet, the drill-string runs, the ESP penetrator and drive, the operation windows and earthquake catalogues for Operations & Seismicity, and the engineering - the survey program and plans, how the pore pressure is read and the pressures measured, the torque-and-drag string, the hydraulics, frac stage tables and monitored wells, production and the decline terms. Every row is checked against the workspace before anything is written, and the same sheet can be imported again and again.

**The command**: on the Setup tab, **Download the sheet** gives this workspace's sheet - every table with its header and a line saying what it takes, already filled with what is declared - and **Check the sheet** reads a sheet - an Excel workbook (its first sheet) or CSV - and plans each row: NEW, CHANGE (it replaces what is declared) or SAME (already declared exactly so). One error and nothing is written; with no error, **Write** (the operator role) writes the NEW and CHANGE rows. From a terminal: `gea declare --workspace C:\site --template pad3.xlsx` writes the sheet as an Excel workbook (`pad3.csv`: as CSV); `gea declare --workspace C:\site --file pad3.xlsx` checks it; add `--apply` to write. A directional survey, a plan, a stage table, a production file or an earthquake catalogue is named by its file beside the sheet, so those are imported from the terminal (or attached on the well page and the Operations & Seismicity tab).

**The layout**: one table after another. A table starts with a row whose first cell is its name in brackets - `[wells]`, `[fleet]`, `[rates]`, `[items]`, `[afe]`, `[surveys]`, `[runs]`, `[components]`, `[penetrators]`, `[esp_drives]`, `[operations]`, `[catalogs]`, `[contract]` (the drilling contract's terms for the invoice check, one row), `[rig_time]` (the rig-time targets, one row), `[block_weights]`, `[casing]` (each well's casing shoes), `[survey_program]` (the survey tools by depth: `0-1500:MWD+SRGM, 1500-:MWD+IFR1`, metres), `[plans]`, `[pore_pressure]` (the indicator, Eaton's exponent, the normally pressured interval, the bit size the d-exponent needs), `[pressures]` (formation tests, kicks, leak-off and formation-integrity tests), `[torque_drag]` (the string as `what:length_ft:od_in:lb_ft:contact_od_in; ...`, the mud and friction factors), `[hydraulics]` (the string as `what:length_ft:od_in:bore_in; ...`, the hole as `what:bottom_md_ft:inside_diameter_in; ...`, the mud weight, the viscometer as `600=60 300=38 100=21 6=7 3=6`, the rate, the nozzles in 32nds), `[casing_design]` (a row per casing string: its size, weight, grade, top and shoe, the hole and mud it was run in; a well's rows together are its program), `[casing_loads]` (the hole to TD, the kick intensity, the design factors, the BHA and drill pipe for the kick volume), `[frac_stages]`, `[frac_monitors]`, `[production]`, `[decline_terms]`, `[settings]` (the earthquake watch and the daily summary: `watch_enabled`, `watch_every_min`, `watch_mode`, `summary_enabled`, `summary_at`, `summary_send`) - and whose other cells name its columns in any order; its rows leave the first cell empty. A row starting with `#` is a comment. Notification channels are not on the sheet - they carry addresses and passwords; set them on the Notifications page. A well is named by its id or its name. A run's components are listed in `[components]` under the run's name, from the bit up; a motor names its catalogue id, or gives its own figures (`motor_rev_per_gal` ... `motor_source`). Fleet ratings are `key=value` pairs separated by `;`, lists with `/`.

**What it writes**: through the same functions the forms and commands use, so each declaration is audited the same way under the importing user, and one `declarations.import` line records the import. It adds and replaces; it never removes: a rate that overlaps one already declared, or a fleet tool already there with another datasheet, is an error to resolve where it lives first.

**The number to check**: a template downloaded and imported unchanged plans nothing but SAME; after a write, the Setup checklist shows the wells' position, elevation and number declared, the Economics tab the rates and AFE, the Drill String tab the runs, the Completion tab the penetrators, and the Plan vs actual, Pore pressure, Torque & drag, Hydraulics & ECD, Frac hits and Decline curves tabs their numbers.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: anything on the sheet - every row is the operator's declaration with its source, and the program checks only that it parses, is complete and does not contradict what is declared.
