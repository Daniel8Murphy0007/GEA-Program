# Alert rules

> A card for each rule that raises an alert from a drilling record. An alert is worth what the person who gets it knows of the rule behind it: what it looks at, where its line is drawn, what else looks the same, and what it cannot see. The page says first what these rules are - a driller's rules with thresholds set by hand, not models fitted to data - and then gives each one a card, made from the program's own modules at the moment the page is opened, so a card cannot describe a rule other than the one that runs.

**The command**: the **Alert rules** tab, under Watch & report. Nothing is declared here: the thresholds are changed where they always were, on the Drilling alerts and Kick & loss tabs, each with its source. **Run its checks** on a card runs that rule's own self-test now; **Run every rule's checks** runs them all, about ten seconds. **Write the report** (the operator role) runs every check and keeps the cards as files. From a terminal: `gea alert-rules --workspace C:\gea-site` prints the cards with the workspace's settings and wells, `--checks` runs every rule's checks first and prints them, `--write` writes the report, and `--cards` prints the rules as the program ships them, with no workspace.

**What a card says**: *what it is for*, in a sentence, and the alert it raises. *How it decides*: the rule as the module that applies it states it - the passage is taken from that module's own description, with its numbers. *When the alert is raised*: the module's own statement, and that the service checks every fifteen minutes. *Cannot do without* and *reads when it is there*: the channels the rule looks for, by name, from the Channels tab's list; and what must be declared before it can run - the drill string for the friction factor, a boundary for the hard line, the stresses for the mud-weight window, a rule for a procedure. *Settings*: each threshold as it stands in this workspace, its default, the bounds it may be set between, what it means, and - where it has been changed - the source given for the change. *In this workspace*: on how many of the wells with a drilling record the rule finds what it cannot do without, which it does not and what they lack, and how many times it has been raised here. *Its checks*: the self-test's count and each check in words. *What it will not call a measurement*. And the file that holds the rule, with the first sixteen characters of its SHA-256, which an inspector can match to the delivered source.

**The rules**: off-bottom pressure falling (a washout), the motor stall, the pick-up weight off its trend, stick-slip in the surface torque, the hook load jumping, the friction factor rising (the Drilling alerts tab); a flowback above its fingerprint and the pits leaving their trend while circulating (Kick & loss); a trip whose mud departs from the pipe moved (Trip sheet); the bit near a hard line (Hard line); the mud against the mud-weight window (Wellbore stability); a declared procedure not met (Procedures). Twelve cards for eighteen alerts. **The other alerts** - an earthquake at a protocol level, a service interval used up, an invoice that differs, a certificate expiring and the rest - are conditions the service watches, not rules read from a drilling record; they are listed under the cards with their tabs, so that every alert the service can raise is on this page.

**What it writes**: reading the page writes nothing and runs no check. **Run its checks** writes nothing: the result is kept in memory until the program is restarted or the module's file changes. **Write the report** writes `reports\alert_rules\alert_rules.json` and `alert_rules.md`, and the audit line `alert_rules.report`.

**The number to check**: `gea alert-rules` with no workspace must end `OK`. It holds the cards to the modules they describe: every alert the service knows is under a card or among the others, and none under two; each card's *how it decides* is found in its module's description now; the washout card's passage says 6 % and 100 psi and the settings beside it are 0.06 and 100; what each rule reads is the Channels tab's own list for it; and a rule's checks are its module's self-test. With `--checks`, every rule's count must be all of its checks.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: how often a rule is right. None of these rules has been set against field events that someone confirmed - a washout found at the trip, a kick confirmed by a flow check - so there is no hit rate and no false-alarm rate to print, and the page prints none. The checks say a rule finds the fault put into a made record and stays quiet on a record without it. The count of alerts raised here says how often the rule spoke, not how often it should have. And a rule's silence is not a clean bill: no alert means the rule did not fire on this record.
