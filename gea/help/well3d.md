# The well in 3D

> Each well's path from its directional survey, set among the mapped formations - interpreted horizon surfaces, or a formation-top map's contours at their elevations - with its picks on it, coloured by what was measured along it, and the drill string replayed from the record. The Volve field in the Norwegian North Sea ships with the program as the worked example: three wells' original surveys, the field's formation picks and five mapped surfaces, released by Equinor.

**The command**: the 3D tab. Choose the scene - this workspace's wells, or Volve. Drag to turn the view, shift-drag (or the right button) to pan, the wheel or a pinch to zoom; on the keyboard, the arrows turn, + and − zoom and Home resets. Colour the wells by formation (the mapped surface above each depth, from the well's own picks), by inclination or dogleg severity from the survey, or - for a well with a drilling-recorder export - by its channels (gamma, rate of penetration, weight on bit) along the bit depth. Tick the surfaces and layers on and off, see through them, show every pick, and stretch the depth by 1 to 5 times. Point at a well or a pick for its measured depth, its depth below sea level, its offset from the slot, the inclination there and the formation it is in. Under Replay, pick a well and press Play or drag the slider: the bit moves down the hole, the hole drilled so far in full colour and the rest faded. A workspace well needs three things to be drawn: its surface position and its reference elevation (Setup) and its directional survey - a WITSML trajectory or a table of measured depth, inclination and azimuth - attached on this tab or with `gea trajectory --workspace C:\site --well Well-30636033 --file survey.csv --azimuth-reference grid --grid TX_CENTRAL --source "RRC imaged records, directional survey"`. Until a workspace well can be drawn, its formation-top contour layers are drawn on their own over the area the Map opens on, the depth stretched 20 times (any of 1 to 50 can be chosen). `gea well3d --scene volve` prints the Volve scene and its checks; `--scene workspace --workspace C:\site` prints this workspace's.

**What it writes**: the survey you attach, byte for byte, in the well's folder (`wells/<well>/trajectory/`), with how to read it in the well's record - the azimuth reference (grid with the grid named, true, or magnetic with the declination), the depth unit, the depth reference, where it came from and its SHA-256 - and an audit line. The view itself writes nothing. The Volve files are in the program's reference folder with their provenance (`reference/volve_3d.provenance.json`): the public copies they were taken from, each at a pinned commit, with the hashes as fetched; `tools/build_volve_3d.py` rebuilds them.

**The number to check**: a path against the survey company's own positions - for Volve the minimum-curvature path computed here reproduces the TVD and displacements written in each trajectory file to under a centimetre, and lands on Equinor's formation picks (which carry their own eastings, northings and depths) to under 0.3 m; a surface against the picks - the table under the view gives, for each surface, how far it sits from every pick of its formation (median a few metres, more at wells the interpretation did not honour); the frame - the Volve slot eastings and northings, unprojected on ED50 zone 31N, land within 0.1 m of the latitudes and longitudes in the operator's own log headers.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a path between survey stations as surveyed - it follows the minimum-curvature arc, the industry's assumption, not a measurement; a well with no survey - it is not drawn, and its picks, where they exist, are shown as points; the hole above a survey table's first station - taken as vertical, the tie-in the table implies, and said so; a surface between its grid nodes, or a contour map between its lines - the surface is drawn as delivered (here every fourth inline and crossline, no point moved) and a contour is drawn only where it was mapped; a fault trace, which has no depth, or an isopach, which is a thickness and not a position; any rock or fluid property between wells - nothing here measures one; the Volve replay as second by second - its times are the survey stations' own time stamps while drilling, so it shows the hole growing station by station (a resurvey run in one go, like 15/9-F-4's, carries one time and is not replayed); a drilling recorder's clock as UTC - its times are shown as the recorder wrote them.
