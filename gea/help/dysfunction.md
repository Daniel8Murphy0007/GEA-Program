# Drilling alerts

> Three things a drilling record shows before the crew has to deal with them, each an old driller's rule written down so it is applied to every stand of every well the same way: the off-bottom pressure falling at a steady flow (a washout), the differential pressure spiking with the bit stopped (a motor stall), and the pick-up weight leaving its own trend connection after connection (the hole getting tight). Each rule is replayed over the whole of each well's record, so the page shows what it would have said and when - beside what the rig did next - and on a record still being written an event raises an alert.

**The command**: the **Drilling alerts** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder export attached on the Wells tab. The tiles count the events of each kind over every record. Each well's card has three parts. **Off-bottom pressure**: a dot at each connection - the standpipe pressure with the bit a foot or more off bottom and the pumps still at the drilling rate, before the slips are set - with the median of the five readings before it as a line, flagged readings in red, and a table of the events: when, how deep, how far the pressure fell, and whether a trip out of the hole followed or the pressure came back. **Motor stalls**: a stick at the depth of each, its height the rise in differential pressure; the table gives the time, the rise, how long, rotary or slide, and whether a trip out followed within a day. **Pick-up and slack-off weight**: the median hook load moving up before the slips and down after them at each connection, rotating and not rotating in their own colours, flagged connections in red, and the events with how each ended. **The rules' numbers** at the foot change the six thresholds, each with its source. From a terminal: `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site` prints the replay; `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site --washout-fall 0.08 --source "drilling engineer, 2026-11-12: this rig's gauge wanders 5 %"` keeps a setting; `gea dysfunction` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/dysfunction.json` in the workspace when a setting is changed (the numbers and each one's source; audit line `dysfunction.settings`); with `--write` or the button, `reports/dysfunction/drilling_alerts.json`, `drilling_alerts.md` and `events.csv` (a row an event: the well, the time, the kind, the depth, what it was and how it ended) and the audit line `dysfunction.report`. An alert - `drilling.washout`, `drilling.stall` or `drilling.tight` - goes to `records/alerts.jsonl`, the Alarms tab and the notification rules, once per event.

**The rules**: WASHOUT - the reading at a connection is compared with the median of the five comparable readings before it in the same bit run, each brought to the same flow by pressure ~ flow^1.8, and only readings within 10 % of that flow are compared at all, so a change of pump rate is not a fall. A reading 6 % and 100 psi or more below is flagged; two flagged readings running open an event; the readings before it stay the baseline until it ends, at the trip out of the hole, at two readings back within 3 %, or at the end of the record. Where fewer than half the connections have ten seconds off bottom at the drilling rate (a crew that stops the pumps before picking up), the recorder's own zero is read instead: standpipe less differential pressure while drilling, which is the off-bottom pressure the recorder was last zeroed at. STALL - on bottom with the pumps on, the differential pressure against its own median over the five minutes before (ten-second blocks, the last half minute left out, and only once the bit has been on bottom for 200 s of those five minutes); a rise of 300 psi and 60 % of that median or more, reached within half a minute, over in a minute and a half, under which the hole made a quarter or less of the footage it had been making. A rise the bit drilled on under is weight or harder rock and is counted apart; so is one that was already half way there half a minute before. It needs a record every five seconds or faster. TIGHT HOLE - each connection's pick-up and slack-off weight against a Theil-Sen line through the previous fifteen of its own kind in the same bit run, against depth; the scatter is 1.48 times the median absolute residual, 2 klbf at least; 3 scatters and 10 klbf or more off the line is flagged; two flagged in three connections open an event, which ends when three running are back on the line, at a trip out, after twelve connections (a new level, not a trend) or at the end of the record. ALERTS - checked every 15 minutes: only a record whose file changed in the last two hours and whose own last sample is within two days of now is looked at (a file from years ago copied in today is not a rig drilling today), and in it an event still open at the end, or one that began in the last half hour.

**The number to check**: one event against the rig's own record of that hour - the pressure, the pump strokes and the tour sheet's remark; and the count of events against what the well file says happened. A rule that fires every few stands on a record the well file calls uneventful has its number set too tight for that rig: change it, with its source. On the self-test `gea dysfunction` runs, a labelled record has a washout that takes a sixth of the pressure over five stands and is found at the trip, three stalls of 700 psi, a hole tightening by 6 klbf a connection to the end of the record, and five stands drilled with the pumps slowed by a tenth: each is found where it was put, the slowed pumps flag nothing, and a sound record gives no event of any kind.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a washout - the pressure fell at a steady flow, which is also a pump losing a valve, a lighter mud, a lost nozzle or a gauge, and the trip finds which; a stall - a pressure spike the bit stopped under: what it did to the motor is in the motor's own report; stuck pipe - a pick-up weight above its own trend, which is cuttings, a ledge, a dogleg after a slide, a change of mud or of how the crew picks up, and the Torque & drag tab holds the model of what the weight should be; a rule's silence - no event means the rule did not fire on this record, not that the string was sound; a kick, a loss or a twist-off - none of the three rules looks for them.
