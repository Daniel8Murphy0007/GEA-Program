# Drilling alerts

> Six things a drilling record shows before the crew has to deal with them, each a driller's rule written down so it is applied to every stand of every well the same way: the off-bottom pressure falling at a steady flow (a washout), the differential pressure spiking with the bit stopped (a motor stall), the pick-up weight leaving its own trend connection after connection (the hole getting tight), the surface torque winding up and letting go with one period (stick-slip), the hook load jumping from second to second (the bit bouncing), and the friction factor that fits each connection's weights climbing (the hole holding the pipe). Each rule is replayed over the whole of each well's record, so the page shows what it would have said and when - beside what the rig did next - and on a record still being written an event raises an alert.

**The command**: the **Drilling alerts** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder export attached on the Wells tab. The tiles count the events of each kind over every record. Each well's card has six parts. **Off-bottom pressure**: a dot at each connection - the standpipe pressure with the bit a foot or more off bottom and the pumps still at the drilling rate, before the slips are set - with the median of the five readings before it as a line, flagged readings in red, and a table of the events: when, how deep, how far the pressure fell, and whether a trip out of the hole followed or the pressure came back. **Motor stalls**: a stick at the depth of each, its height the rise in differential pressure; the table gives the time, the rise, how long, rotary or slide, and whether a trip out followed within a day. **Pick-up and slack-off weight**: the median hook load moving up before the slips and down after them at each connection, rotating and not rotating in their own colours, flagged connections in red, and the events with how each ended. **Surface torque: stick-slip**: the largest swing of each 100 ft of hole as a share of the torque, red where a two-minute block was flagged, and the events - when, how deep, for how long, how large, every how many seconds, at what rotary speed, and whether a trip out of the hole came within a day; beside the heading, the minutes set aside (the pipe rocked, a swing at the rotary's own speed, a swing with no period). **Hook load: bit bounce**: the largest jump of each 100 ft about the hook load's own level, and the events. **Friction factor at each connection**: the open-hole friction factor that makes the string declared on the Torque & drag tab weigh what the hook read as it was picked up and as it was slacked off, a dot a connection, the declared factor as a line, and the events - from what factor to what; a well with no string declared says so and links to that tab. **The rules' numbers** at the foot change the ten thresholds, each with its source. From a terminal: `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site` prints the replay; `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea dysfunction --workspace C:\site --washout-fall 0.08 --source "drilling engineer, 2026-11-12: this rig's gauge wanders 5 %"` keeps a setting; `gea dysfunction` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/dysfunction.json` in the workspace when a setting is changed (the numbers and each one's source; audit line `dysfunction.settings`); with `--write` or the button, `reports/dysfunction/drilling_alerts.json`, `drilling_alerts.md` and `events.csv` (a row an event: the well, the time, the kind, the depth, what it was and how it ended) and the audit line `dysfunction.report`. An alert - `drilling.washout`, `drilling.stall`, `drilling.tight`, `drilling.stickslip`, `drilling.bounce` or `drilling.sticking` - goes to `records/alerts.jsonl`, the Alarms tab and the notification rules, once per event.

**The rules**: WASHOUT - the reading at a connection is compared with the median of the five comparable readings before it in the same bit run, each brought to the same flow by pressure ~ flow^1.8, and only readings within 10 % of that flow are compared at all, so a change of pump rate is not a fall. A reading 6 % and 100 psi or more below is flagged; two flagged readings running open an event; the readings before it stay the baseline until it ends, at the trip out of the hole, at two readings back within 3 %, or at the end of the record. Where fewer than half the connections have ten seconds off bottom at the drilling rate (a crew that stops the pumps before picking up), the recorder's own zero is read instead: standpipe less differential pressure while drilling, which is the off-bottom pressure the recorder was last zeroed at. STALL - on bottom with the pumps on, the differential pressure against its own median over the five minutes before (ten-second blocks, the last half minute left out, and only once the bit has been on bottom for 200 s of those five minutes); a rise of 300 psi and 60 % of that median or more, reached within half a minute, over in a minute and a half, under which the hole made a quarter or less of the footage it had been making. A rise the bit drilled on under is weight or harder rock and is counted apart; so is one that was already half way there half a minute before. It needs a record every five seconds or faster. TIGHT HOLE - each connection's pick-up and slack-off weight against a Theil-Sen line through the previous fifteen of its own kind in the same bit run, against depth; the scatter is 1.48 times the median absolute residual, 2 klbf at least; 3 scatters and 10 klbf or more off the line is flagged; two flagged in three connections open an event, which ends when three running are back on the line, at a trip out, after twelve connections (a new level, not a trend) or at the end of the record. STICK-SLIP - the record is read in blocks of two minutes of rotary drilling on bottom; in each the surface torque (a channel named Convertible Torque or Rotary Torque) has its straight-line trend taken out and is transformed, and the swing is the amplitude of what lies at periods of 3 to 15 seconds - root two times its RMS - as a fraction of the block's median torque (the Drill string tab's oscillation index). A block is flagged at a swing of 10 % or more that one period carries: the strongest period, with its second harmonic where the band holds it, has 40 % or more of everything the torque did about its trend. Set aside and counted, not flagged: a swing as large with no period; a block whose torque touches zero every cycle (the pipe rocked left and right while sliding); and a period that is the rotary's own speed or two or three times it, folded onto a slower one by the once-a-second sampling (a bent joint, a three-cone bit). BIT BOUNCE - in the same blocks the hook load has its nine-second running mean taken out; the bounce is half the spread between the 10th and 90th percentile of what is left, flagged at 5 klbf and a quarter of the weight on bit. For both, two flagged blocks running open an event, which ends when three running are quiet, when the bit has been off bottom for half an hour, or at the end of the record; both need a record every two seconds or faster. FRICTION FACTOR - the soft-string model of the Torque & drag tab, with the string, mud, cased-hole factor, shoe and block declared there and the well's survey (or the recorder's own path), is asked at each connection what open-hole factor gives the pick-up weight with the string not rotating, and the slack-off weight; where the hole is too straight for friction to move the weight by 10 klbf no factor is read, and where a quarter of the weights are ones no factor from 0 to 0.8 gives, the page says the declarations are not the rig's and judges nothing. The median factor of the last five connections against the median of the twenty before them: a rise of 0.05 and three of their connection-to-connection scatters is flagged, and an event runs until three running are not - so a hole that settles at a new level stops being flagged, and the chart shows where it stands. ALERTS - checked every 15 minutes: only a record whose file changed in the last two hours and whose own last sample is within two days of now is looked at (a file from years ago copied in today is not a rig drilling today), and in it an event still open at the end (stick-slip or bounce under way, a friction factor still rising), or one that began in the last half hour.

**The number to check**: one event against the rig's own record of that hour - the pressure, the pump strokes and the tour sheet's remark; and the count of events against what the well file says happened. A rule that fires every few stands on a record the well file calls uneventful has its number set too tight for that rig: change it, with its source. On the self-test `gea dysfunction` runs, a labelled record has a washout that takes a sixth of the pressure over five stands and is found at the trip, three stalls of 700 psi, a hole tightening by 6 klbf a connection to the end of the record, and five stands drilled with the pumps slowed by a tenth: each is found where it was put, the slowed pumps flag nothing, and a sound record gives no event of any kind. The same record given a surface torque has a saw-tooth of 4 on 10 every seven seconds through two stands (one stick-slip event, its period seven seconds), a stand with the pipe rocked (set aside) and a stand with the hook load jumping 8 klbf (one bounce event); and a made hole whose friction factor is 0.24 for forty connections and then climbs 0.008 a connection is read back at 0.23 to 0.24 and flagged within ten connections of the climb - while the tight-hole rule, which follows a slow climb, says nothing. On the public Utah FORGE records, sampled once a second, the torque of 16A(78)-32 is flagged for about ten of its 258 hours of rotary drilling in 29 events, the period lengthening with depth from four seconds near 4,800 ft to eight near 10,960 ft, as a longer string's must.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a washout - the pressure fell at a steady flow, which is also a pump losing a valve, a lighter mud, a lost nozzle or a gauge, and the trip finds which; a stall - a pressure spike the bit stopped under: what it did to the motor is in the motor's own report; stuck pipe - a pick-up weight above its own trend, which is cuttings, a ledge, a dogleg after a slide, a change of mud or of how the crew picks up, and the Torque & drag tab holds the model of what the weight should be; stick-slip - a swing in the SURFACE torque: what the bit's speed did is measured only by a tool at the bit, a top drive that damps the torque hides the swing while the bit still sticks, and sampled once a second a swing faster than three seconds a cycle (most stick-slip above about 4,000 ft) is blurred, so what is read is a floor; bit bounce - the hook load at the deadline, through the whole string and the mud, so a deep bit can bounce with nothing showing; sideways vibration and whirl - they do not reach the surface sensors and no rule here speaks of them; stuck pipe or its forecast - the friction factor that fits the weights has risen, which is cuttings left in the hole, a wall cake, a ledge, the mud, or a string no longer the one declared, and the factor itself is fitted, not measured; a rule's silence - no event means the rule did not fire on this record, not that the string was sound; a kick, a loss or a twist-off - none of the six rules looks for them.
