# The drilling recorder

> A rig's electronic drilling recorder exports one row per second: the date and the time in two columns, then the WITS record-1 channels with their units in the header - hole depth, bit depth, rate of penetration, weight on bit, rotary speed, standpipe pressure, hook load, pump output, block height, torque, the on-bottom flag, gamma, strokes, trip tank, the recorder's interpolated TVD. A value the recorder did not have is -999.25 or an empty cell; the file stops when the rig leaves the well and starts again when it is back. The `edr` port reads that layout as delivered (the historian port takes one timestamp column and says so when it meets this one; nothing is converted by hand), streams a three-million-row export into memory, and writes the DRILLING RECORD: per section and for the well, the recorder's own arithmetic.

**The command**: `gea edr --file C:\site\_incoming\30636033.csv` prints the record; `--out C:\site\_incoming\30636033_record` writes it as json, md and html with the MD-TVD table as `md_tvd.csv`; `--selftest` proves the port on a synthetic export. In a site: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action add-file --file C:\site\_incoming\30636033.csv --name "Well 30636033"` copies the file in verbatim and `--action refresh` writes the record under `reports\wells\Well-30636033\`; the well page shows it. `gea files --workspace C:\site --action list` detects the layout by its first line (kind `edr`). `gea ingest --file X.csv --port edr` is the same reader at the command line.

**What it writes**: `drilling_record.json` / `.md` / `.html`: the rows, the span on the clock and the hours actually recorded, the sections (a gap longer than a minute starts one) each with its start, end, hours, the gap before it, hole depth from and to, feet drilled, hours on bottom (the recorder's flag) and rotating (on bottom with rotary speed above zero), the mean and median rate of penetration while on bottom, the connections (the flag falling and rising), and the section's peak weight on bit, rotary speed, standpipe pressure, torque, hook load and pump output; for the well, total depth, when it was reached, the recorder's TVD there, feet drilled per on-bottom hour; per channel the coverage and the count of sentinels, blanks and non-numeric cells; `md_tvd.csv` - hole depth against the recorder's interpolated TVD, one row per 100 ft, labelled an interpolation; the lateral - where the recorder's TVD comes within 1% of its maximum, and the hole beyond it; `gamma_log.las` - the recorder's gamma while on bottom as a log by bit depth, one-foot bins, the median and the sample count per bin, indexed to bit depth and said to be shallow by the sensor's distance behind the bit, with the lithology-from-GR index this program applies to any gamma log (picks from the log's own 5th and 95th percentiles, SAND/SHALE flags per bin) in the record; and the observations - values below zero where the quantity cannot be, rate-of-penetration spikes, the differential pressure's negative stretches - counted and named, nothing altered.

**The depth events**: before anything is computed, the hole depth channel is read for what it can be trusted for, and every place it lies is named in the record. A STALE OPENING is a section that opens on a depth the recorder carried over from the last well and holds it, nothing drilled, until it is left by a jump - excluded. A RE-REFERENCE is a depth set back and then counted up again by running pipe, faster than any bit drills or with the recorder's own rate of penetration at zero, within the same section - the window is excluded. A SIDETRACK is a set-back of 500 ft or more followed by six hours time-drilled under 20 ft/h - a new borehole from that depth: total depth is the final borehole's, the footage drilled counts every borehole, and the gamma log and the TVD table are kept off the abandoned hole. A CORRECTION is a smaller fall (the overstated footage is taken back) or a depth set forward off bottom (the understated footage is added), so that without a sidetrack the footage drilled is exactly total depth less the first depth on bottom. A fall that is none of these is UNRESOLVED, handled as a correction and said to be so. Separately, a stretch of 30 minutes or more with the on-bottom flag set, the depth still and the rate of penetration zero is the flag left on with nothing drilled: counted in the flag's hours, kept out of the drilling hours. Each event is a shape read from the file; the daily drilling report decides what happened.

**The number to check**: the record's total depth against the completion report; the recorder's TVD at total depth against the directional survey's; the on-bottom hours against the daily drilling reports; `--selftest` must print three sections of 600 ft/h, a 750 ft lateral past 2,000 ft, 13 table rows and an 835-bin gamma log whose lithology index steps at 2,300 ft; the depth events against the daily drilling reports, one by one; `gea survey --file gamma_log.las` must say the log is in feet and converted, recognize the gamma, and say NOT ESTIMATED without a density channel; the sentinel count on Gamma is the recorder's own, not a fault of this program.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the TVD - it is the recorder's interpolation from the surveys it was given, and the table says so on every row; a formation top, a landing point or a kick-off depth - the record reads depths and a flag, not geology or a survey; a landing point is the directional survey's to give, and a SIDETRACK is the shape of the depth channel until the daily drilling report confirms it; drilling performance - feet per hour is arithmetic on the file, not a judgement; a channel's reading where the recorder wrote -999.25 or nothing - it is missing and counted, never filled; a formation from the gamma log - the lithology index is a gamma index on a log indexed to bit depth, the sensor offset unstated; a value the observations count - a block height below zero or a 5,000 ft/h rate is carried as written and named, not corrected; a well's identity - the file name is the recorder's job number until the operator declares the well (set-well).
