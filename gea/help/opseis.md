# Operations and seismicity

> Each well's operations - drilling, completion, disposal, injection - set against the earthquake catalogue around it: what happened near the well while it worked and in the months after, what each event would reach under a regulator's protocol, and whether the earthquake rate near the well differs while it works from the rest of the record. The Mahoning Valley, Ohio, ships as a worked example from the public record.

**The command**: the Operations & Seismicity tab. Choose the scene - your wells, or the worked example - and the protocol (British Columbia's Kiskatinaw order, Alberta's Subsurface Order No. 2, Oklahoma's completion notice, Ohio's 2014 permit conditions, Texas's response plan), the association radius (5 km by default), the days after an operation that still count (90) and the completeness magnitude. Each well shows its operations as bands on a time line, every event within the screening distance (20 km) as a circle by its distance from the well and sized by magnitude, the events table with the rig's state at an event during a drilling record, the protocol level each reaches, and the rate comparison. The operator declares operation windows (or the declarations sheet's `[operations]`) and attaches catalogue exports with the window, minimum magnitude and circle they were queried for (or `[catalogs]`); the program cannot reach a catalogue service itself, so the tab gives the ComCat query to open in a browser. From a terminal: `gea seismicity --workspace C:\site`; `--scene mahoning` for the example; `--protocols`; `--query`; `--attach FILE --start --end --min-magnitude --lat --lon --catalog-radius-km --source`; `--declare-op --well W --kind completion --start --end --source`; `--set` keeps the settings.

**The live watch**: while an operation is under way the program can read the USGS catalogue itself every few minutes and raise an alert when an earthquake reaches the protocol's level - see the help page on the earthquake watch and the alerts.

**What it writes**: `seismicity/operations.json`, `seismicity/catalogs.json` with each catalogue file kept verbatim beside it (its SHA-256 recorded), and `seismicity/settings.json` in the workspace; each declaration, attachment, removal and change of settings a line in the audit log. The protocols ship with the program (`reference/seismicity_protocols.json`), each with its document; the worked example's catalogue (`fixtures/comcat_mahoning_1990-2026.txt`) and its wells and dates (`reference/mahoning_example.json`), each with its source.

**The arithmetic**: an event's distance is the great-circle distance from the well's declared surface position (converted to WGS84). It is DURING an operation inside its window, AFTER one within the association days that follow, and OUTSIDE otherwise; an event near two wells' operations is named on both, each with the other beside it. The rate comparison counts the events at or above the completeness within the radius over the catalogue's window: n inside the operation windows (with their association days) and m outside. If one constant rate held, n would be binomial in n + m with the share of time inside; the probability of n or more is the exact conditional test for two Poisson rates. HIGHER DURING OPERATIONS means that probability is under 1 % and the rate inside is the higher; otherwise NOT DISTINGUISHED. With no event outside, the background is given as its 95 % upper bound, 3 events over its time.

**The number to check**: a distance against a map; for the worked example, Northstar 1's eight events within 5 km during its injection year and the 90 days after against none in the rest of 1990 to 2026; Poland-CLL2's four events during its stimulation and one the next day; the Poland events' ML 2.44 in ComCat against the M 3.0 the regulator and the published study give - a protocol is read against its own magnitude of record.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a cause - a coincidence in time and place is what the record shows; the catalogue's completeness - declared (the query's minimum magnitude by default), not measured, and a sparse network misses small events, so a quiet background is a floor; independence - the test counts every event, so an aftershock sequence weighs as many; the distance to the lateral - it is from the surface position; a protocol's verdict - the level an event reaches by the catalogue's magnitude, while the regulator's catalogue and the permit's own conditions govern.
