# The rock inventory

> The 17 rocks, minerals and fluid the strata survey ranks a measured log against - each one's published bulk density and compressional velocity, as a range, from the cited tables - on a page of its own: the table, the ranges drawn so their overlaps show, a lookup for a measured density (and velocity), and the inventory checked against real cores and logs it was not fitted to.

**The command**: the **Rocks** tab. **Look up a measurement**: a bulk density in g/cc (0.5 to 4.5), and a compressional velocity in m/s when there is one (1,000 to 9,000); the entries whose published ranges hold it are listed nearest first and marked on the chart. From a terminal: `gea rocks` prints the table, the checks and the sources; `gea rocks --density 2.9 --vp 6900` looks up a measurement; `--json` for either. The strata survey that uses the inventory on a whole log is the **Survey** tab (`gea survey --catalog ktb_hb_complog_6020_excerpt`, or a LAS file of the site's own).

**What it writes**: nothing - the page and the command read the inventory shipped with the program. Each entry: its kind (rock, mineral, fluid), the published bulk density and its range (Telford, Geldart & Sheriff 1990, Applied Geophysics; Schoen 2015, Physical Properties of Rocks), and the compressional-velocity range with its midpoint (Christensen & Mooney 1995, Journal of Geophysical Research 100; Schoen 2015). The checks: the Volve 15/9-19 A core plugs - bulk density per plug from the core analysis's grain density and porosity, with brine at 1.03 g/cc, against the published sandstone range (it agrees when nine plugs in ten or more fall inside); the KTB main hole's density log - the rocks the inventory ranks first over 19 stations against the published paragneiss-amphibolite section; the same log with velocity - whether the felsic and mafic families of the published banding both appear.

**The number to check**: 17 entries (9 rocks, 7 minerals, 1 fluid), all 17 with a velocity range; 65 of 70 Volve plugs inside 2.05-2.55 g/cc; gneiss ranked first on the KTB log with basalt second - `gea rocks` prints all three, and the rock inventory model card in the report samples carries the same evaluation.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a rock's name. Rocks are ranges and the ranges overlap - a density of 2.65 g/cc sits inside limestone, granite, gneiss and quartz - so a lookup gives a short list, nearest first, and says how many entries hold the value; it never picks one. Amphibolite and basalt overlap in density and are told apart only by velocity, and laboratory velocities read high against fractured rock in a hole, so some stations of a real log fit no entry, and the page says so rather than stretching a range. The entries are the published tables' values: none is adjusted to a well, a core or this site.

Related: `gea help catalog`, `gea help data-in`.
