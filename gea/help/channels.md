# Channels

> What a well's drilling record carries, how good each channel is, and who reads it. Every tab that reads a drilling record reads it through a few channels - the hook load, the pump rate, the pit volume - and says little, or says "not read", when one is missing or is not what its name says. This page looks at the record itself, channel by channel: where it came from, how many of its samples carry a reading, whether it reads one value throughout, the readings dropped and the spikes, how far behind the record's end it stops, and which tabs and alert rules look for it. It is the page to open first when a tab says it cannot read a well.

**The command**: the **Channels** tab, under Wells & data. Pick the **Well**; every well with a drilling recorder's export attached can be picked, and only the one shown is read. Nothing is declared here. **Write the report** (the operator role) keeps the page as files. From a terminal: `gea channels --workspace C:\gea-site` prints the first well's page, `--well "Well A"` another's, and `--write` writes the report; with no workspace the self-test runs.

**Channel by channel**: each channel as the file names it, with its unit. *Where it reads* is the record from its first sample to its last in 48 stretches, darker where more of the samples carry a reading - a channel that came on late or died early shows at a glance. *Readings* is the share of samples with a value; *no value* counts the recorder's -999.25 and empty cells. *Middle* and *19 in 20 between* are the median and the range from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile: a hook load in the thousands or a depth in metres stands out there. A channel that reads **one value** throughout is wired and dead - a trip tank at 0.0 for a whole well. *Dropped* counts readings gone for three seconds or less between two that read; *spikes* counts single readings far from both neighbours (by more than half the channel's 19-in-20 range) while the neighbours agree. *Last reading* is how far before the record's end the channel stops.

**The record as a whole**: four checks of what the channels say of each other. The clock: the cadence, the breaks longer than a minute, and samples where time stands still or goes backwards. The bit against the hole: samples where the bit reads more than a foot deeper than the hole. The hole depth stepping back: a depth re-set, a sidetrack or a spike (the other pages read a hole depth with those taken out). And the pump rate after the pumps stop: a recorder that works its pump rate out from the strokes goes on reading its last figure after the strokes stop, while the standpipe pressure falls at once; the page counts the stops at which that happened and how long the rate went on reading.

**Who reads it**: every tab and every alert rule that reads a drilling record, with the channels each looks for, by the names it knows - the lists are the tabs' own. A reader that says itself it cannot do without a channel, and does not find it, is marked **cannot read it**, with what is missing; one that finds the channel reading one value throughout has **nothing to read**. *Reads it* means the names were found, and no more: whether they hold enough for the tab to judge is the tab's own to say. A channel no tab looks for by its name is listed as that - it may be the same quantity under a name the tabs do not know.

**A record still being written**: the page says how long ago the file was last written, by this computer's clock, and each channel's last reading against the record's end. That is the file's delay. The delay between a sensor and the recorder is not in a file.

**What it writes**: reading the page writes nothing. **Write the report** writes `reports\channels\` with a folder for the well: `channels.json`, `channels.md` and `channels.csv` (a row a channel), and the audit line `channels.report`.

**The number to check**: `gea channels` with no workspace must end `OK`. It makes channels with known faults - two readings dropped for a second and one for two, three spikes, a channel at 39.7 throughout, one that stops 600 s early, a pump rate reading on for 40 s after each of four stops, the bit twelve feet below the hole for 200 samples, a hole depth stepping back 40 ft, a two-hour break - and each must be found and sized exactly; and lists of channel names with and without a pit volume, a trip tank, a torque and a differential pressure, against which the tabs that need them must say so.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a calibration. A channel that reads steadily and plausibly can still be wrong by a constant - a hook load not zeroed, a depth off by a joint - and nothing in a record says so. A spike is a reading unlike both its neighbours; a pressure pulse can be exactly that. When the bit reads below the hole the two depths do not add up, and the page does not say which moved. And the delay it gives is the file's, not the sensor's.
