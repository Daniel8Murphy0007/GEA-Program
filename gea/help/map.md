# The site map

> Everything in the workspace that has a position, in one frame: the wells where their surface positions were declared, the structure layers the site brought (faults and folds, formation contours, areas, points), the World Stress Map's SHmax directions, the seismic stations, the Seismicity Response Areas, and the outline of the US states and counties underneath. A well without a declared position is listed as not placed and is never drawn anywhere. Click the map for what every layer says about that spot.

**The command**: the Map tab. Drag to pan, the wheel or + − to zoom, Home to return to where the map opened (the placed wells, else the response area, else the area most of the layers cover). Tick a layer to draw it, "fit" to zoom to it; Rings draws distance circles around every placed well. Point at the map for the position (WGS84) and the nearest placed well; click it - or press Enter for the centre - for each layer's own statement there: the nearest fault and its distance and bearing, the contours it lies between, the area it falls in, the nearest points, and the distance and bearing to every placed well. The keyboard does the same once the map has focus: arrows pan, + and − zoom, Home returns. A well's marker opens its page; "declare it" beside a not-placed well opens the setup checklist.

**What it writes**: nothing. The map reads the workspace: each well's declared surface position, each layer's shapefile (read once per run of the program and again when it changes), the stress map, the stations and the response areas. The outline is shipped with the program (`reference/us_outline.json`, from the US Census Bureau's 2017 cartographic boundary files by way of the us-atlas package, with its provenance beside it), so the map needs no network.

**The number to check**: a placed well against its survey plat - its marker is drawn at the declared latitude and longitude moved from the declared datum to WGS84, and the move is printed beside it (about fifty metres for NAD27 in West Texas); a layer's line against the map it came from, at the scale that map was made.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a well's position - it is the operator's declaration, drawn where it was declared; a line drawn at this zoom as the line in the file - a vertex that would fall in the same screen cell as the one before it is left out of the drawing (never moved), and the full line is what "what is here" measures against; the outline as a survey - it is the Census Bureau's cartographic generalisation, good to a few hundred metres; a distance to a mapped fault as a hazard - it is a fact of the map.
