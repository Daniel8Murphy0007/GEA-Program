# Seismic - the second leg

> Raw seismic records in (miniSEED, SAC, or fetched from an FDSN archive), spectra and persistent lines out, and one measured number before anything else is promised: the distance at which a known rig shows in this station's record.

**The command**: `gea seismic --action info --file day.mseed` reads a file (the format is decided by its content); `--action spectrum` and `--action lines --win 600` give the Welch PSD and the lines that persist above the local floor in the band (`--band 1 50` by default, where pumps, rotary and engines sit); `--action fetch --base texnet --network TX --station XXXX --channel HHZ --start ... --end ... --out tx.mseed` downloads from TexNet or EarthScope (`--base iris`); `--action detect --file tx.mseed --station-lat LAT --station-lon LON --sources rigs.csv` is the detectability test, with the rigs' positions and working windows in the CSV (`source_id, lat, lon, start_utc, end_utc[, kind, note]`). `--action selftest` runs the test on a synthetic scene and labels it `SIMULATION_SELF_TEST`.

**What it writes**: `info` prints each trace with its start, end, rate, sample count, encoding and gaps - in counts, with the instrument response left as recorded; `spectrum --out` a CSV of frequency, PSD and dB; `lines --out` a JSON of the persistent lines (frequency, excess over the floor, the fraction of windows present); `fetch` the miniSEED plus a `.request.json` beside it with the URL and time; `detect --out` a JSON with a verdict per source (`DETECTED`, `NOT_DETECTED`, `AMBIGUOUS` when the rig never worked alone, `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOWS`), the quiet baseline, the lines that belong to each detected source and not to the quiet hours, and the radius bracketed between the farthest rig heard and the nearest not heard.

**The number to check**: the radius line of the detect report. "Between 20 km and 45 km" is the station's measured reach for rigs like those on the list; "not a single number here" means a near rig was missed while a far one was heard and the table, not a radius, is the result; `NO_QUIET_BASELINE` means the record never had an hour with every listed rig down, so nothing could be compared and the test did not run.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a well's position or track - one station detects, it does not locate, and locating is the array step that comes after this number exists; a radius beyond the farthest rig on the ground-truth list; anything about a rig that is not on the list or never worked alone; a line's source - a persistent line is consistent with machinery and does not name the machine; and any quantity in physical units until a station's response file has been applied and named. The scene behind `selftest` assumes body-wave spreading and a Q for its own tones; that is the scene's assumption for checking the arithmetic, not a property of any ground.

Related: `gea help data-in`, `gea help files`.
