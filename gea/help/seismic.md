# Seismic - the second leg

> Raw seismic records in (miniSEED, SAC, or fetched from an FDSN archive), spectra and persistent lines out, one measured number before anything else is promised - the distance at which a known rig shows in this station's record - and, with an array, the direction it came from.

**The command**: `gea seismic --action info --file day.mseed` reads a file (the format is decided by its content); `--action spectrum` and `--action lines --win 600` give the Welch PSD and the lines that persist above the local floor in the band (`--band 1 50` by default, where pumps, rotary and engines sit); `--action fetch --base texnet --network TX --station XXXX --channel HHZ --start ... --end ... --out tx.mseed` downloads from TexNet or EarthScope (`--base iris`); `--action detect --file tx.mseed --station-lat LAT --station-lon LON --sources rigs.csv` is the detectability test, with the rigs' positions and working windows in the CSV (`source_id, lat, lon, start_utc, end_utc[, kind, note]`). `--action selftest` runs the test on a synthetic scene and labels it `SIMULATION_SELF_TEST`. The station's response: `--action response --stationxml station.xml` lists what the file holds; `--action remove-response --file tx.mseed --stationxml station.xml --output VEL --pre-filt 0.5 1 40 45 --out tx_vel.mseed` turns counts into m/s (or `DISP` m, `ACC` m/s^2); `spectrum`, `lines` and `detect` take `--stationxml` and do it first; `fetch --with-response` fetches the StationXML beside the record. The array: `--action beam --files s1.mseed s2.mseed ... --sensors sensors.csv --band 1 20 --start ... --end ...` gives the back-azimuth and slowness the band's energy crossed the array with (`sensor_id, lat, lon` in the CSV, one record per sensor, three or more); `--action array-detect --files ... --sensors sensors.csv --sources rigs.csv` is the array's detectability test - did the array point at each listed rig in the windows it worked alone; `--action locate --bearings bearings.csv` crosses the bearings of two or more arrays (`lat, lon, back_azimuth_deg, sigma_deg`); `--action array-selftest` runs all of it on a labelled synthetic scene. The time dimension: `--action bearings --files ... --sensors sensors.csv --win 600 --name A1 --out a1.json` is the beam on every window of an array's record - a bearing with the array's tolerance where the beam was coherent, a gap where it was not, and the change points; `--action track --histories a1.json a2.json [--truth lateral.csv] --out track.json` crosses two or more arrays' histories window by window into positions with their ellipses, fits the longest continuous segment into a heading and a length, and judges it against the ground truth (`point_id, lat, lon, utc` - the lateral's surveyed points, or a permit's surface hole with its date); `--action track-selftest` runs the tracker on a labelled scene of a bit advancing along a lateral past two arrays. Several rigs at once: `--action signatures --file rec.mseed --sources rigs.csv --out sigs.json` learns each rig's own lines from the windows it worked alone; `--action multi-beam --files ... --sensors sensors.csv --signatures sigs.json` gives a bearing per rig in one window, each from that rig's own bins; `--action multi-bearings ... --out a1.json` does it window by window, and `--action multi-track --histories a1.json a2.json [--truth truth.csv]` crosses two or more arrays into a track per rig. `--action signature-selftest` and `--action field-selftest` run both on labelled scenes. Ground truth from a permit export: `gea permits --file export.csv --out rigs.csv [--within LAT LON KM] [--mapping map.json]` finds the columns by name and writes the rigs CSV with an import note beside it; `--action sar-film --hours 8 --step 600 --out film.json` writes the SAR panel's film - the same scene played forward in time, one frame per window, every frame stamped `SIMULATION_SELF_TEST`. On a site: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action add-seismic --name "Pad 3 node" --files tx.mseed --lat LAT --lon LON --sources rigs.csv [--stationxml station.xml] [--sensors sensors.csv --files s1.mseed s2.mseed ...]` registers a station or an array (the Seismic page does the same by upload; `--permits export.csv` in place of `--sources` converts a permit export on the way in), and `--action refresh-seismic` runs the whole leg on it; `--action add-track --name "Pad 7 lateral" --stations <array-1> <array-2> [--truth lateral.csv]` makes a track over two or more array stations and `--action refresh-track` runs the tracker on it - the Tracks card on the Seismic page is the same by upload, and the track view shows the map with every position's ellipse, a slider through time, and the bearings over time - the Seismic page's Refresh button is that command as a job. The page's SAR panel is the control panel and the screen: `--action sar-film` as a job makes the film, and the screen plays it - the trace, the spectrogram column arriving, the beam power map swinging onto the working rig, the bearing against the rig's true bearing with the array's tolerance wedge beside it, the tally per rig, and at the end the array detectability test's verdict on the whole scene, the same function a real record goes through.

**What it writes**: `signatures --out` a JSON of every source's lines with the windows they were learned from and the reason any source has none; `multi-beam --out` a bearing per source with the bins it used, the lines another source also claims, and the whole-band bearing beside them; `multi-track --out` a position history and a track per source. `info` prints each trace with its start, end, rate, sample count, encoding and gaps - in counts, with the instrument response left as recorded; `spectrum --out` a CSV of frequency, PSD and dB; `lines --out` a JSON of the persistent lines (frequency, excess over the floor, the fraction of windows present); `fetch` the miniSEED plus a `.request.json` beside it with the URL and time (and `.stationxml` with `--with-response`); `remove-response` a FLOAT64 miniSEED in the chosen unit with a `.units.json` sidecar carrying the water level, the pre-filter and the check of the stages against the declared sensitivity; `beam --out` a JSON with the back-azimuth, the slowness and apparent velocity, the best-bin coherence and the coherent frequencies, the array response function's half-width, whether the geometry has aliasing lobes at this band, the other peaks, and the plane-wave limit; `array-detect` a verdict per rig (`POINTED`, `NOT_POINTED`, `INCOHERENT`, `AMBIGUOUS`, `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOWS`) with the beam's bearing against the true one and the tolerance it was judged by; `locate` the crossing point with its 1-sigma ellipse, the crossing angle, the residual of each bearing and whether the point is in front of every array; on a site, `refresh-seismic` writes the Seismic Station Report (html, md, json) and the spectrum, lines, detect, beam and array JSONs under `reports/seismic/<station>/`, which the Seismic page shows; `sar-film` writes `reports/seismic/SIMULATION/sar_film.json` (the frames) and its summary, never under a real station's folder; `refresh-track` writes the Seismic Track Report (html, md, json), every array's bearing history, `positions.json` and `verdict.json` under `reports/seismic/tracks/<track>/`; `permits` writes the rigs CSV and `<out>.import.json` - the columns used, the rows dropped and why, the end dates assumed; `detect --out` a JSON with a verdict per source (`DETECTED`, `NOT_DETECTED`, `AMBIGUOUS` when the rig never worked alone, `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOWS`), the quiet baseline, the lines that belong to each detected source and not to the quiet hours, and the radius bracketed between the farthest rig heard and the nearest not heard.

**The number to check**: with several rigs working, the bins each bearing used and the rival-peak ratio beside it - a bearing taken from two or three bins, or with another peak on the grid within a tenth of its height, is not a direction; the radius line of the detect report; for a track the verdict line - TRACKED means at least 80 % of the positions inside the truth's time span fell within their own 1-sigma ellipse of where the truth was, with three or more compared, and the heading difference printed beside it; for an array the tolerance beside each POINTED verdict - it is the array's own resolution at that band and slowness, and a bearing is never finer than it. "Between 20 km and 45 km" is the station's measured reach for rigs like those on the list; "not a single number here" means a near rig was missed while a far one was heard and the table, not a radius, is the result; `NO_QUIET_BASELINE` means the record never had an hour with every listed rig down, so nothing could be compared and the test did not run.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a well's position or track from one station (it detects, it does not locate) or from one array (it gives a direction and an apparent velocity, never a distance) - a position needs two or more arrays' bearings crossing at an angle, and comes with the ellipse the crossing earns; a position in a window the tracker lists as a gap (fewer than two coherent arrays, a crossing under 15 degrees, a point behind an array), or finer than its ellipse; a heading from fewer than three positions; positions on either side of a jump larger than their ellipses joined into one track (they are segments, listed apart); a track verdict without ground truth; a rig's working window from a permit date (a permission, not a drilling log - the import note counts every end date it assumed); a bearing for a rig that never worked alone (it has no signature, and nothing in the record is known to be its); a bearing for either of two rigs whose lines fall on each other (NOT_SEPARABLE - the bins they share separate nothing); a bearing whose own lines alias on the array's geometry (ALIASED - the peaks it cannot tell apart are listed instead); a signature as proof of a machine (another rig with the same pump at the same rate wears the same one); motion smaller than the ellipses the positions carry (the track says NOT_RESOLVED, because a line fitted through any scatter has a heading); a bearing to a source nearer than about five apertures, where the wavefront is not plane; a direction finer than the array response function allows; a medium velocity (the apparent velocity across the array is measured; the velocity a lag location needs is an input, named with the result); a radius beyond the farthest rig on the ground-truth list; anything about a rig that is not on the list or never worked alone; a line's source - a persistent line is consistent with machinery and does not name the machine; and any quantity in physical units until a station's response file has been applied and named - and with it applied, nothing outside the pre-filter band, nothing from a channel the file does not cover, and nothing from a station file whose stages disagree with their own declared sensitivity (that file is refused, not patched). The response is built from the file's stages the way evalresp builds it; the acceptance suite holds it to evalresp's numbers on a real IRIS channel. The scene behind `selftest` assumes body-wave spreading and a Q for its own tones; that is the scene's assumption for checking the arithmetic, not a property of any ground. The SAR panel's screen plays only that labelled scene: every frame says `SIMULATION_SELF_TEST`, the rigs are drawn where the scene put them, and nothing on it is a measurement; a real record is refreshed on its station and the page shows what the record earned - a bearing, a crossing, or nothing - never a film.
