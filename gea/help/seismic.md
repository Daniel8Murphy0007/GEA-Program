# Seismic - the second leg

> Raw seismic records in (miniSEED, SAC, or fetched from an FDSN archive), spectra and persistent lines out, one measured number before anything else is promised - the distance at which a known rig shows in this station's record - and, with an array, the direction it came from.

**The command**: `gea seismic --action info --file day.mseed` reads a file (the format is decided by its content); `--action spectrum` and `--action lines --win 600` give the Welch PSD and the lines that persist above the local floor in the band (`--band 1 50` by default, where pumps, rotary and engines sit); `--action fetch --base texnet --network TX --station XXXX --channel HHZ --start ... --end ... --out tx.mseed` downloads from TexNet or EarthScope (`--base iris`); `--action detect --file tx.mseed --station-lat LAT --station-lon LON --sources rigs.csv` is the detectability test, with the rigs' positions and working windows in the CSV (`source_id, lat, lon, start_utc, end_utc[, kind, note]`). `--action selftest` runs the test on a synthetic scene and labels it `SIMULATION_SELF_TEST`. The station's response: `--action response --stationxml station.xml` lists what the file holds; `--action remove-response --file tx.mseed --stationxml station.xml --output VEL --pre-filt 0.5 1 40 45 --out tx_vel.mseed` turns counts into m/s (or `DISP` m, `ACC` m/s^2); `spectrum`, `lines` and `detect` take `--stationxml` and do it first; `fetch --with-response` fetches the StationXML beside the record. The array: `--action beam --files s1.mseed s2.mseed ... --sensors sensors.csv --band 1 20 --start ... --end ...` gives the back-azimuth and slowness the band's energy crossed the array with (`sensor_id, lat, lon` in the CSV, one record per sensor, three or more); `--action array-detect --files ... --sensors sensors.csv --sources rigs.csv` is the array's detectability test - did the array point at each listed rig in the windows it worked alone; `--action locate --bearings bearings.csv` crosses the bearings of two or more arrays (`lat, lon, back_azimuth_deg, sigma_deg`); `--action array-selftest` runs all of it on a labelled synthetic scene. On a site: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action add-seismic --name "Pad 3 node" --files tx.mseed --lat LAT --lon LON --sources rigs.csv [--stationxml station.xml] [--sensors sensors.csv --files s1.mseed s2.mseed ...]` registers a station or an array (the Seismic page does the same by upload), and `--action refresh-seismic` runs the whole leg on it - the Seismic page's Refresh button is that command as a job.

**What it writes**: `info` prints each trace with its start, end, rate, sample count, encoding and gaps - in counts, with the instrument response left as recorded; `spectrum --out` a CSV of frequency, PSD and dB; `lines --out` a JSON of the persistent lines (frequency, excess over the floor, the fraction of windows present); `fetch` the miniSEED plus a `.request.json` beside it with the URL and time (and `.stationxml` with `--with-response`); `remove-response` a FLOAT64 miniSEED in the chosen unit with a `.units.json` sidecar carrying the water level, the pre-filter and the check of the stages against the declared sensitivity; `beam --out` a JSON with the back-azimuth, the slowness and apparent velocity, the best-bin coherence and the coherent frequencies, the array response function's half-width, whether the geometry has aliasing lobes at this band, the other peaks, and the plane-wave limit; `array-detect` a verdict per rig (`POINTED`, `NOT_POINTED`, `INCOHERENT`, `AMBIGUOUS`, `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOWS`) with the beam's bearing against the true one and the tolerance it was judged by; `locate` the crossing point with its 1-sigma ellipse, the crossing angle, the residual of each bearing and whether the point is in front of every array; on a site, `refresh-seismic` writes the Seismic Station Report (html, md, json) and the spectrum, lines, detect, beam and array JSONs under `reports/seismic/<station>/`, which the Seismic page shows; `detect --out` a JSON with a verdict per source (`DETECTED`, `NOT_DETECTED`, `AMBIGUOUS` when the rig never worked alone, `INSUFFICIENT_WINDOWS`), the quiet baseline, the lines that belong to each detected source and not to the quiet hours, and the radius bracketed between the farthest rig heard and the nearest not heard.

**The number to check**: the radius line of the detect report, and for an array the tolerance beside each POINTED verdict - it is the array's own resolution at that band and slowness, and a bearing is never finer than it. "Between 20 km and 45 km" is the station's measured reach for rigs like those on the list; "not a single number here" means a near rig was missed while a far one was heard and the table, not a radius, is the result; `NO_QUIET_BASELINE` means the record never had an hour with every listed rig down, so nothing could be compared and the test did not run.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a well's position or track from one station (it detects, it does not locate) or from one array (it gives a direction and an apparent velocity, never a distance) - a position needs two or more arrays' bearings crossing at an angle, and comes with the ellipse the crossing earns; a bearing to a source nearer than about five apertures, where the wavefront is not plane; a direction finer than the array response function allows; a medium velocity (the apparent velocity across the array is measured; the velocity a lag location needs is an input, named with the result); a radius beyond the farthest rig on the ground-truth list; anything about a rig that is not on the list or never worked alone; a line's source - a persistent line is consistent with machinery and does not name the machine; and any quantity in physical units until a station's response file has been applied and named - and with it applied, nothing outside the pre-filter band, nothing from a channel the file does not cover, and nothing from a station file whose stages disagree with their own declared sensitivity (that file is refused, not patched). The response is built from the file's stages the way evalresp builds it; the acceptance suite holds it to evalresp's numbers on a real IRIS channel. The scene behind `selftest` assumes body-wave spreading and a Q for its own tones; that is the scene's assumption for checking the arithmetic, not a property of any ground.
