# The client well report

> One printable document per well, and one for the pad, assembled from what the workspace already holds: the drilling record and its depth events with who checked them, a chart of depth against days, the declared location and what the mapped structure says there, the well path from its directional survey in plan and section with the mapped tops, and the declared cost against the market while it was drilled. Each number says where it came from, and the report lists what it cannot say yet and what would supply it.

**The command**: on a well's page, **Write the well report** (the operator role); on the Reports page, **Write the pad report** or one button per well. From a terminal: `gea well-report --workspace C:\site` writes every well's report and the pad report; `--well Well-30636033` writes one; `--pad` the pad alone. The month-end packet writes and includes them for every well with a drilling record.

**What it writes**: `reports/wells/<well>/well_report.html`, `.md` and `.json`, with its drawings beside it as SVG (`well_report_progress.svg`, `well_report_plan.svg`, `well_report_section.svg`, one per price series) and inline in the HTML, which prints with no drawing split across two pages; `reports/pad_report.*` with `pad_report_progress.svg`. Each run is a line in the audit log with the report's ID and what it still lacks. The rendered text passes the same vocabulary check as every client report before it is written.

**The number to check**: the summary against the drilling record it came from (total depth, feet drilled, days, hours on bottom - the same numbers, read as delivered); the well-path table against the survey stations (minimum curvature, the dogleg in degrees per 30 m); a mapped top's depth below the reference against the two contours the location lies between, subtracted from the declared reference elevation; the cost against the Economics tab, which is where it is declared.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a declared number - the surface location, the reference elevation, the US Well Number, a day rate, an item, the AFE - is printed as declared, with its source, and never as measured; a mapped top at the well - a contour map is read as the two contours the surface location lies between, and a well that deviates meets each top somewhere else; a well path drawn without a survey - with no survey attached, the section says so; a missing part as zero - a well with no surface location, no survey or no declared cost has the part left out and named under "What this report will not say", with what would supply it; a price as the cause of a cost.
