# A well from its records

> A well known from paper - the regulator's well file, the completion report, the inclination survey, an old gas test - entered by clicking, each value kept with the document it came from, and checked against itself: the survey's depths and displacement, the gas test's deliverability curve, its rates and its shut-in pressure.

**The command**: the **Wells** tab, **Add a well from its records**: the well's name and, when there is one, the document (a PDF or a scan, kept verbatim and hashed as the well's source). Then the well's page, one form at a time, each with a **Source** box for the document and the date it was filed: **Who and where** (the API number, the operator of record, the position, which puts the well on the Map and on the Operations & seismicity tab); **The completion** (depths, the producing interval, the formation, the nearest lease line); **Casing** (a link to the Casing & kick tolerance tab, where each string goes in as for any well); **Inclination survey** (the stations, one per line, `measured depth, inclination`, with fractions as the form writes them: `1289, 1 1/4`); **Gas well tests** (the shut-in pressure and its depth, the shut-in tubing pressure, the gas gravity, the temperatures, the open flow the document states, and the points, one per line: `rate, flowing bottom-hole pressure`, optionally then the wellhead pressure, the choke and the form's rate figures). **Fill the forms with the worked example** puts a public well file's survey and test in the forms to try it. **Write the records review** writes the report. A well that is not the site's belongs in a workspace of its own: Administration, **Workspaces**, **Make it and open it** (see install).

**How it is read**: the survey: each course's displacement is its length times the sine of its average inclination, accumulated down the hole (as Texas Form W-12 computes it); the true vertical depth by minimum curvature on one azimuth; the accumulated displacement agrees with the form's when it is within 0.5 ft or 0.5%, the difference being the form's rounded sine tables; at the top of the producing interval it is the farthest the hole can be from vertical, set against the nearest lease line. The gas test: Rawlins and Schellhardt's back-pressure equation q = C (Pr^2 - Pwf^2)^n, fitted by least squares in log-log; a stabilized well's n lies between 0.5 and 1.0, and where the fit's does not, the open flow is bounded through the highest-rate point with n = 1.0 and n = 0.5 instead (Pwf = 14.65 psia); each rate recomputed from the form's own figures (coefficient x pressure x Ftf x Fg x Fpv); a choke pressure above the wellhead's flagged; the shut-in tubing pressure carried down a real-gas column (z by Dranchuk and Abou-Kassem on Sutton's pseudocritical properties, as the casing design does) and set against the stated shut-in pressure; the pressure gradient against fresh water's 0.433 psi/ft.

**What it writes**: the well in `wells\<well>\` (kind `records`; the document in `source\`), what the forms hold in `wells\<well>\well_records.json`, each with who entered it, when, and its source (audit lines `well.add`, `well.records.completion`, `well.records.survey`, `well.records.gas_test`), and the report `reports\wells\<well>\records_review.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON; audit line `report.records_review`). The signed dossier carries the document, the declarations and the review.

**The number to check**: the accumulated displacement against the form's own; the gas test's exponent n against 0.5 to 1.0; the open flow the document states against the bounds the points allow; each recomputed rate against the stated one; the shut-in pressure the tubing pressure gives at the gauge depth against the one stated.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: everything on it - each value is what a document says, entered by a person, with the document named; the checks say whether the documents agree with themselves and with the textbook, not what the well did. A survey with no direction gives the farthest the hole can be from vertical, not where it is.

**Sources**: Rawlins and Schellhardt, Back-Pressure Data on Natural-Gas Wells and Their Application to Production Practices, US Bureau of Mines Monograph 7, 1936; Railroad Commission of Texas, Forms G-1 (gas well back pressure test) and W-12 (inclination report), and 16 TAC 3.11 (inclination surveys); Lee and Wattenbarger, Gas Reservoir Engineering, SPE Textbook Series 5, 1996, ch. 7 (deliverability testing); Sutton, SPE 14265, 1985; Dranchuk and Abou-Kassem, JCPT 1975.
