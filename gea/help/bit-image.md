# The image - drill-bit seismic from the ground

> While a well is drilled the program knows where the bit is - the survey and the depth against time put it within feet every second - so the rig is a seismic source of known position moving along the well. Stations on the ground around the pad, each correlated against the one beside the substructure and nothing on the rig, give the differences in arrival time that resolve the rock's velocity layer by layer and place the first layer below the bit. This page runs that end to end on a labelled scene and says what it recovered against the scene's own figures.

**The command**: the **Seismic** tab, the card **The image**: the number of ground stations, the array's outer ring, the ground noise and a seed, then **Run the scene** (about ten seconds, as a job). The tiles say how far the recovered velocity column is from the scene's (per cell, and each interface), where the layer below the bit was found against where the scene put it, and how good the time picks were; the section drawing shows the scene's layers beside the recovered column with the well path and the stations; the moveout drawing shows the picked time differences against the scene's own for four bit positions; the property column and the recorder's list fold out below. From a terminal: `gea seismic --action bit-image` prints the same run (`--stations 36 --radius 9000 --noise 2 --seed 3` change the scene; `--json` the whole result; exit code 0 when every verdict holds); `gea workspace --path C:\site --action bit-image` writes the report.

**How it is read**: the scene - a layered earth (velocities and rocks the program never sees), a well vertical to the kick-off, built at 10 degrees per 100 ft to horizontal and run 5,000 ft in the pay, the bit advancing 50 ft per window; stations in rings around the pad, the reference 40 ft from the wellhead; the bit's signal broadband noise in 8-80 Hz the processing never sees, arriving at each station delayed by the least-time ray through the layers (Snell's law), spread as 1/r, attenuated with Q = 80, with a reflection from the first layer below the well's deepest point, under white ground noise of the stated level against a reference arrival of 50. The correlation: each station against the reference, window by window; the peak read to a fraction of a sample by a parabola; picks below an SNR of 6 left out. The column: the differences inverted for the velocity of each 100 ft cell from the surface to the deepest bit position - Gauss-Newton in slowness, the ray path lengths as derivatives (Fermat), a second-difference smoothing, the step halved when it would raise the misfit; interfaces where neighbouring cells differ by 8% or more, read down to half a cell above the deepest bit position. The layer below the bit: the two-way arrival scanned over depth on the recovered column, the velocity below the deepest bit position taken as the last cell's, the correlations' envelopes stacked along each trial depth. The property column: Gardner's density, the overburden summed from the surface, the rocks of the inventory whose velocity range holds each layer, Wyllie's time-average porosity for the reservoir rocks among them.

**What it writes**: `reports\seismic\SIMULATION\bit_image.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON) with `section.svg` and `moveout.svg` beside them, `bit_image.summary.json` for the card, and the audit line `seismic.bit_image` with the verdicts and the scene's parameters. Every file is stamped SIMULATION_SELF_TEST.

**The number to check**: with 24 stations to 6,000 ft and noise 1, the column within 3% RMS over 27 cells and every interface (500, 1,800 and 2,400 ft) at its cell; the layer below the bit within 50 ft of the scene's 2,900 ft, against a quarter wavelength of 55 ft; picks good to 0.5 ms with none wrong. The resolution the page states - a quarter wavelength at the band's dominant frequency - is the physics' limit: no number of stations resolves a thinner layer; more stations lower the noise.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: all of it. The scene is an assumption - flat layers of one velocity each, a bit whose signal is white in the band, white ground noise - and the page says that the arithmetic recovers such an earth under such noise to the stated precision; it says nothing about any ground. Head waves along a fast layer, converted waves, dipping layers and lateral changes are not in the scene; the first field test is one well and one station - a correlated arrival at a stated distance - then a column against a sonic log. The porosity, density and rocks of the property column are what a log at the well calibrates, not a log; saturation needs resistivity, which no seismic array gives.

**Sources**: Rector and Marion, "The use of drill-bit energy as a downhole seismic source", Geophysics 56(5), 1991; Poletto and Miranda, Seismic While Drilling, Elsevier 2004; Shapiro and Campillo, "Emergence of broadband Rayleigh waves from correlations of the ambient seismic noise", GRL 31, 2004; Dix, "Seismic velocities from surface measurements", Geophysics 20, 1955; Gardner, Gardner and Gregory, Geophysics 39, 1974; Wyllie, Gregory and Gardner, Geophysics 21, 1956.
