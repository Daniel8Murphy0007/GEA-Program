# Casing design and kick tolerance

> Each casing string against the loads the hole can put on it - burst, collapse and tension, its strength by API's formulas, the smallest safety factor along it and where - and how big a gas kick each open hole below a shoe can take and still be shut in without breaking down the shoe.

**The command**: the **Casing & kick tolerance** tab: a tile per string (passes or the check below its design factor), the burst and collapse loads against the rating by depth for the chosen string, the safety factors, the kick tolerance of each open hole; the declarations (the operator role); **Write the report**. The worked example (three strings to 10,000 ft) is under Scene. From a terminal: `gea casing --example`; `gea casing --workspace C:\site --well SWD-1` prints a well; `--strings "surface:13-3/8:54.5:J55:0-1500:17.5:9.0; intermediate:9-5/8:47:N80:0-7000:12.25:9.8; production:7:29:P110:0-10000:8.5:11.5" --bha-od 6.5 --bha-length 600 --dp-od 5 --gas-sg 0.65 --surface-temp 70 --geothermal 1.5 --source "drilling program rev 2"` declares it (each string what:od:lb_ft:grade:top-shoe:hole_in:mud_ppg, a wall in inches last when the size is not in the program's table of API sizes; `--td`, `--td-hole` and `--td-mw` for a hole drilled below the deepest string); `--write` writes the report. In the declarations sheet: the `[casing_design]` table (a row per string) and `[casing_loads]` (the terms).

**What it needs**: each string's outside diameter, nominal weight, grade (H40 to Q125) and, for a size not in the table, its wall; its top (0 to the surface; a liner's hanger depth) and shoe in feet measured depth; the hole it was run in and the mud it was run in. The hole below each shoe is the next string's (its hole size and mud), and below the deepest the declared TD. The pore pressure and fracture gradient by depth come from the Pore pressure tab for the well, or one of each declared for the well. The true vertical depths come from the survey, else the drilling recorder's own Interpolated TVD, else the directional plan, else the well is taken as vertical - the page says which. For the kick tolerance's volume: the BHA's outside diameter and length and the drill pipe's outside diameter.

**How it is read**: the ratings are API TR 5C3's (ISO/TR 10400): internal yield 0.875 (2 Yp t / D); collapse in the yield, plastic, transition or elastic regime by D/t, with the bulletin's factors A, B, C, F and G in the grade's yield; the collapse read at the reduced yield Yp (sqrt(1 - 0.75 (Sa/Yp)^2) - 0.5 Sa/Yp) under the axial tension Sa at each depth; the body yield in tension. Burst: inside, gas to the surface from the pore pressure at the bottom of the hole below the shoe, no more than the fracture pressure at the shoe less the gas above it; outside, formation water (0.465 psi/ft). The gas is a real gas: its density p M / (z R T) at each depth's pressure and temperature (70 F at the surface rising 1.5 F per 100 ft unless declared), z by Dranchuk and Abou-Kassem's fit of the Standing-Katz chart on Sutton's pseudocritical properties for its gravity (0.65 unless declared), the column integrated up the well; a gas gradient declared in psi/ft replaces it (`--gas 0.1`; `--gas none` returns to the real gas). Collapse: outside, the mud the string was run in; inside, emptied to the shoe (or a declared fraction of its depth). Tension: the buoyed weight (1 - MW/65.5) hanging from the top, against the body yield with the design factor and with the overpull. Design factors 1.1 burst, 1.125 collapse, 1.6 tension and 100 klbf overpull unless declared. Kick tolerance: MAASP = 0.052 (FG - MW) D_shoe; a kick at the mud plus 0.5 ppg (or the window's pore pressure where higher) shuts in at SIDPP = 0.052 (kick - MW) D_bottom; the tallest gas column the shoe holds is (MAASP - SIDPP) / (mud gradient - gas gradient), no more than the open hole, the gas's gradient taken at the bubble's middle on bottom and at the shoe; its volume around the BHA and pipe at the bottom, and around the pipe at the shoe brought back to the bottom by the real-gas law p V / z T (Boyle's law with a fixed gradient) - the smaller; and the same for a swabbed kick, gas pulled in with no underbalance.

**What it writes**: the declarations in `drillstring\casing_design.json` (audit line `casing_design.declare`), the audit line `report.casing_design`, and the report `reports\wells\<well>\casing_design.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON); `reports\casing_design_example.*` for the worked example. The signed dossier carries the declarations and each declared well's result.

**The number to check**: each string's smallest safety factor against its design factor, and where along the string it falls; a 9-5/8 in 47 lb/ft N80 rated 6,870 psi burst and 4,750 psi collapse as API's table gives; the kick tolerance below each shoe against the company's minimum.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the ratings - API's formulas on the declared pipe body, not the connection's, not a mill test, not a string worn by drilling; the loads - textbook cases on the declared mud and window, without temperature or the cement; tension - the buoyed weight on the measured length, without the drag of a deviated hole or the bending in a dogleg; kick tolerance - one gas bubble of a sweet natural gas (no correction for CO2, H2S or nitrogen) at the declared geothermal temperature, not the circulating one; the volume a well can take, not one it has taken.

**Sources**: API TR 5C3 / ISO/TR 10400, Formulas and Calculations for Casing, Tubing, Drill Pipe and Line Pipe Properties (the ratings, the collapse regimes and factors, the reduced yield under tension); Bourgoyne, Millheim, Chenevert and Young, Applied Drilling Engineering, SPE Textbook Series 2, 1986, ch. 7 (the design loads); Rabia, Well Engineering and Construction, 2002 (the tension check with overpull); IADC WellSharp and the kick-tolerance method of the IADC drilling manual (MAASP, the single bubble); Sutton, "Compressibility Factors for High-Molecular-Weight Reservoir Gases", SPE 14265, 1985 (the pseudocritical properties); Dranchuk and Abou-Kassem, "Calculation of Z Factors for Natural Gases Using Equations of State", JCPT 1975 (z).

Related: `gea help pore-pressure`, `gea help hydraulics`, `gea help declare`.
