# Trip sheet

> The mud the hole took or gave on every trip, against the pipe that was moved. Pulling pipe takes steel out of the hole and the hole must be given that volume of mud; running pipe puts steel in and the hole gives it back. A hole that takes less than the steel pulled is being filled by something else - the first sign of a swabbed kick - and one that takes more, or gives back less, is losing mud. The page writes the crew's trip sheet from the drilling record: each trip's mud by the trip tank where the record carries one that moves, else by the pits, beside what the pipe accounts for.

**The command**: the **Trip sheet** tab. **The wells** counts every trip of 1,000 ft or more with the pumps off (the Rig time tab's own trips) and gives, pulling out and running in, the median barrels per 100 ft of the well's trips with their scatter. Pick a well. **The pipe** (the operator role) takes its outside and inside diameters in inches, its weight with tool joints in lb/ft where known, how it is pulled (dry: the steel; wet: the steel and what the pipe holds) and run (open, the pipe filling: the steel; closed against a float: the steel and the bore), and where the figures came from; **Declare the pipe** keeps it and **Take it off** removes it. **The sheet** lists every trip: where it was read from, the mud measured, the mud expected, the departure and the verdict; **show** draws one - the mud against the pipe moved, with what the pipe accounts for as a dashed line - and gives it 500 ft at a time. **Write the report** keeps it. From a terminal: `gea trip-sheet --workspace C:\site` prints the page; `gea trip-sheet --workspace C:\site --well "Well 30636033" --pipe 5,4.276 --weight 22.6 --pull dry --run open --source "pipe tally"` declares a pipe; `--remove-pipe` takes it off; `--write` writes the report and `--json` prints the page; `gea trip-sheet` alone runs the self-test on volumes that are known.

**What it writes**: `records/trip_sheet.json` in the workspace - each well's declared pipe with its source, who declared it and when - and an audit line `trip_sheet.pipe` (or `trip_sheet.pipe_removed`). The report, on request: `reports/trip_sheet/trip_sheet.json` and `.md`, `trips.csv` (a row a trip) and `intervals.csv` (a row for each 500 ft of each trip), with the audit line `trip_sheet.report`. Reading the page writes nothing.

**How a trip is read**: only the change while the pipe was being handled counts - moving, or in the slips with the pumps off. The volume is read through a 20-second running median, because a pit sloshes. A change of 5 bbl or more inside half a minute is mud moved at surface - a tank refilled or emptied - and is taken out, with the footage run meanwhile; the change during a break inside the trip (circulating, filling pipe) is reported and not counted. A trip in which more was moved at surface than the pipe accounts for, or of which less than half could be read, is not judged. Where neither the trip tank nor the pits moved with the pipe (under 0.15 bbl per 100 ft) the trip is NOT READ: the hole was filled from a tank the record does not carry - or took nothing at all, which the record cannot tell from it - and a blank is not called a short fill. With a pipe declared, a trip departs past 5 bbl or a quarter of the expected, whichever is more. With none, each trip is set against the well's own other trips in the same direction read from the same tank - their median and their scatter - and departs past 5 bbl or three scatters; that needs four like trips whose scatter is no more than 0.6 of their median, and says so when they do not agree. Steel is taken at 2,748 lb a barrel: a pipe of 22.6 lb/ft with its tool joints displaces 0.822 bbl per 100 ft.

**The alerts**: on a record still being written - its file changed in the last two hours, its last sample near now - the service reads the sheet every fifteen minutes and raises an alert once for a trip that departs, while it is under way or within half an hour of its end: `trip.fill` when there is more mud at surface than the pipe accounts for (a short fill pulling out, a hole that gave more running in), `trip.loss` for losses. A third, `trip.unread`, is for what the reading cannot settle: a trip under way in which nothing in the record moves with the pipe, where the trip before it was read - the hole is being filled from another tank, or it is taking nothing, and the record cannot tell which. Each goes to the Alarms tab and to the notification rules (`gea help alerts`, `gea help notifications`). **The wells** marks a record that is being written, and **The sheet** the trip under way.

**The number to check**: one trip's measured mud against the driller's own trip sheet for that trip; and the median barrels per 100 ft against the pipe's book displacement. On the Utah FORGE public pad (`gea help public-data`) no well's record carries a trip tank that moves - 16B(78)-32's trip tank reads zero throughout and 16A(78)-32's stands still or jumps - so every trip is read from the pits, and no pipe is declared, so each well is set against itself. 16B(78)-32: 38 like trips pulling out at a median of 0.85 bbl per 100 ft (scatter 0.48) and 85 running in at 1.78 (scatter 0.90); for scale, the steel of a 5 in drill pipe of 22.6 lb/ft is 0.82 and its bore 1.78 - the well's end-of-well report says what pipe it was. 58-32: 0.60 pulling out over 21 trips, 1.48 running in over 34. 56-32: 2.04 running in over 28 trips; pulling out, its trips do not agree with one another and none is judged. 78B-32: neither direction agrees. 16A(78)-32: half its trips are not read at all. On the self-test `gea trip-sheet` runs, a short fill of 6.58 bbl, a trip with half its mud lost, 15 bbl moved at surface and 12 bbl during a break are each found as what they are, and a trip with no volume moving is not read.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: a kick or a loss - a departure is a place in the record to look at, and the flow check, the daily report and the driller's own sheet say what it was; the pit volume as the hole's alone - mud is mixed, moved and lost at surface during a trip, and only the fast moves are taken out; the pipe - it is the operator's declaration, and a string of several sizes is not one pipe: the heavy-weight pipe and the collars displace more as they come through the table, and a casing or liner run stands out from the drill-pipe trips because it is not drill pipe; the well against its own trips as a check of the pipe - it finds the trip that differs from the others and cannot find what is wrong on every trip; a trip marked NOT READ as a hole that took nothing - or as one that did not: the record carries no volume that moved with the pipe, and cannot tell a hole filled from another tank from one that took nothing at all. The first 1,000 ft of a trip: a trip is a trip once the pipe has moved that far, and neither the page nor its alerts judge it before - the flowback at each pumps-off is the Kick & loss tab's.

Related: `gea help kick` (the flowback at each pumps-off, and gains and losses while circulating), `gea help rig-time` (the trips themselves), `gea help public-data`.
