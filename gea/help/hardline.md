# Hard line

> How near each well runs to a boundary the site has declared - a lease line, a unit boundary, the edge of a project area, or an area to keep out of - and where its bit is heading. The rule that goes with a boundary is a distance to keep from it, the setback; the hard line is the boundary moved in by that distance (for an area to keep out of, moved out by it). For every well with a survey the page gives the distance from the hard line at its nearest and where along the hole that is, how much of the hole is within the warning distance, inside the setback or over the boundary, where the bit is, and how much more hole on its present direction reaches the hard line. On a record still being written an alert is raised as the bit comes near.

**The command**: the **Hard line** tab. **Declare a boundary** takes a name, whether the wells stay inside it or keep out of it, the polygon - one of a layer the site brought (a shapefile of the lease, the unit or the plat, added on the Map tab; its polygons are listed by their labels) or corners typed in, a latitude and a longitude on each line, with their datum - the setback in feet (0 when the line is itself the limit), the warning distance in feet, and where the line and the rule came from. **The pad from above** then draws the boundary, its setback band and warning band, each well's path, its wellhead, its bit and a dashed line from its nearest point to the boundary; the table gives the numbers. **Write the report** keeps them. From a terminal: `gea hardline --workspace C:\site` prints the table; `gea hardline --workspace C:\site --declare "Section 14 lease" --layer lease --feature 0 --setback-ft 330 --warn-ft 100 --source "lease plat; field rule"` declares a polygon of a layer; `gea hardline --workspace C:\site --declare "Section 14 lease" --corners "31.9981,-102.0773;31.9981,-102.0601;31.9836,-102.0601;31.9836,-102.0773" --datum NAD83 --setback-ft 330 --source "lease plat"` declares corners; `--keep-out` makes it an area to keep out of; `gea hardline --workspace C:\site --remove section-14-lease` takes one off; `--write` writes the report and `--json` prints the page; `gea hardline` alone runs the self-test on paths and boundaries with every distance known. An assistant asks with the tool `hard_line` (`gea help mcp`).

**How it is measured**: in plan - a boundary is taken as a vertical surface, and depth does not enter. Each well's surveyed path, in the 3D tab's frame (a local transverse Mercator centred on the wells, north true at the centre), is sampled every 2 m, and each point's distance to the nearest point of the boundary is found - to an edge or to a corner, whichever is nearer, and by the rings whether the point is inside. The distance to the hard line is that distance less the setback; this is exact, since the hard line is the set of points one setback from the boundary. The survey's own one-sigma across the line at the nearest point, by the well's survey program (the Collision tab's), is printed beside the distance and is not taken off it. **The bit** is the bottom of the hole from the well's drilling record when it has one, carried beyond the last survey station on that station's inclination and azimuth (a hole more than 500 ft deeper than its last station is not surveyed there, and is not projected that far: the page says so and gives the last station); without a drilling record it is the last station. **Ahead** is the distance to the hard line 500 ft further along that direction, and **reaches it in** is how much more hole on that direction comes to the hard line, looked for over 3,000 m.

**The alert**: when alerts are set up (`gea help alerts`), every 15 minutes each well whose drilling record is still being written is looked at, and `hardline.warn` is raised when its bit is within the warning distance of a hard line, `hardline.setback` when it is inside the setback, and `hardline.crossed` when it is over the boundary - each once for a well, a boundary and a level.

**What it writes**: `records/hardline.json` in the workspace - each boundary with its polygon or corners, its setback, its warning distance, its source, who declared it and when; `hardline.declared` and `hardline.removed` lines in the audit log. **Write the report** writes `reports/hardline/hardline.json`, `hardline.md` and `hardline.csv` (a row a well and a boundary) and a `hardline.report` line.

**The number to check**: on the Utah FORGE public pad (`gea help public-data`), with the project's published extent in the folder, the boundary is the polygon the project calls its project area, declared with a setback of 0 ft - none is published - and a warning distance of 330 ft. The monitoring well 78B-32 stands nearest it: 85 ft from the line at 8,104 ft MD, on the south side, where the survey's one-sigma across the line is 6 ft; 58-32 is 269 ft from it at its wellhead; 56-32 is 1,523 ft inside. The two deviated wells run toward the east side: 16B(78)-32's last station, at 10,947 ft MD, is 524 ft from it, and held on its 101 degrees at 65 degrees of inclination its bit would reach the line in 582 ft more hole; 16A(78)-32 is nearest the west side (526 ft, at 1,148 ft MD), its bit - 191 ft beyond its last station by the drilling record - is 575 ft from the east side, and on its direction would reach it in 723 ft more hole. Each can be checked on the Map tab against the same polygon.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the boundary - it is the polygon the site declared, from the file or the corners it gave on the datum they carry, and a plat or a survey of the ground decides where a lease line is; the setback and the warning distance - the rule is the regulator's and the operator's, and both are the site's statement; the well's position to better than its survey - the one-sigma is printed and not taken off; the bit beyond the last survey station - it is projected, and the next survey decides; a distance in depth; and a well with no surface position, reference elevation and directional survey, which is listed with what it needs and is not judged.
