# Pressure plume

> Where the ledger's barrels have raised the formation's pressure, on a grid, through time - a SIMULATION. The Fluid balance tab's daily volumes go into the declared formation on a plan grid centred on the injection well and the pressure is stepped forward day by day; the rise is drawn as contours at every month's end of the record and a year on, both if the wells continue at their last rates and if they shut in now; the cells over a declared threshold are the area of review, in acres and reach; every mapped fault within the radius is read for its rise and its slip tendency at each snapshot, and the first day its margin to the friction line would be used up. A projection on a uniform declared formation, to be checked against a neighbour's gauge - never a reading of one.

**The command**: the Pressure plume tab, once the Fluid balance tab has an injection well with a formation, a volume record and a surface position, and the formation has its permeability, thickness and porosity declared. Pick a snapshot to draw it in plan; the faults are solid when declared sealing and dashed when open, amber inside the area of review and red past the friction line. Settings: the threshold (50 psi unless changed), the grid's cells per side, the radius the faults are taken within, how far to project; a fault layer is declared SEALING with its source, and is OPEN otherwise, because a map says nothing about what a fault does to flow. The 3D tab draws the same contours at the declared top of the disposal interval. From a terminal: `gea plume --workspace C:\site` prints the snapshots; `gea plume --workspace C:\site --threshold-psi 25 --cells 101 --radius-km 15 --projection-days 730` keeps settings; `gea plume --workspace C:\site --sealing Hennings-2021-basement-faults --source "the operator's fault study 2025"` declares a layer sealing (`--open` undoes it); `gea plume --workspace C:\site --write` writes the report; `gea plume` alone runs the self-test.

**What it writes**: `records/plume.json` (the settings and the sealing declarations with their source) in the workspace; with `--write` or the button, `reports/plume/plume.json` and `.md`; the signed dossier carries `results/plume.json` (every snapshot's figures, without the contour coordinates) beside `results/fluid_balance.json`; the settings and the report are lines in the audit log.

**The arithmetic**: single-phase, slightly compressible flow in field units on a square grid of cells of side dx (ft) centred on the injection well in a local transverse Mercator frame. A cell stores V_p c_t = dx^2 h phi c_t / 5.6146 barrels per psi; two cells exchange T = 0.001127 k A / (mu L) barrels per day per psi, A = h dx and L = dx, the harmonic mean of k h across the face; the formation is uniform in k, h and phi (a map of either is the next step, and the page says they are uniform). Each day every well's barrels go into its cell as a source and the pressure is advanced explicitly in sub-steps kept under the stability limit (0.45 of the smallest cell's storage over its transmissibilities); the grid's edge holds the initial pressure and lies 2 x the radius of investigation at the end of the projection out, or 2,000 ft beyond the farthest well. A sealing fault zeroes the transmissibility of every face between two cells on opposite sides of its segments. The area of review counts the cells at or above the threshold (acres = cells x dx^2 / 43,560) and its reach is the farthest such cell from the injection well. Each fault's rise is the largest cell value along its segments; its slip tendency is `slip`'s arithmetic (Morris 1996 Ts; the pressure to slip at the declared friction) with that rise as the pore-pressure increase, at the declared gradients and SHmax azimuth, for the layer's own dip or the declared dips. The projections start from the last day's field: CONTINUED holds each well's last thirty days' mean rate, SHUT IN sets every rate to zero. The grid is checked against the line source (Theis) at 4 and 8 cells from the main well at the end of the record, on the page, and on a uniform grid at a constant rate in the self-test (within 6 % at 3, 6 and 10 cells).

**The number to check**: the balance line - the barrels the grid holds as pressure against the barrels put in (the difference left through the edge, or the storage a sealed fault denied); the page's line-source comparison; on the self-test, the symmetric field, the contour at the 6-cell rise a circle of 6 cells' radius, the area of review within 15 % of pi r^2, the far side of a sealing fault shaded and the near side piled up.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: any of it - the plume is a projection on declared numbers, labelled SIMULATION on every line, and a neighbour's own gauge is the check (the calibration against it is the next module); the pressure at the injection well itself (its cell is an average over the cell, not the wellbore); a fault sealing or open (declared, default open, said in the table); a fault's depth, dip or activity beyond what the layer states; the area of review as a regulatory boundary (the threshold is the site's declaration, and the Commission's own rule for the well governs); heterogeneity it has no map of.
