# GEA Driller - the directional driller's agent

> A program on the rig PC that reads the rig's own feed and the pre-drill model, places the bit in the layer with a position filter, and, every 30 ft, recommends in the driller's units what to do next - a policy trained by reinforcement learning in a physics twin of the lateral - with the driller's answer, every command, and the records pulled into GEA.

**The command**: on the rig PC, `GEA-Driller-0.85.0.tar.gz` from the release (or the kit's `installers` folder, fetched on the Audit / Update tab): unpack it and run `sudo sh install.sh` on Linux or `install.cmd` on Windows, once; edit its config - the EDR's WITS0 feed (`tcp`, the host and port; `serial`, the COM port), the pre-drill model (the target layer's top TVD at the heel, its thickness and dip, the gamma in, above and below, the deep-resistivity tool, the heel's MD and TVD), the tool's cap, the limits - and restart it. The driller's screen is at port 8790 of the rig PC: `http://192.168.1.60:8790/live`. It shows the section with the model's layer window, the hole drilled through it and - since 0.78.0 - the **position filter**'s band: where the layer is from the readings, as a geosteerer places it (a particle filter over the layer's offset from the model, its dip error and its thickness, moved every decision and kept to the guesses that explain the gamma and the deep-resistivity distance), with the probability the bit is in the layer, above or below it and the distance to the top and the base with its spread, sixty feet ahead; the agent reads the same estimate, and so does the textbook driller it is judged against. Then the recommendation in words - *Build +1.5°/100 ft (toolface up, 25% steering); 25 klb WOB, 120 rpm, 500 gpm* - with its reasons (what the deep-resistivity tool says, the gamma against the model's values, the model's bracket, the limits) and the confidence of each choice, the rig's numbers against the limits, and **Accept**, **Reject** or **Did something else…** with the operator's name. In GEA: the **Drill string** tab's card **GEA Driller** - the rig PC's address and the well, **Pull**: the records come in checked against their hashes, and the table says how many decisions, accepted, rejected, done otherwise, the hole under advice, the armed sections and the commands. **Open the driller's screen** opens the rig PC's screen from its address. Before there is a rig PC: **Start the twin as the rig** runs the driller's program on this computer with a scene drilling itself under the agent - **Open its screen** is the driller's screen with the twin behind it, labelled SIMULATION - and **Pull from it** brings its records in; **Stop it** closes them. **Shadow a drilled well** (since 0.79.0): a well of this workspace with a drilling-recorder export is replayed through the advisor - every 30 ft the agent sees what the rig had shown to that point and nothing past it, and its recommendation is set beside what the record did next: the build actually made over the following 90 ft, the weight, speed and flow used, and at each stretch the gamma marks as out of the layer, whether the advisor called the way back at the first reading. The report, **SHADOW MODE**, is under the well's reports with a drawing of the lateral against the layer; the layer model is read off the well's own gamma in hindsight and says so - and grades how well it fits the well (good, fair, poor: a well steered to a rock that is not the low gamma reads poor, and the agent's calls against a poor model mean little) - unless the site's pre-drill model is given: a file `wells\Well-30636033\driller\model.json` with the config's `model` block (the target layer's top TVD at the heel, its thickness and dip, the gamma in, above and below, the heel's MD and TVD) is used whenever it is there (`gea workspace --path C:\site --action driller-shadow --well Well-30636033`). The export is read on this computer and nothing of it leaves it. Agreement with the driller says the two would have done alike, not that either was right. **The third dimension** (since 0.80.0): the lateral has an azimuth. The config's model carries the plan's azimuth (`plan_azimuth_deg`; empty, the first survey's at the heel is taken), the layer's dip across the plan line (`cross_dip_deg`, positive deepening to the right), the corridor the hole may run in to the right and left of the plan line (`corridor_ft`, the lease line and the offset wells) and where the heel sits across it (`heel_offset_ft`); the feed's surveys and the continuous azimuth (WITS item 0832, or the config's mapping) place the bit by minimum curvature along the plan, right of its line and in depth, and the screen draws the plan view - the corridor as a band, the hole against the plan line - under the section. The recommendation gains the aim - where to point the azimuth, along the plan or 4° left or right of it, and the turn that asks from where the hole points: *Build +1.5°/100 ft and aim 4° right of the plan (azimuth 44.0°), turning right 2.0°/100 ft (toolface 53°, 42% steering)* - with the build and the turn held together inside the DLS limit, and **Did something else…** takes the aim, or the turn made instead; the position filter carries the cross dip and reads it only when the hole moves across the plan line, leaving the model's value standing until then. In the twin the layer is a surface, the faults have a strike, the tool walks, and a hole that leaves the corridor by 150 ft is lost; the agent is judged on the hole in the corridor as well as in the layer (`gea-driller evaluate` prints both). Shadow mode reads the record's `azimuth_deg` column when it has one and sets the turn the advisor would have asked beside the turn the record made; without it the record's turns are not judged, and the report says so. **The vibration index from the feed** (since 0.81.0): a rig without a downhole vibration tool left the agent blind to the stick-slip and the whirl it was trained to ease, and the closed loop's vibration limit could never trip. Now the rig PC reads an index off the surface channels every EDR sends: over the last minute (`rig.vibration_window_s`), the surface torque's RMS about its mean as a fraction of the mean - the measure the Drill string page calls the oscillation index - times root two for the amplitude, and the same of the weight on bit and the hook load; stick-slip is the torque amplitude over 0.4, lateral vibration the weight amplitude over 0.2 (the twin's scale, where 1.2 is the limit), and the index is the larger. The downhole tool's item (WITS 0830) is taken instead whenever it comes, and every decision records which it read: the tile on the screen says *downhole tool* or *from surface data, stick-slip*, the reasons say *vibration 1.35 derived from the surface data (stick-slip, the surface torque swinging ±54%) over the limit 1.2*, and the limit disarms the loop on either. A surface proxy, labelled; on the twin as the rig it tracks the twin's own index within a few hundredths. The lateral file GEA writes for shadow mode carries, per foot, the surface channels' mean and RMS over the seconds of that foot (`torque_mean`, `torque_rms`, `wob_mean`, `wob_rms`, `hkld_mean`, `hkld_rms`, `rpm_mean`, `rpm_rms`, `spp_mean`, `spp_rms`), the replay reads the index off them for every decision, and the report says how often it stood over the limit and how often the advisor asked for less weight or more speed there than the record used; a record without torque or weight statistics says so and the advisor sees none. **The bar and the ceiling** (since 0.82.0): the agent is judged between two drillers on the same held-out scenes. The textbook driller is the bar - what a careful directional driller and geosteerer do by the book from the same observation the agent gets and nothing more: aim at the middle of the layer as the filter places it, steer back hard when the filter says the bit is out, lean away from a boundary the gamma's trend is running at, the rated weight in a calm hole in the layer and not while the hole fills, over the vibration limit lighter and faster first and heavier and slower when that did not quiet it, circulate at 40% of a bed, ream a balled bit, hold the plan line; it has a driller's memory of its own last decisions and none of the twin's secret. The oracle is the ceiling - the same driller told the twin's own layer at the bit and 120 ft ahead of it, which no policy can beat because it sees what no tool measures. `gea-driller evaluate` prints the three rows and how far the agent stands from the bar toward the ceiling; the self-test refuses a policy that does not beat the textbook, and the gate holds it under the oracle. The figures are a simulation's, labelled; a stronger textbook lowers the agent's standing without changing the agent, and that is the point of it. Over a cellular modem the rig PC joins GEA-VPN as a member of kind `driller` and GEA pulls from `http://10.77.0.4:8790`. The closed loop: `closed_loop.allowed` in the rig PC's config puts the switch on its screen; an operator arms it by name for one section of at most 500 ft (**Arm for this section**), every decision then goes to the downlink as a command, and the loop disarms itself on a stale feed, a crossed limit, the section's end or **Disarm now** - and this version's only downlink is the null one, which keeps every command on record and sends none, because no vendor's downlink protocol ships with it. From a terminal: `gea workspace --path C:\site --action driller-pull --box http://192.168.1.60:8790 --well SWD-1`; on the rig PC `gea-driller sim --seed 7 --pace 3 --port 8790`, `gea-driller evaluate --scenes 20`, `gea-driller train --seconds 600 --out policy.json`, `gea-driller selftest`.

**What it writes**: on the rig PC, `decisions.jsonl` (every observation, recommendation, confidence, reasons and answer), `commands.jsonl` (every arming, command and disarming with its reason) and `surveys.csv`, each with a `.sha256` beside it, and `manifest.json`. In GEA, `wells\SWD-1\driller\` holds the same files as pulled, `manifest.json` and `last_pull.json` with the summary; the audit lines `driller.pull`, `driller.sim_start`, `driller.sim_stop`. The twin as the rig writes under `field\sim-driller\data`.

**The number to check**: on the rig PC's screen, the feed's age (a decision is made only on a live feed) and, in GEA's table, accepted against rejected and done otherwise - an agent whose advice is rejected more than taken is not helping, and the records say so. For the agent itself, `gea-driller evaluate` prints the policy between the textbook driller and the oracle on sixty held-out twin scenes - the hole in the layer, the sweet spot, the corridor, feet a day, dogleg, stuck pipe, losses, and the agent's standing from the one toward the other - labelled SIMULATION; the self-test refuses a policy that does not beat the textbook.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the twin's figures are a simulation's, however many scenes, and say nothing of a real formation; a recommendation is the agent's reading of the feed, the model and the filter's estimate, not a survey; the filter's band is a probability, not a log, and the hole's position on its screen is projected from the surveys and the continuous inclination and azimuth, not measured at the bit, and its offset from the plan line inherits every survey's azimuth error; the hole-cleaning and balling the agent reasons from are the model's estimates; the vibration index read off the surface data is a proxy for what a downhole tool would measure, calibrated on the twin, not on a string; and "commands sent" is zero in this version by construction.

Related: `gea help drillstring`, `gea help recorder`, `gea help vpn`.
