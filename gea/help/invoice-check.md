# The invoice cross-check

> The drilling contractor's daywork invoice, day by day, against the daily drilling reports and the drilling record, at the contract's terms as the operator declares them: each day AGREES, is in QUESTION, or DIFFERS, with the dollars each difference puts in question and the line it comes from.

**The command**: the **Invoice check** tab. Choose the well; declare the contract's terms once (where they came from, the hour the report day starts on the rig's clock and whether a report is dated by the day it starts or ends, the day rates - operating, and standby, repair, moving and force majeure where the contract has them - and the repair allowance in hours a job and a month); attach the daily drilling reports and the invoice as CSV files, each with its source. **Write the report** writes the client report with its PDF. From a terminal: `gea invoice-check --workspace C:\site --well W` prints the check; `--terms-source "..." --day-start 06:00 --operating 30000 --standby 25000 --repair 0 --repair-hours-month 24` declares the terms; `--attach-ddr FILE --source "..."` and `--attach-invoice FILE --source "..."` attach; `--write` writes the report; `--template ddr` or `--template invoice` prints the headings it reads.

**The files**: saved from a spreadsheet as CSV and read by their headings. A daily drilling report: one row per line of the time breakdown - Date (the report's), Hours (or From and To), Code (the IADC operation code) or Activity, and Depth From and Depth To where it has them. An invoice: one row per charge - Date (or From and To for a span), Rate Type (operating, standby, repair, moving, force majeure; anything else - fuel, rentals - is listed and not checked), Quantity, Unit (day or hour), and Unit Price or Amount.

**The checks**: the report's lines add up to 24 hours; hours reported drilling (code 2) against the record's hours on bottom making hole; the day's end depth and footage against the record's; a line coded drilling, reaming, circulating or trips over a span the record shows the pumps off, the rotary still and the bit still; a repair line over a span the record shows the rig working; more than 24 hours billed in a day, a day billed twice, billed with no report or nothing in the record; standby hours (code 33, or an activity that says standby or waiting on the operator's orders) billed at the operating rate; rig repair (code 8) past the allowance billed at the operating rate (IADC daywork contract, paragraph 4.4); a unit price that is not the contract's rate for its kind; quantity times price that is not the amount; and, as a question, a day billed at the operating rate on which the record shows the rig quiet six hours or more beyond the hours billed at standby, repair or force majeure.

**What it writes**: the files and their index in `economics\invoices\`, the terms in `economics\invoices\terms.json`, the audit lines `invoice.terms`, `invoice.attach`, `invoice.remove`, `report.invoice_check`, and the report in `reports\wells\<well>\invoice_check.*` (PDF, HTML, markdown, JSON), kept in report history.

**The number to check**: a day's dollars in question against its finding - the hours times the difference between the two rates, over 24; the record's hours against the well's charts for the same report day.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: what is owed - the contract and the parties decide; this sets three accounts side by side at the terms as declared. The clocks - the report's and the recorder's are taken to be the rig's same clock. Quiet time on the recorder - waiting on cement, rigging up and repairs within the allowance are operating time with the pumps off, so quiet is a question, not a difference. A charge that is not a day rate.
