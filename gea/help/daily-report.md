# Daily report

> The daily drilling report, written from the drilling record. A rig sends one sheet every morning: where the hole was at the start of the day and at its end, what the rig did hour by hour, how it drilled, what it tripped, what went wrong. The crew writes it from memory, to the quarter hour. The drilling recorder has the same day second by second, and the Rig time, Trip sheet, Drilling alerts and Kick & loss tabs each read one part of it. This page puts those parts on one sheet, for one well and one report day, and leaves room for what only the crew can say.

**The command**: the **Daily report** tab, once a well has a drilling recorder's export attached on the Wells tab. Pick the well; **The days** lists every report day of its record with the footage, the drilling and tripping hours, the connections and the trips. Pick a day. The sheet gives the depths, a bar of the day's 24 hours by operation, the time log, the hours by rig state, the readings while drilling, the mud the record carries, the trips, and the places in the day's record to look at. **Remarks** (the operator role) takes the crew's words for the day and the plan for the next, kept with the name of who typed them. **Write this day's sheet** writes the files and gives a link, **Open the sheet to print**; **Write every day** does it for every day of the well's record. `gea daily-report --workspace C:\site --well "Pad 3 H-1" --date 2026-09-01` prints the sheet (the last day of the record without `--date`), `--remarks "waited on cement four hours" --plan "drill out the shoe"` types the remarks, `--write` writes the files and `--every-day` writes them all; without a workspace it runs the self-test on a record whose day is known.

**How a day is read**: the report day starts at the hour the contract's terms give on the Invoice check tab - 06:00 until that is declared - and a sheet is dated by the day it starts on, or by the day it ends on where the terms say the contractor dates his reports so; every sheet says the hours it covers, by the drilling recorder's own clock. **The time log** is kept as a daily report is, to the quarter hour: each quarter hour is named by what the rig mostly did in it, from the Rig time tab's own states - drilling (rotating or sliding), tripping in, tripping out, reaming and washing, circulating, in the slips, off the hook, stationary. A connection's slips are counted with the drilling or the tripping around them, a single quarter hour of circulating or working the pipe between two quarters of drilling is joined to them, and quarters with no record are shown as that, so the lines always come to 24 hours. The exact hours of every state, to the second, are in the table under the log. **The depths** are the recorder's hole depth with its spikes taken out, at the start of the day and at its end; **the footage** is the new hole the record shows being made, a rise a bit can make in a second, summed over the day. Where the two differ the sheet says so and why it can: a correction of the depth, a plug back, a sidetrack, or hole made where there is no record. **While drilling** gives the middle value of the weight on bit, the rotary speed, the torque, the flow, the standpipe and differential pressure and the rate of penetration over the seconds on bottom, with the range nine tenths of them sat in.

**What it writes**: reading the page writes nothing. **Remarks** writes `records\daily_report.json` and an audit line `daily_report.remarks` (or `daily_report.remarks_removed`). **Write this day's sheet** writes, under `reports\daily\` in a folder for the well and one for the day, `daily_report.html` (one page, no script, made to print), `daily_report.md`, `daily_report.json` and `time_log.csv` (a row a line of the log), with the audit line `daily_report.report`.

**The number to check**: a day's footage and its hours drilling against the contractor's own daily report for that day - the Invoice check tab sets the two side by side when the contractor's reports are attached. On the Utah FORGE public pad (`gea help public-data`) the five wells' records hold 270 report days: 16B(78)-32 has 88, from 2023-04-25, and its best is 2023-04-30 with 1,706 ft of new hole in 12.0 h on bottom and 16 connections; 16A(78)-32 has 75, every one of whose time logs comes to 24 hours, at a middle of 15 lines a day; on 18 of them the depth and the footage differ and the sheet says so. `gea daily-report` with no workspace must end `OK`: on its made record the two days' footage is the record's, the trip is on the first day's log out and then in between two lines of drilling, and no line of the log is the slips alone.

**What this page will not call a measurement**: the time log's lines - each quarter hour is named by what the rig mostly did, so ten minutes of circulating inside an hour of drilling are in the hours by rig state and not on a line of their own; the rig states themselves - they are the Rig time tab's rules on hook load, block, bit, pumps and rotary, with that page's cautions; the footage - a depth the driller corrected moves it; the alerts - each is a place in the record to look at. And everything a daily report holds that a recorder does not: the crew and its hours, the cost, the mud check, the bit's grading, the assembly unless a drill-string run is declared, the weather, and the reason for anything. Those are the crew's to write, in the remarks. No sheet has yet been set beside a contractor's signed report for the same day.
